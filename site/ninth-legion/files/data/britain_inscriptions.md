# Legio IX Hispana in Britain: every attestation in the Roman Inscriptions of Britain (RIB Online)

Compiled from https://romaninscriptionsofbritain.org. **Every record below was verified by fetching the RIB Online page** (marked `verified_online: true` in the JSON). Nothing is cited from memory except where explicitly labelled "inference" or "general knowledge". Method: (1) RIB full-text searches for *Hispana*, *Hisp\**, *VIIII*, *IX*, *nona*, *Ninth*, *imaginifer*, *Scalesceugh*, *legio AND Vindolanda*; (2) the 1983 RIB I Epigraphic Index, "Legion IX Hispana" (https://romaninscriptionsofbritain.org/epindex/military-affairs-legions); (3) a complete prev/next walk of the RIB II.4 tile-stamp series 2462 (2462.1 to 2462.17*, ending at 2463.1.i) plus 2461.1-2; (4) a page-by-page sweep of every RIB III number 3001-3550 (because RIB 3047 is invisible to the site search: bracketed restorations defeat the tokenizer); (5) the series introduction at https://romaninscriptionsofbritain.org/instrumentum/2462.

## 0. Summary count

- Stone inscriptions naming the legion: **11** (RIB I: 254, 255, 256, 257, 260, 659, 665, 673, 680 = 9 certain; RIB III: 3042 probable, 3047 probable/restored).
- Legionary tile-stamps (RIB 2462): **116** catalogued impressions/entries from at least 16 dies (2462.1-2462.16, incl. 9A/9B/9C worn-die states), plus 1 rejected antiquarian report (2462.17*) and 2 unassignable LE[G] stamps (2461.1-2, "VI or IX").
- Graffiti: **1** (RIB 2501.7, London samian: imaginifer of Legio IX). RIB 2491.72 is a graffito on one of the Carlisle tiles (same object as 2462.3.i).
- Lead sealings: **none** of the Ninth in RIB 2411 (searches for VIIII / IX / nona / Hispana return no sealing).
- Writing tablets (Tab. Vindol., Tab. Luguval., Bloomberg): **none** mention the Ninth. Hits for *VIIII*/*nona*/*Ninth* among tablets are all *cohors VIIII Batavorum* (e.g. Tab. Vindol. 127-137, 574-586) or numerals. A boolean search *legio AND Vindolanda* (59 rows) gives no IX/VIIII/nona snippet.
- Modern forgery: RIB 3542* (IMP C / LEG IX, cut in 1959).
- **Last dated attestation: RIB 665, York, 10 Dec 107 - 9 Dec 108 (Trajan trib. pot. XII).** RIB's own statement of the departure (RIB II.4 intro to 2462): York was the legion's base "from a.d. 71 until the legion's departure from Britain, probably towards the end of Trajan's reign"; RIB 659 and 680 commentaries put the arrival of Legio VI at York "about a.d. 120"; RIB 1319 commentary puts the Sixth's arrival in Britain in a.d. 122.

### RIB 254 - Lincoln (Lindum)  
<https://romaninscriptionsofbritain.org/inscriptions/254>
- **Status:** attestation  
- **Object:** Tombstone (Limestone)  
- **Find context:** At a depth of about 2.4 m. in a garden on the south side of the churchyard of St. Peter-at-Gowts, about 870 m. south of the Stonebow, or south gate of the extended colonia at Lincoln. (found About 1800); now: The original is lost.
- **Text:** `Q(uinti) Corneli Q(uinti) f(ili) Cla(udia) (tribu) eq(uitis) le(gionis) VIIII [(centuria)] Cassi Martialis an(norum) XL stip(endiorum) XIX h(ic) s(itus) e(st)`  
- **Diplomatic:** `Q CORNELI Q F CLA EQ LE VIIII [.] CASSI MARTIALIS AN XL STIP XIX H S E`
- **RIB translation:** Quintus Cornelius, son of Quintus, of the Claudian voting-tribe, trooper of the Ninth Legion from the century of Cassius Martialis; aged 40 years, of 19 years’ service, lies buried here.
- **RIB date:** (no indication); criteria: -  
- **Date used here:** 60-71 (inference: legion at Lincoln c. 60-71 (RIB gives no date))
- **Person:** Quintus Cornelius, son of Quintus, Claudia tribe  
- **Rank:** eques (legionary trooper), century of Cassius Martialis; aged 40, 19 stipendia
- **Notes:** RIB gives no date. The Ninth was based at Lincoln from c. AD 60 until its move to York in AD 71 (RIB II.4 intro to 2462; RIB 3073 commentary: "the epitaphs of two serving legionaries at Lincoln, when the Ninth legion was still based there (RIB 256 unambiguously, and RIB 254)"). Formulae (h.s.e., no Dis Manibus, no cognomen for the legion) fit a Neronian/early Flavian date; date range below is that inference, not RIB's. Original lost (known from Willson MS); listed in RIB 1983 index under Legio IX Hispana.
- **Bibliography (RIB):** JRS xl (1950) 116 Willson, E. J., Soc. Ant. London MS. Willson vi f. 15 Tomlin, R.S.O., Britannia Romana (2018), 31, no. 2.13 Trismegistos 154747 Epigraphische Datenbank Heidelberg (EDH) HD021172

### RIB 665 - York (Eboracum/Colonia Eboracensis)

- **Find context:** At a depth of 7 m. in King’s Square, York, near the house at the corner of King’s Square and Goodram Gate and within a few metres of the site of the Roman south-east gateway. (found 1854); now: Now in the Yorkshire Museum.

- **RIB translation:** The Emperor Caesar Nerva Trajan Augustus, son of the deified Nerva, Conqueror of Germany, Conqueror of Dacia, pontifex maximus, in his twelfth year of tribunician power, six times acclaimed imperator, five times consul, father of his country, built this gate by the agency of the Ninth Legion Hispana.
- **RIB date:** a.d. 107-108; criteria: titulature  
- **Date used here:** 107-108 (RIB: imperial titulature (trib. pot. XII = 10 Dec 107 - 9 Dec 108; imp. VI, cos. V))
- **Person:** Emperor Trajan (Imp. Caesar Nerva Traianus Aug. Germanicus Dacicus); the legion as builder  
- **Rank:** legio VIIII Hispana (building agency)

Mann has noted (to R.S.O.T.) may have been centred to balance (1) imp caesar, without the conjectured but redundant portam and fecit.
- **Notes:** Monumental commemorative tablet (W 1.118 x H 1.143 m) found 1854 at 7 m depth in King's Square near the SE gate of the fortress; Yorkshire Museum YORYM 1998.21. Full text: [I]mp(erator) Caesar [divi N]ervae fil(ius) Ne[rva Trai]anus Aug(ustus) Ger[m(anicus) Dacicus po]ntifex maximu[s tribuniciae po]testatis XII imp(erator) V[I co(n)s(ul) V p(ater) p(atriae) portam] per leg(ionem) VIIII Hi[sp(anam) fecit]. This is the LAST DATED attestation of Legio IX Hispana in Britain (and the last dated record of the legion anywhere on stone): the gate of the York fortress rebuilt in stone "by the agency of the Ninth Legion Hispana". CIL vii 241; Tomlin, Britannia Romana (2018) no. 4.16.

### RIB 3042 - Hayling Island  
<https://romaninscriptionsofbritain.org/inscriptions/3042>
- **Status:** probable  
- **Object:** Altar (?)? (Bembridge Limestone (a shelly limestone))  
- **Find context:** Found in excavating the Iron Age and Roman temple (SU 724 029), as part of the stone packing of a Saxon post-hole (found 1980); now: Now in Department of Archaeology, Anthropology and Geography, University of Winchester, (formerly King Alfred’s College), Winchester, but intended for Hampshire Cultural Trust at Chilcomb House, Winchester (pers. comm. Tony King).
- **Text:** `[...] ?[Na]ẹvian[us ...] [... l]eg(ionis) VIII[I ...] [...]`  
- **Diplomatic:** `[...] [..]ẸVIAN[...] [...]EG VIII[. ...] [...]`
- **RIB translation:** […] Naevianus […] of the Ninth Legion […]
- **RIB date:** Late 1st century a.d.; criteria: context  
- **Date used here:** 43-100 (RIB: archaeological context (late 1st c.); attribution to the Ninth is probable, not certain)
- **Person:** ?[Na]evianus  
- **Rank:** [... l]eg(ionis) VIII[I] - soldier/officer of the Ninth (rank lost)
- **RIB commentary (excerpt):** 1. The name is a cognomen in -ianus formed from a simpler cognomen (e.g. Scaeva) or a nomen (e.g. Naevius). Unless it is unique, there are very few possibilities. The only one that occurs more than once or twice is Naevianus. 2. The suprascript bar extends to the very edge of the fragment, confirming the trace of a third vertical stroke in the numeral, and implying that a fourth has been lost. ## 3. Legionary tile-stamps of Legio IX Hispana: RIB 2462 (complete series)

RIB II.4 introduction (verbatim, verified): "Tile-stamps of Legion IX Hispana have been found in fair quantity at York, its base from a.d. 71 until the legion's departure from Britain, probably towards the end of Trajan's reign; but a stamp sometimes said to have been found at Lincoln (RIB 2462.9 (i)), and thus datable earlier than a.d. 71, has been shown to be more probably a stray of York origin. The legion did not start to stamp its tiles before the closing years of the first century. Examples have been found at Aldborough, Leicester, Malton, Old Winteringham and Templebrough in north-eastern Britain, as well as at Carlisle, Stanwix and the military tilery at Scalesceugh in the north-west. Stamps from the last-named three sites give the legion's numeral in the form VIIII, as does the stamp from Leicester (RIB 2462.1), whereas the stamps from York and other sites employ the form IX. Malton has produced stamps from two dies (RIB 2462.14, 15) which have not been found elsewhere." ## 6. What RIB says about the date of the Ninth's departure

- RIB II.4 intro to 2462: York was the base "from a.d. 71 until the legion's departure from Britain, probably towards the end of Trajan's reign".
- RIB 659 commentary: "This inscription must antedate the arrival of the Sixth Legion at York about a.d. 120."
- RIB 680 commentary: "As the Sixth Legion replaced the Ninth Hispana at York about a.d. 120, the person here commemorated must have died by that time, or, if in retirement, within a few years of that date."
- RIB 1319 commentary: Pons Aelius "must belong to the inception of the Wall in a.d. 122. This was the year of the arrival in Britain of Legio VI Victrix".
- RIB 3047 commentary: Legio VI "did not arrive in Britain until fifty years after the army left Dorset".
- RIB 3364 commentary: Titus Annius' death in action attributed (Birley) to "the warfare in Britain at Hadrian's accession (Hist. Aug. Hadr. 5.2), which seems likely"; formulae date the stone c. 100-125.
- RIB 2462.12.iii commentary: Longthorpe was the "base of part of Legion IX Hispana until c. a.d. 61".

## 7. Cross-checks outside RIB

- Wikipedia, *Legio IX Hispana* (fetched 2026-09-14): "The last attested activity of the Ninth in Britain is during the rebuilding in stone of the legionary fortress at York (Eboracum) in 108"; NB its translation of RIB 665 says "five times acclaimed Imperator" whereas RIB restores imp(erator) V[I] (six times). Cites RIB 673 (dated 71-125), Nijmegen stamps 104-120, the Aachen altar of L. Latinius Macer, the Ewijk phalera LEG HISP IX.
- Livius.org, *Legio VIIII Hispana* (fetched): last datable activity in Britain "in 108/109, when it built a stone fortress at York"; VI Victrix arrives c. 121; L. Aemilius Karus as evidence for survival to c. 142/3 (career), legion absent from the Marcus Aurelius legion list.
- Graafstal, E.P., "What Happened Next? Hadrian's Wall, the expeditio Britannica and the Fate of the Ninth Legion", *Britannia* 56 (2025) 25-55 (abstract fetched): argues the legion vanished c. 123-4 in a British crisis after Hadrian's 122 visit; uses RIB 665, RIB 3364, RIB 1051; notes the Nijmegen vexillatio Britannica (100+ stamps) while the main body stayed at York; VI Victrix as replacement c. 123.
- Wright, R.P., "Tile-Stamps of the Ninth Legion found in Britain", *Britannia* 9 (1978) 379-82 (the basis of RIB 2462; landing page located, not read).
- A popular report (HeritageDaily 2013) of a further LEG VIIII H tile found at Scalesceugh in 2010 could not be verified (site bot-blocked); it is NOT in RIB Online, whose Britannia coverage runs to vol. 54 (2023); the only post-1993 Scalesceugh stamps in RIB are Brit. 24.7 (Legio XX) and Brit. 37.9 (IMP stamps).

## 8. Negative checks (what is NOT there)

- RIB 258 (T. Valerius Pudens, leg. II Adiutrix, Lincoln) and RIB 259 (Q. Valerius Victorinus, legion lost) are Lincoln legionary tombstones that do NOT name the Ninth.
- No Ninth-Legion lead sealing (RIB 2411), no diploma, no Vindolanda/Carlisle/London tablet names the legion. Tab. Luguval. 1 and Brit. 23.p146 (Carlisle stilus tablet of AD 83, Legio XX) were checked.
- No RIB III (3001-3550) stone names the Ninth except 3042 and 3047 (and the falsum 3542*). RIB 3073 and 3121 mention the Ninth only in commentary.
