The attention of the citizens of New
York is at present united upon the
Grand Jury. It is felt everywhere that
the first indictment against Tweed was
only the first move in a long series of
attacks against the Tammany leaders.
Yesterday afternoon the Grand Jury
handed in two indictments, for which
Recorder Hackett made out two bench
warrants. These were handed over to
Sheriff Brennan, but have not yet been
served. The Court of General Sessions
was crowded yesterday by persons anx
ious to find out against whom the indict
ments were made. As far as can be
ascertained, it is understood that Tweed
and Connolly will move to quash the
indictments found against them as
illegal. A provision of the law of loiU
says: No action shall originate with
the Grand Jury of the inferior court of
General Sessions while the Grand Jury
of the higher Court of Oyer and
Terminer is sitting." The indictments
against Connolly and Tweed were found
under precisely these circumstances.
Mr. O' Conor, Prosecuting Attorney, has
declared this provision of the law to be
merelv technical, which could not hold
good 'before the jury, and has advised
the Grand Jury to continue their inves
tigations without reference to this clause
of the law.
