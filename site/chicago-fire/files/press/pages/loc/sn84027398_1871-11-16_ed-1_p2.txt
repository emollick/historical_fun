Titlrs Under Tax Sales.
Justice Dillon, of the United States
Circuit Court, a few days since rendered
an opinion, which is of importance to
county officers in this Staue. The suit
was brought by A. N. Lancaster, against
the County Auditor and Treasurer of
Page county:
The plaintiff claimed to be the owner
of certain lands in Page county which
had been sold for taxes in 1864, and for
which a deed was given to the purchaser.
In 1897 the land was again sold for tax
es to James Cal'a* an, Jr. In 1870, the
plaintiff'went to the County Auditor's
offl*e and offered to redeem the lands
from the last sale, but the Auditor re
fused to accept the money, on the ground
that he was no longer the owner of the
land, and the only person who could re
deem it, was the holder of the tax deed
under the former sale. The suit was
brought to redeem the laud, and to en
join the Treasurer from making a deed
under the last sale.
The court held that the plaintiff had
the right to redeem that the Auditor
had no right to refu-e to issue the re
demption certificate and that heshoutd
have received the money tendered by
the owner of the patent title. The fact
that an outstandiug deed existed could
not bar the owner from protecting his
interest from the inception of a new es
tate under the last sale.
