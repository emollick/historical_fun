A Bold Operation. We heard vet
terday of one of the boldest attempts at
robbery we have heard for some time.
On Friday night at half past nine
o'clock, the store of R. Larkin, No. 194
Market street, was closed up, and Mrs.
Larkin and one or two others were
sitting in the rear end of the store, hav
ing a social chat. The Iront door had
been left unlocked, and by this means
a negro man obtained entrance unob
served. He at once went to work and
packed up ready for carrying off a lot
of dry goods worth, perhaps, twenty
five dollars. The persons sitting in the
rear of the^tore heard some noise, but
paid little or no attention to it, and
the rascal could have made off
with his plunder without being detected,
but success made hiin bold. Going
to the rear end of the store he got a
sack to put the stolen goods in. Re
turning to where his plunder was con
cealed, in the front of the store, he made
such a noise a* to attract the attention
of Mrs. Larkin and others, who discov
ered the thief. Hoping to secure the
fellow, they locked the door, but he
pulled down the bolls and, springing
the doors open, made his escape. He
left behind him a new hat and a purple
silk bonnet, both evidently stolen. He
is described as a mulatto of small stat
ure and highly pox marked. It is a
great pity that there was no man in the
store at the time to prevent the escape of
the bold scoundrel.
