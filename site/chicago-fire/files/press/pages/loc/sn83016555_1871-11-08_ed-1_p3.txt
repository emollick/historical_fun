No Ordinance.— Several days ago a man
was arrested on Willow street for burying
a deceased infant in the yard of the prem
ises The mother had kept the corpse in
the house three days, being unable to pay
funeral expenses. The man was president
of some benevolent association and interred
the body on solicitation of the sick mother.
The examination took place before Recorder
Houghton, when that official failed to find
any ordinance or clause prohibiting the of
fense, consequently there is no punishment.
There is nothing but a natural custom to
govern in this or similar cases, but custom
does not prescribe or enforce penalties.
Perhaps it woul^be well for our City Coun
cil to enact a law and fix a penalty in order
that offenses may hereafter be punished.
