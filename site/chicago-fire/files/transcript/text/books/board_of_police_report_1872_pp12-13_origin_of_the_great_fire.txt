The “great fire" occurring October 8th, and 9th, tS/t, is
without a parallel in the history of the world, and to attempt
to make a correct statement or record of the circumstances at¬

From investigations made by the Hoard of Police, it was as¬
certained that the fire originated in a small frame structure
located in the rear of No. 137 DcKoven street, owned and used
by a Mr. Thomas O’Leary as a cow stable, t he fire com¬
menced at about half past nine o’clock, p. m. A strong south¬
westerly wind was blowing at the time: no rain had fallen for
several weeks previous : consequently all combustible matter
was prepared for ignition.

The origin of the fire could not be definitely arrived at, but
from all the circumstances connected with the case, it is cur¬
rently believed to have been set through the careless action of
some person, or persons, at present unknown.

This portion of the city was occupied by the poorer classes,
principally Bohemian emigrant families, and being in the vicin¬
ity of several planing mills, shingle mills, and factories, had col¬
lected a large quantity of shavings from these places, and stored
them in the basements and yards of their premises for winter
fuel, in fact, shavings were scattered for blocks in every di¬
rection.

All the apparatus responding to the first alarm, were soon
upon the ground, and were placed in the most advantageous
positions in very quick order; the second and third alarms were
given in succession, almost instantaneously, bringing the entire
department to the rescue, but of little avail.
