A Good Man Pullen.
The telegraph announced yesterday morning
the death of the Rev.. Ira M. Weed, at Ypsilanti,
Mich. Mr. Weed removed to this city in the fall
of 1847, and subsequently reaided hero for several
years. His death will bo deeply mourned by all
tbe older members of the Presbvtcrian and Con
gregational churches of this city, to whom' ho
was well known as a man who in all respects
adorned the profession of which he was an hon
ored member. The writer, who has known him
intimately for nearly a quarter of a
century, can truly say that he
never knew him to say or to do
am thing that was inconsistent with the highest
tvpo of a Christian gentleman. He was the first
pastor of tho Presbyterian Church at Ypsilanti,
and under his most successful ministry it became
one of the largest and most efficient churches in
the State. For several years his failing health has
obliged him to do very little clerical service, and
he retired to Ypsilanti to sleep among those for
whose welfare lie devoted many of the best years
of his life. He was pre eminently a good man,”
and his name will be held iu respect and honor
by all who knew him.

The Committee on Hick and Hospitals report
the ■whole number of patients treated during the
week ending December 1 at fifteen hundred and
thirty-five. The whole numbertreated last week
was two thousand one hundred and fifteen. Only
four deaths are reported. From District No. 1
Dr Reed reports three new cases of small-pox.
The vaccination of the inmates of the barracks is
being pushed vigorously. The "Washington Park
barracks (have had careful and complete vaccin
ation throughout, and the work is in aolivo
progress at the llalsted street bar
racks. Dr. Reed reports the barracks
at both places in good sanitary condition, and
good health prevails. From District No. 2, Dr.
Wagner reports a slight Increase in typhoid and
malarial fevers, and the cases of varioloid of a
mild type. There is an increase also of rheumatic
affections. Dr. Bogue, Superintendent of District
No. 3, while reporting a slight decrease In the
namber of patients treated, gives an increase in
the number of cases of scarlet fever, two deaths
occurring from that disorder in the Centro
Avenue barracks. District No. 4—Dr. Powell
reports a decrease in the number of patients,
with no prevailing disease. Dr. Freer, District
No. c, reports a slight increase of patients, and no
prevailing disease.
