## 0. Ten-line summary

* The O'Leary property was the **east half of Lot 12, Block 38, School Section Addition** (Michael McDermott's affidavit of 15 Oct 1871, quoted in Andreas II:708: "at No. 137 DeKoven Street, on the east half of Lot 12, in Block 38, School Section Addition"). Lot 12 is 50 ft wide, so the parcel is 25 × 100.75 ft, its west line **133.4 ft** and east line **158.4 ft** east of the Jefferson line (survey-plat dimensions, see §1).
* Block 38 was platted 358 ft E-W (1833 School Section plat, column label) and originally 396 ft N-S; the 15-ft alley was opened in 1855 "on the N. ¼ line" (99 ft south of Taylor); De Koven St (60 ft) was cut through the original block; the south side of De Koven (lots 15-21) is therefore also Block 38.
* Numbers step 2 per 25-ft half-lot on both sides: north side 141 = E½ 13, 139 = W½ 12, **137 = E½ 12**, 135 = W½ 11, 133 = E½ 11 …; south side 140 = H-lot 2, 138 = H-lot 1, 136 = W½ 18, **134 = E½ 18 (Catherine Sullivan, Daniel Sullivan's home)**, 132 = W½ 19, **130 = E½ 19 (William White)**, 128 …
* Barn: 16 × 20 ft, 14-16 ft high, at the alley end of the lot; O'Leary cottages (double, 36 ft N-S total, ~16 ft wide) at the street end; Dalton's one-storey cottage (135) on posts 4 ft high, on his west lot line, front 38-40 ft back, 40 ft deep, with an 8-ft board fence from house to his 12×20 shed at the alley.
* From the front of White's lot (x 233-258, y −60) every straight line to the barn crosses Dalton's lot between y = 38 and 78 (see §2.6 table) — i.e. passes through the Dalton house footprint unless the flames were seen above its roof. From the front of Sullivan's own house (x 183-208) the lines to the barn's west half clear Dalton's house but cross the O'Leary rear cottage / yard.
* Modern anchor: Cook County parcel 17-16-324-029 (Fire Academy) still carries the 1835/1874 lot-line vertices; its west edge is 18.3 ft east of the 1871 Jefferson line and its east edge 20.1 ft west of the 1871 Clinton line (Jefferson and Clinton were widened from 40 ft to ~74-75 ft; Taylor from 60 to ~80 ft; De Koven unchanged at 60 ft).
* The barn site (x 133-153, y 85-101) falls in the open yard immediately north of the Fire Academy's front wing (rear wall at y ≈ 80.6); the "Pillar of Fire"/"Tongues of Flame" sculpture stands on the plaza at the NE corner of De Koven and Jefferson, roughly 90-120 ft WSW of the barn site.
* Weather (Signal Service, Chicago, via Musham 1940 and NWS): 7 a.m. 51 °F, SW 19 mph, RH 62 %; 2 p.m. 85 °F (unofficial), SW 35 mph; 4 p.m. 79 °F, 22 mph, clear; sunset 5:30; wind fell to a "gentle breeze" by dark and "the same gentle breeze … from the southwest" at 9 p.m.; a 60-mph anemometer reading near midnight is dismissed by Musham as instrument error. Rain since 3 July 1871: 1.40 in (Musham/Andreas) — 3.55 in for 7 Jul-8 Oct per NWS (≈8 in below normal); none 29 Sep-8 Oct.
* Not obtained (blocked or unavailable): Bales 1997 article and 2002 book text (only Google-Books snippets); the Andreas premises plat cited in the report (not on pp. 708/709/714/716 fetched here); 1870 (13th) and 1872 (15th) Edwards directories; NCEI/NOAA original Signal Service forms; HathiTrust, Chicago Collections, CHM image server (Cloudflare).

### 1.3 Modern anchor (Cook County parcel) and how every lat/long in the CSV was derived
* Source: Cook County GIS "Parcels 2021" (Socrata 77tz-riq7, GeoJSON, `parcels/cook_parcels_2021_block.geojson`), PIN **17-16-324-029** (Chicago Fire Academy, class EX). The polygon (22 vertices) measures 320.0 ft along De Koven and 194 ft N-S (79.2 + 15.0 + 99.8 on the east edge; 4 × 20 + 34.9 + 79.2 on the west edge).
* **Why the parcel is a good anchor**: its vertices sit on the old lot lines — east-edge segments 15.0 ft (the alley) and 99.8 ft (the De Koven-front lot depth, plat 100.75); west-edge vertices every 20.0 ft (the G-sub 20.15-ft lots); south-edge vertices at 65.3, 90.2, 140.0, 190.1, 290.0 ft from its SW corner, which are the plat lot lines 83.4, 108.4, 158.4, 208.4, 308.4 **each minus 18.3 ft**; north-edge vertices at 40.3, 90.2, 139.7, 190.1, 290.0 = plat 58.4, 108.4, 158.4, 208.4, 308.4 minus 18.3 (spread 0.4 ft). Hence the parcel's west edge is 18.3 ± 0.5 ft east of the 1871 Jefferson line (street widening), and its east edge is 358.4 − 18.3 − 320.0 = 20.1 ft west of the 1871 Clinton line. North: 79.2 ft remain of the ~100-ft north half (Taylor widened ~21 ft on this side). South edge: on the De Koven north line (De Koven unchanged; OSM centreline 30 ft south).
* **Origin O** = parcel SW corner (−87.6421042, 41.8690785) moved 18.3 ft along the parcel's south edge (bearing 268.95°): **O = 41.869078 N, −87.642172 W** (±2 ft). x-axis = parcel south-edge direction (bearing 88.95° = grid east; true north of the grid is 1.05° west of the grid's y-axis… i.e. grid north bears 358.95°); y-axis perpendicular. Flat-earth conversion at 41.8693 N (1° lat = 364 567 ft; 1° lon = 271 540 ft).
* Every row in `plan_coordinates.csv` is (x, y) in that frame plus lat/lon from `from_plan()`; the "source" and "resolution" columns say which dimension produced it. Rows in short:
  - Academy parcel corners — GIS polygon, ±2-3 ft absolute.
  - Block corners — plat dimensions above (±2 ft).
  - Lot west lines on De Koven (0, 58.4, 108.4, 158.4, 208.4, 258.4, 308.4) — plat, ±1 ft; alley lines y = 100.75/115.75 — plat.
  - O'Leary lot 133.4-158.4 × 0-100.75 — E½ lot 12 (Andreas affidavit) — plat ±1.5.
  - O'Leary barn 133.4-153.4 × 84.75-100.75 — **estimate**: 20 ft E-W × 16 ft N-S on the west side of the lot against the alley, passage on the east (Musham 1940 plan and text "along the east side of the lot and passing the barn was a passageway to the alley"; Andreas: barn's south line 8 ft north of Dalton's rear ≈ 78 + 8 = 86). If the barn was 16 ft E-W × 20 ft N-S (the Andreas plat as given in the report, 9-ft passage) use 133.4-149.4 × 80.75-100.75 — both variants are in `plan/musham_plan_digitized.csv`/§2.1. ±3 ft.

### 1.4 Post-1909 numbering
Under the 1909 system (State St = 0; Clinton 540 W, Jefferson 600 W; 100 numbers per 660 ft) the E½ of lot 12 (153-178 ft east of the Jefferson centreline) computes to about **574-578 W. De Koven**; **558 W. De Koven** is the Fire Academy's address for the whole block (landmark file: "558 West DeKoven Street (then numbered as 137 DeKoven Street)"). ### 2.2 The two disagreements in the record
1. **Barn orientation.** As given in the report: Andreas's 1885 plat draws the barn 20 ft N-S × 16 ft E-W on the west side of the parcel with a 9-ft passage on the east; Musham 1940 draws 20 ft E-W × 16 ft N-S. I could not find that Andreas plat on the pages fetched (708, 709, 714, 716; the only plat on 714 is "West line of fire"), so I cannot check it, but the arithmetic is: 25-ft lot − 16-ft barn = 9-ft passage (Andreas version) vs 25 − 20 = 5 ft (Musham version). Musham's plan is explicitly labelled "16'x20'" yet drawn 20.6 ft wide × 18 ft deep; Andreas's text (8 ft between Dalton's rear at ≈78 and the barn's south wall) gives the barn's south wall at ≈86 ft, which fits a 16-ft-deep barn on the alley (84.75-100.75) better than a 20-ft-deep one (80.75-100.75), but the difference (4 ft) is inside the uncertainty of Dalton's "forty feet deep". Both variants are tabulated; the choice moves the barn's south wall by 4 ft and its east wall by 4 ft — small compared with the sight-line margins in §2.6, but it should be carried as a ±4 ft uncertainty.
2. **Alley width.** 1855 ordinance and the 1891 plate: 15 ft (plate labels the block-38 alley only "opened by ordinance"; the "15" is in the ordinance table and on the Bunker-side alley "15.5"/"15"); Musham 1940: 15; the modern parcel's east-edge vertex segment: 15.0 ft; 1875 survey of the adjoining blocks: 15; the report gives Andreas's plat 16 and Sullivan's testimony ~12 (Sheahan & Upton's summary of Sullivan is silent on it). I take 15 ft (recorded and surviving in the parcel geometry); a witness's "12 ft" is the kind of figure people give by eye.

## 3. De Koven Street width and sidewalks
* Width **60 ft**: 1891 plate "60" at both ends of the block; Musham plan "De Koven Street 60 feet wide"; Sanborn 1906 "60'" at Jefferson and Clinton; modern OSM centreline 29.4-31.8 ft from the parcel edge (unchanged). (The 1875 survey plat also carries a "40" beside "60" at Halsted, i.e. the street was dedicated at 40 and widened to 60 before 1871 at least east of Jefferson — not verified for this block.)
* **Sidewalk widths: no source found gives them.** Musham: "The sidewalks were of wood and in many places built on posts"; the Board report: "sitting on the sidewalk on the south side"; the stereograph shows a plank walk ≈ 5-6 ft wide on posts (estimate from the plank count/perspective) in front of the O'Leary cottage. Chicago's 1871 sidewalk ordinance widths were not retrieved.

## 4. Why 137 = E½ lot 12 (the "E ½ of lot 13" reading and the numbering)
1. Direct statement: McDermott affidavit (Andreas II:708) "No. 137 DeKoven Street, on the east half of Lot 12, in Block 38".
2. The 1891 plate labels the 25-ft strip east of the G-sub "E ½ of lot 13" (`atlases/grid_blk38_south_dekoven.png`) and puts house numbers at one per 50-ft lot: 141 on that strip (x 83.4-108.4), 137 in lot 12, 133 in lot 11, 129, 125, 121 in lots 10, 9, 8. Sanborn 1906 shows every 25-ft half-lot numbered: 141, 139, 137, 135, 133, 131, 129, 127, 125, 123, 121 — eleven numbers on 11 × 25 = 275 ft = 358.4 − 83.4. Hence 141 = E½ 13, 139 = W½ 12, 137 = E½ 12, 135 = W½ 11 (Dalton, "next to O'Leary's on the east"), 133 = E½ 11 (Lee "two doors east"). The same rule on the south side (Sanborn 1906: 140, 138, 136, 134, 132, 130 … each 25 ft; 1891 plate: 140 on H-lot 2, 136 on lot 18, 132 on lot 19, 128/126 on lot 20, 122 on lot 21) gives 134 = E½ 18 and 130 = E½ 19, which is exactly Bales's tract-book finding for White (E½ lot 19).
3. Musham's plan, calibrated on its own scale bar with the west face of Shult's saloon as the Jefferson line, puts the "137" lot lines at 133 and 158 ft — the E½ of lot 12 to within 1 ft.

## 5. Environmental conditions (wind, humidity, temperature, rain)
* Signal Service, Chicago office (181 W. Washington; records lost in the fire; quoted by Musham 1940 pp. 95-96, 110, 172 from "Musham, Notes on the Weather at the Time of the Chicago Fire"): Sunday 8 Oct: 7:00 a.m. 51 °F, wind SW 19 mph ("a gentle breeze"), relative humidity 62 %, clear; 2:00 p.m. (unofficial) 85 °F, SW 35 mph "a high breeze"; 4:00 p.m. official 79 °F, 22 mph, "still clear and bright"; sunset 5:30 p.m. "leaving a flaming red sky, and the wind died down to a gentle breeze"; "At 9:00 p.m. the same gentle breeze was blowing from the southwest" (Musham citing Sheahan & Upton p. 160; Andreas II:715); "Just before midnight Sergeant Kaufman … read the anemometer on the roof and found that the wind was registering a speed of sixty miles per hour. This velocity is undoubtedly much too high." Musham's regional review (p. 171-72): "There were no high winds blowing; in only a few instances did the wind reach thirty miles an hour … The early hours of the night were very quiet." Reduced barometer Chicago 8 Oct: 7:00 a.m. 30.06, 4:00 p.m. 29.89, 11:00 p.m. 29.94 in; 9 Oct no observations (office destroyed). Forecast issued 7:35 a.m. 8 Oct: "The barometer will probably fall, with increasing southerly winds, and rising temperatures, followed by cloudy or threatening weather during the night." Witnesses: "a high wind" (Rogan), "The wind was very high, and the fire spread very rapidly" (P. O'Leary), "a severe breeze sprang up" about when the fire reached Taylor St (Musham, foreman No. 6).
* NWS Chicago 150th-anniversary page (`texts/nws_lot_1871fire.txt`; maps `astronomy/nws_*.png`): 7 Jul-8 Oct precipitation 3.55 in (≈ 8 in below normal, 2nd driest on record as of 2021); September 0.74 in on 3 days (15, 16, 28); no precipitation 29 Sep-8 Oct; highs mostly 70s-80s, dew points 30-50 °F, winds gusting 15-25 mph most days; 8 Oct highs low-mid 80s, RH 35-40 % afternoon/evening (Aurora observer), SW winds peaking ≈ 35-45 mph (observer estimates, "including within the city of Chicago"); 9 Oct highs upper 70s, gusts 35-45, RH ≈ 40 %, cold front late afternoon/evening, 0.10 in at Evanston; 10 Oct highs < 50 °F, RH ≈ 60 % by 7 a.m., winds W and light.
* Rainfall deficit per Musham p. 88 (citing Andreas II:703): "From July 3, when 1 50/100 inches of rain fell, to October 9, there was only 1 40/100 inches: August 3, 29/100; August 28, 54/100; August 30, 1/100; September 14, 36/100; September 15, 5/100; September 28, 11/100." Landmark file: "2-¾" of rain between July 3rd and October 8th, compared to an average rainfall of 8-¾"". (These totals disagree with the NWS 3.55 in; different station/records.)
* Light: sunset 5:30 p.m. LMT, end of astronomical twilight 7:02, moonset 3:03 p.m., moonrise 12:42 a.m.

## 6. The Saturday-night (Lull & Holmes) fire, 7-8 Oct
Musham pp. 89-90: "Between 10:00 and 11:00 p.m. on Saturday, October 7, a fire was discovered in the boiler room of Lull and Holmes's planing mill at 209 Canal Street. The mill, a two story brick building 60 by 100 feet in size, stood on the west side of the street between Jackson and Van Buren in a neighborhood known to insurance men as the 'Red Flash.' An alarm was turned in on Box 248 … A fourteen mile breeze was blowing from the south … The wind shifted to the southwest, blowing strongly. Within twenty minutes the blocks to the north and northeast were ablaze." Musham's map "The Saturday Night Fire" shows the burned area between W. Monroe/Adams and Van Buren, Canal and the river. greatchicagofire.org (`texts/gcf_landmarks_saturday_night_fire.html`): ignited "around 10 p.m."; "well into Sunday afternoon before this fire was extinguished, by which time four square blocks and close to a million dollars in property had gone up in smoke"; ten blocks north of De Koven. Landmark file: "four square blocks of cottages … Eight blocks from the mill fire was located the house and barn of the O'Leary family." Consequence (Musham/Andreas): No. 6 "Little Giant" and the other West Side companies exhausted; hose and engines damaged.

## 7. The site since 1871
* Landmark file (1971): O'Leary bought the lot in 1864 from Cyrus Clark for $500 and sold 8 Dec 1879; 1937 plaque (now "on the first floor of the Academy, near the spot where the great fire is said to have begun"); 1954 sale to the Chicago Land Clearance Commission ($6,500); 1955 the house then on the lot burned as a test blaze; **Chicago Fire Academy dedicated 15 May 1961**; landmark designated 1971; sculpture "Tongues of Flame" (a.k.a. Pillar of Fire) by Egon Weiner "on a paved plaza outside the building, at the northeast corner of the intersection of West DeKoven and South Jefferson Streets" (greatchicagofire.org: "just to the west of the entrance at Jefferson and DeKoven"). Relative to the 1871 plan the sculpture is therefore near x ≈ 20-60, y ≈ 0-15, i.e. on Shult's saloon/G-sub ground, 90-120 ft WSW of the barn site; the barn site itself (x 133-153, y 85-101) lies in the yard just north of the Academy's front wing (rear wall y ≈ 80.6), the cottages' site inside the front wing.
* No archaeological investigation of the site was found in any source consulted (landmark file, GCF site, Musham, NWS); the 1961 construction and the 1971 designation report contain no survey of the O'Leary lot.
* Overpass queries for a memorial node failed repeatedly (proxy resets); coordinates for the sculpture are therefore inferred from the texts only.
