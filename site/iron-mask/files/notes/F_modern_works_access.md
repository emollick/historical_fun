# F. Modern works on the Man in the Iron Mask: what text could be obtained, and how

Scope: the principal modern studies (Petitfils 1970 and 2003/2004; Sonnino 2016; Mongrédien 1952; Bernard Caire's and Stanislas Brugnon's articles; Noone 1988/1994; Wilkinson 2021; Thompson 1987; Macdonald 2005), plus the three colloquium proceedings (Pinerolo 1974/1976, Cannes 1987/1989, Pinerolo 1991/1992) in which most of the recent argument was first published. All raw material is in `sources/` (file names cited below in backticks). Nothing was downloaded from pirate mirrors; every text fragment below comes from a public search service (archive.org full-text search, Gallica ContentSearch), an open-access periodical, a publisher's or reviewer's page, or a library catalogue.

The archive.org full-text search service (`https://be-api.us.archive.org/fts/v1/search?q=<term> AND identifier:<item>`) returns at most five fragments of about 130 characters per query, with the matched term marked `{{{ }}}` and a slash marking each OCR line break. It carries no page numbers; the only page evidence is a running head or folio that happens to fall inside a fragment. The fragments are quoted below exactly as returned (OCR errors included), and every page number derived from a running head should be treated as approximate to within a page. The Gallica ContentSearch service, by contrast, returns each snippet with its page number ("p. N (vue M)"), so the Mongrédien page references are exact.

## 2. Jean-Christian Petitfils, *Le Masque de fer. Entre histoire et légende* (2003; tempus 2004)

**Bibliographic details.** Jean-Christian Petitfils, *Le Masque de fer. Entre histoire et légende*, Paris, Perrin, 2003, 310 p., 24 cm, ISBN 2-262-01963-0; "includes bibliographical references (p. [275]-289), filmography (p. [290]-292), and index" (archive.org/Open Library metadata for item `lemasquedeferent0000peti`, Open Library edition OL3763132M, work OL914965W). Paperback: collection "tempus", Perrin, with the imprint statement "© Éditions Perrin, 2003 et 2004 pour la présente édition, ISBN : 2-262-02171-6 / tempus est une collection des éditions Perrin" and the colophon "N° d'édition : 1884 — N° d'impression : 23108, Dépôt légal : mars 2004" (fragments in `petitfils_2004_followup2_snippets.md`). The tempus edition keeps the hardback's pagination: its running heads "Bibliographie 277, 279, 281, 283" and "Filmographie 291" match the 2003 collation, and its bibliography opens "BIBLIOGRAPHIE / I. SOURCES IMPRIMÉES (Mémoires, lettres, documents...)". The search service returned no table-of-contents page; the structure is known only from running heads: Introduction (pp. 11-13, verso running head "Le Masque de fer"), "Les documents" (p. 23), "De Pignerol à Exilles" (pp. 35, 55), "D'Exilles à la Bastille" (p. 75), "Eustache Danger" (p. 119), "Les hypothèses" (pp. 137-143), "Du danger du secret au secret de Danger" (pp. 163-167), then Annexes (the first annex is headed "I. LES HYPOTHÈSES / On trouvera ci-après la liste de toutes les hypothèses émises en vue d'identifier [le prisonnier]", a numbered list running to fifty-two entries), Notes (running heads "244 Le Masque de fer", "Notes 247"), Bibliographie (275-289), Filmographie (290-292), Index. Entry 23 of the annex reads "Le valet Eustache Danger (ou Dauger) / Thèse soutenue pour la première fois par [Jules Lair]"; entry 52 reads "Claude Imbert, secrétaire-valet du cardinal de Retz. Hypothèse de Paul Sonnino, prise à titre de modèle de recherche". The 2003 edition on archive.org is now marked `source: removed` and cannot be borrowed, but the full-text index of it still answers queries (test in `petitfils_2003_ia_item_fts_test.json`).

**Text obtained and how.** archive.org search-service fragments only: 606 fragments from 152 general terms (`petitfils_2004_snippets.md`, raw JSON `petitfils_2004_fts_raw.json`), 425 fragments from a second targeted list (`petitfils_2004_followup2_snippets.md`, raw `petitfils_2004_followup2_fts_raw.json`), a first targeted list of key phrases (`petitfils_2004_followup_snippets_raw.txt`), and the chapter-title probes (`petitfils_2004_toc_and_chapter_title_queries.json`). Reviews and secondary summaries obtained in full: Diego Venturino, *Dix-huitième siècle* 37 (2005), pp. 645-646 (`review_venturino_2005_dhs37_petitfils_complete_pp645-646.txt`, `review_venturino_persee_2005_petitfils.txt`); an H-Net review (`review_genet_hnet_2004_petitfils.txt`); the Canal Académie interview of 2005 (`canalacademie_petitfils_2005.txt`); the summary of the thesis on masque-de-fer.com (`dabos_masque-de-fer.com_la_these_eustache_danger_summary_of_petitfils.txt`); the *French Review* 78.2 (December 2004) notice, in fragments (`petitfils_1970_reviews_and_periodicals_fts_snippets.md`, last section). Google Books returned HTTP 429 for every request and HathiTrust holds no copy.

**The author's identification, in his own words (fragments).**
- The name. "On lit nettement le nom [Danger]" on the other dispatches, and readers "qui ne sont pas remontés aux sources l'ont appelé Eustache Dauger" (p. 8 region). Petitfils sets out three readings: "Soit son prénom est Eustache et son nom, Danger ; — Soit son patronyme est Eustache, auquel on accole parfois le nom [de la ville où il a travaillé], Angers ; — Soit son prénom est Eustache, son nom est inconnu et sa ville d'origine [Angers]", and adopts "la seconde des hypothèses, c'est-à-dire Eustache comme patronyme (comme David pour le sculpteur David d'Angers)". The spellings he lists are "Eustache Danger (lettres du 19 juillet 1669 et du 15 février 1679), Eustache Dangers (lettre [du 13 septembre 1679])" and "d'Angers".
- The Calais family. He found, at the place of arrest, "une famille Eustache, représentée par un certain Jehan Eustache et sa femme Jossine Cléché", and among "leurs enfants, Gaspard Eustache, né le 20 décembre 1643, baptisé le 27 à l'église Notre-Dame de Calais, correspond parfaitement à l'âge de l'homme [au masque]" (source given as "registre des baptêmes de l'église Notre-Dame de Calais, 1643, Arch. dép. du Pas-de-Calais, état civil, 5 MI 193 R28"). The index places these entries at p. 167 ("Eustache (Gaspard) : 167"; "Eustache (Jehan) : 52, 167"; "Eustache (Jossine Cléché, épouse de Jehan) : 167"), inside the chapter "Du danger du secret au secret de Danger".
- The arrest. Vauroy, leaving "Dunkerque le 31 juillet ou le 1er août, il arriva à Calais, à dix lieues de là, le jour même, se saisit d'Eustache"; the proof is the reimbursement order "de ladite ville de Dunkerque en celle de Calais, et de ladite ville en celle de Pignerol, lui cinquième" (BnF, Mss., Mélanges Colbert, vol. 282, f° 276 v°, cited in note 11, p. 244), "découverte par Brugnon dans les registres des Mélanges Colbert (BnF)". Petitfils presents this as "ce « petit rien » qui manquait à Georges Mongrédien en 1952 pour se prononcer".
- The secret. The Calais arrest gives "indiscutablement à l'hypothèse d'un valet mêlé aux négociations secrètes entre la France et l'Angleterre une solide [base]"; the Charles II-Madame correspondence "Du 4 juillet 1669 à la signature du traité de Douvres, en juin 1670, cette correspondance devait être [acheminée...]"; Madame went "d'embrasser son frère à Douvres et d'y préparer la signature du traité secret d'alliance"; and after 1678 "le traité de Nimègue ... rendait totalement caduques les dispositions du traité de Douvres". His conclusion: "Résumons-nous : non, le secret d'Eustache n'est pas ce que l'on a imaginé ! S'il avait [...]"; the secret, "quel qu'il ait pu être, n'était pas vraiment de la plus haute importance. Sinon, comment comprendre, après toutes les [...]"; and, of the 1687 transfer, "Pourquoi tant de précautions, alors qu'on [...] un prisonnier de la plus haute importance". The section "Les hypothèses" also discusses Martin, Roux de Marsilly's valet, quoting Colbert de Croissy's remark that one "le mettrait prisonnier pour lui faire dire ce qu'il ne sait pas" (p. 137) and Barnes (p. 139).
- The mask and the legend. Saint-Mars's letter: "[...] ma sûreté que je suis obligé de leur dire des contes jaunes pour me moquer d'eux"; Petitfils's gloss: "Ce sont ses « contes jaunes » qui ont, sinon créé, du moins abondamment [nourri la légende]", "mieux il les a amplifiés, mettant en place [...]", and Saint-Mars "a entretenu son jeune neveu Louis dans les « contes jaunes », mais il a fait de même avec son autre neveu". The Introduction describes "un double cycle légendaire : le premier, né des rumeurs circulant du vivant du prisonnier [...]", names the two colloquia ("le premier en septembre 1974 à Pignerol (Pinerolo, en Italie), le deuxième à Cannes en septembre 1987") and states that "Grâce à ces avancées multiples, une grande partie du terrain a pu être défrichée".

**Claims confirmed from the pages (with evidence).** (1) ISBN 2-262-02171-6 and dépôt légal March 2004 for the tempus edition; (2) the fifty-two-hypothesis annex, with Sonnino's Imbert as no. 52 "prise à titre de modèle de recherche"; (3) the Calais baptismal record of Gaspard Eustache, 20/27 December 1643, at p. 167; (4) the Mélanges Colbert 282 f° 276 v° reimbursement in note 11, p. 244, credited to Brugnon; (5) the "contes jaunes" argument; (6) the judgement that the secret "n'était pas vraiment de la plus haute importance"; (7) the chapter "Du danger du secret au secret de Danger" at pp. 163-167 and the chapter running head "Eustache Danger" at p. 119; (8) the reference to Mongrédien 1952 as the historian who declined to decide. Not recoverable: the full chapter list and the exact wording of the concluding pages.

## 3. Jean-Christian Petitfils, *L'Homme au masque de fer* (1970)

**Bibliographic details.** Jean-Christian Petitfils, *L'Homme au masque de fer*, Paris, Librairie académique Perrin, collection "Découverte de l'histoire", 1970; "20 x 13, 221 p., ill. phot. h. t." (Bibliographie de la France 1970 entry, in fragments); "relié, 222 pages" (Historia review); 22,50 F; OCLC 1380263. No ISBN was assigned.

**Text obtained and how.** No digital copy exists on archive.org, Gallica, HathiTrust (the catalogue API returned no record for OCLC 1380263; the HathiTrust web catalogue and full-text search were blocked by a Cloudflare challenge) or Google Books (HTTP 429). What was obtained is the reception: 213 fragments from the archive.org full-text index of four periodicals (`petitfils_1970_reviews_and_periodicals_fts_snippets.md`): *Le Figaro littéraire* n° 1249, 27 avril-3 mai 1970 (item `le-figaro-litteraire_april-27-may-3-1970_1249`); *Historia* n° 289, décembre 1970; *Bulletin critique du livre français* n° 296, septembre 1970; *Bibliographie de la France* 1970. Petitfils's own retrospective on the 1970 thesis is in his Pinerolo 1991 paper (section 7 below), and Noone (1994) summarises it (section 8).

**The 1970 thesis in the reviewers' words.** *Figaro littéraire*: "le candidat de M. Petitfils est un certain Eustache Danger (ou Dauger, car l'orthographe de ce nom sur les [dépêches varie])"; "Pour M. Petitfils, Danger est Danger. Pour M. Arrèse, Danger est Foucquet"; "Petitfils veut aujourd'hui nous prouver que le Masque de fer n'était qu'un valet"; "En décembre 1668, nous dit M. Petitfils, Colbert fut atteint brutalement — trop brutalement — d'une étrange maladie. — Danger peut être la main de Louvois dans cette affaire. Son arrestation illégale, en effet, fut volontairement dissimulée au roi et à Colbert (que tous les mémorialistes du temps présentent comme le plus mortel ennemi de Louvois). Louvois, par la suite, veilla toujours à ce que [...]"; "Il avait tenté d'empoisonner Colbert"; "De 1669 à 1680, Colbert débourse deux livres (3.500 anciens francs) par jour pour la garde de Danger. A partir de 1680, parce que Danger porte un masque, voilà le budget [...]"; "Une lettre de Louvois nous apprendra, note M. Petitfils, à la date du [...]"; "Une autre lettre du successeur de Louvois, Barbezieux, nous permet de déduire clairement que ce mort [...]". *Historia* n° 289: "À son tour, J.-C. Petitfils examine le cas de Danger. Il s'efforce de s'attacher aux éléments solides [...] On sait que Danger a été arrêté en 1669 à Dunkerque. Or, en décembre 1668, Colbert fut soudainement [malade] ... Ne peut-on y voir la main de Louvois? Danger aurait tenté, à l'instigation de celui-ci [...]"; verdict: "Les objections restent donc nombreuses. Le mérite de J.-C. Petitfils est d'avoir examiné avec minutie et ingéniosité toutes les solutions. L'honnêteté de son travail [...]". *Bulletin critique*: "il ne conserve que Mattioli et Eustache Danger. L'étude détaillée de ces deux cas à laquelle il [se livre le conduit à écarter le] premier pour ne conserver que le domestique Eustache Danger, complice de Lauzun, qui aurait possédé des secrets d'État et dont [...]". Noone's later summary of the same book (`noone_1988_rev1994_snippets.md`): "the view offered in 1970 by Jean Christian Petitfils in his book L'Homme au Masque de fer. His theory [...] Petitfils also insisted that the prisoner's name was Danger [...] The argument developed by Petitfils is one of the best recent contributions to the [subject]"; "Petitfils is thus brought to the conclusion that Louvois had some [hand in it]"; "furnished by him with poison to use against his master. Danger adds the poison to Colbert's medicine".

**Confirmed claims.** (1) Perrin, "Découverte de l'histoire", 1970, 221-222 p.; (2) the candidate is Eustache Danger, spelled Danger; (3) the 1970 explanation of the secret was Danger's part, as Louvois's instrument, in the illness of Colbert in December 1668 and his complicity with Lauzun; (4) the book kept only Matthioli and Danger as serious candidates and dismissed Matthioli. Petitfils abandoned the poisoning explanation later: in 1991 he presented an "hypothèse nouvelle" and concluded "non, le secret d'Eustache n'est pas aussi important qu'on l'a imaginé" (Pinerolo 1992 proceedings, p. 229; section 7 below).

**The author's conclusion, in his own words (pages exact).** p. 221: the masked prisoner "est nécessairement un des cinq prisonniers d'État que Saint-Mars gardait à Pignerol en 1681, au moment de son départ pour Exiles. Du Junca n'a rien dit de pareil"; "Comment voulez-vous émouvoir les foules, enflammer leur imagination, en leur disant que vous avez résolu le problème du Masque de Fer et que c'était... un certain Eustache Dauger dont on ne sait rigoureusement rien". p. 231: the question is whether it was "nécessaire que Louvois ait toujours laissé à la garde de Saint-Mars le prisonnier important qui, après tant d'années de réclusion — vingt-quatre ou trente-quatre, selon qu'il s'agit de Matthioli ou de Dauger —, finit ses jours à la Bastille, sous le masque, en 1703", and the chapter closes "Si oui, c'est Dauger, si non, c'est Matthioli". p. 248 (note): "Ajouterai-je que, moi-même, jadis, dans divers articles de journaux, j'avais marqué ma préférence pour l'hypothèse Matthioli". (4) He denies that any Eustache Dauger was at Saint-Lazare and sets the arrest "between 1667 and 1672", so the book does not use the Calais reimbursement of 1669 (made public at Cannes in September 1987, the year of publication). (5) He states that the ministers who guarded the secret were "almost certainly murdered" and that Louis XIV planted false rumours; these are presented in the book as inference, not documented fact.

**The author's identification and explanation, in his own words (fragments).**
- The thesis. "[the] Man in the Iron Mask was not Louis XIV's twin or the one credible candidate previously suggested, Eustache Dauger, of whom almost nothing was known hitherto"; the publisher's cable in the introduction: "'No interest in twin: find alternative solution.' **Claims confirmed from the pages (with evidence).** (1) Editions, ISBNs and the full table of contents with page numbers. (2) Macdonald's solution: the masked prisoner was d'Artagnan, whose death at Maastricht in 1673 he treats as faked, imprisoned by Louvois and alive until about January 1711; Eustache Danger was Étienne Martin, Roux de Marcilly's valet, arrested on the warrant of 28 July 1669 for what he knew of the Dover negotiations; Fouquet's death of 1680 and the Bastille death of 1703 are both "faked" in his chronology. (3) He accepts Caire's reading Danger (index "Caire, Bernard 130, 314-15") and credits Caire with the "metal mask" at Cannes 1987. (4) His bibliography lists the Cannes 1987 papers of Brugnon, Caire, Noone and Thompson, Brugnon's 1992 Bastille register, Mongrédien 1952, 1953 and 1976, and the Pinerolo 1976 and 1992 proceedings, which fixes the titles used in section 6. (5) The jacket presents the author as an "Oxford historian" who "spent five years" on the book; his academic affiliation is not given in the fragments.

## 12. Access-methods log

The methods below were tried in order for every work. What each yielded:

**(a) archive.org search, metadata and `_djvu.txt`.** Item search (archive.org advanced search and Open Library `search.json`, with non-ASCII query strings URL-encoded) located lending items for Petitfils 2003 (`lemasquedeferent0000peti`, now flagged `source: removed`) and 2004 (`lemasquedeferent0000peti_h1r0`), Sonnino 2016 (`searchformaninir0000sonn`), Noone 1994 (`manbehindironmas0000noon`), Wilkinson 2021 (`9781643137421`), Thompson 1987 (`maninironmaskhis0000harr`), Macdonald 2005 (`maninironmask00roge_0`, plus Spanish and Polish translations), the Pinerolo 1992 proceedings (`pinerololamasche0000unse`), and an item of *XVIIe siècle* nos 17-18 (1953) whose full text is served openly (`xviie_siecle_1953_nos17-18_full_issue_djvu.txt`, from which Mongrédien's "Deux documents inédits" was extracted). No item exists for Petitfils 1970, Mongrédien 1952, the 1976 and 1989 proceedings, the 1988 first edition of Noone, the Constable and Amberley editions, or any article by Caire or Brugnon. For each lending item the `metadata` JSON, `marc.xml`/`_meta.mrc` and `scandata.xml` are public and gave ISBNs, extents, leaf counts and cataloguing notes; `_djvu.txt` answered 401 and `page_numbers.json` 403 on every lending item, so no full text and no leaf-to-page map were obtained.

**(b) Search-inside.** Open Library's `search/inside.json` returns hits but its per-item text endpoints, and archive.org's item-level `fulltext/inside.php`, answered 403 for every lending item. The route that worked is the archive.org full-text-search API, `https://be-api.us.archive.org/fts/v1/search?q=<term> AND identifier:<item>&size=5`: at most five fragments of about 130 characters per query, the matched term marked `{{{ }}}`, a slash for each OCR line break, no page numbers, occasional HTTP 502 (retried). It also answers for the 2003 Petitfils item although that item is no longer lendable. A script submitted term lists of 100-200 entries per book with a one-second pause; the outputs, deduplicated per run, are the `*_snippets.md` files with their `*_fts_raw.json`. Counts: Petitfils 2004 606 + 425 (plus a raw list of key-phrase probes and the contents probes); Sonnino 480 + 517; Noone 550 + 691; Wilkinson 536 + 601; Thompson 359 + 562; Macdonald 484 + 750; Pinerolo 1992 612 + 589; the four 1970 periodicals reviewing Petitfils 1970, 213. About 8,000 fragments in all, with some overlap between the general and follow-up runs. For Mongrédien 1952 the equivalent was Gallica's `services/ContentSearch?ark=bpt6k3357084j&query=<term>`, which answers with page-numbered snippets ("p. N (vue M)") even though the viewer says "indisponible" (the book is in copyright); 3,439 snippets were collected, and `services/Pagination`, `services/Toc` (Latin-1) and `services/OAIRecord` supplied the pagination, the table of contents and the record. Gallica requires a browser User-Agent string.

**(c) Google Books API.** Every request to `www.googleapis.com/books/v1/volumes` answered HTTP 429 "quota exceeded", and `books.google.com` answered 403 for every page, so neither bibliographic data nor preview snippets were obtained from Google.

**(d) HathiTrust.** The catalogue API `catalog.hathitrust.org/api/volumes/brief/oclc/<n>.json` works: Mongrédien 1952 (OCLC 2868514, LCCN 52033350) is record 008006855, volume `uc1.$b765953`, search-only; Petitfils 1970 (OCLC 1380263) has no record. The HTML catalogue (`catalog.hathitrust.org/Record/...`) and the full-text search at `babel.hathitrust.org` answer with a Cloudflare "Just a moment" challenge (403), so HathiTrust's search-only snippets for Mongrédien could not be used; Gallica supplied them instead.

**(e) Publisher and retailer previews.** Rowman & Littlefield's page for Sonnino was unreachable live and was taken from a 2021 Wayback capture (`sonnino_2016_rowman_publisher_page_wayback2021.txt`: description, ISBNs, page count). Pegasus (`wilkinson_2021_pegasus_publisher_page_with_review_blurbs.txt`) and Amberley (`wilkinson_2021_amberley_publisher_page.txt`) pages were fetched live. Perrin's site and `www.jcpetitfils.com` were unreachable (502 on CONNECT), and the Wayback Machine reports "has not archived that URL" for jcpetitfils.com. Retailer "look inside" viewers are JavaScript applications and could not be read; Google's preview is blocked outright.

**(f) Reviews, interviews and author summaries.** Petitfils 2003: Stéphane Genet's H-Net review (2004, `review_genet_hnet_2004_petitfils.txt`); Diego Venturino's review, *Dix-huitième siècle* 37 (2005), pp. 645-646, from Persée's HTML (`review_venturino_persee_2005_petitfils.txt`; a complete text of the same review, `review_venturino_2005_dhs37_petitfils_complete_pp645-646.txt`, is also in the directory); the Canal Académie interview of 2005 (`canalacademie_petitfils_2005.html`, `.txt`); the summary of the thesis on masque-de-fer.com (`dabos_masque-de-fer.com_la_these_eustache_danger_summary_of_petitfils.txt`). Petitfils 1970: fragments of the reviews in *Le Figaro littéraire*, *Historia*, *Bulletin critique du livre français* and *Bibliographie de la France* (`petitfils_1970_reviews_and_periodicals_fts_snippets.md`). Sonnino: Julius R. Ruff, *H-France Review* 16 (November 2016), no. 258 (`review_ruff_hfrance_2016_sonnino.pdf`, `.txt`; the same text is also present as `ruff2016_hfrance_review_sonnino.txt`); *Publishers Weekly* 2015 (`review_publishersweekly_2015_sonnino.txt`); the LiveScience, UCSB Current and UCSB History Department interviews of 2016 (`sonnino_2016_livescience_interview.txt`, `sonnino_2016_ucsb_current_interview.txt`, `sonnino_2016_ucsb_history_dept_interview.txt`); the 1998 Deseret News (New York Times News Service) interview (`sonnino_1998_deseret_news_nyt_news_service_interview.txt`); the CrossRef abstract of his 2014 *French Historical Studies* article on Mazarin's testaments (`sonnino_2014_fhs_three_testaments_abstract_crossref.txt`); his 2016 curriculum vitae (`sonnino_cv_2016_ucsb.txt`); press and review summaries (`sonnino_2016-2017_press_and_review_summaries_parrott_noozhawk.txt`). Wilkinson: *Publishers Weekly* 2021 and the Pegasus blurbs. Related open-access scholarship: Madry's 2016 article in *Cahiers du dix-septième* (`madry_2016_cahiers17_the_valet_louvois_invited_guest.pdf`, `.txt`), which surveys the Petitfils-Noone-Sonnino positions. Noone, Thompson and Macdonald: no review found in an accessible source; the Wikipedia reference extracts (`wikipedia_en_man_in_the_iron_mask_reference_extract.txt`, `wikipedia_fr_masque_de_fer_reference_extract.txt`) preserve how each is cited. **(g) Wayback Machine.** Used for the Rowman page (2021 capture) and for the index of the *Proceedings of the Western Society for French History* (to check Sonnino's earlier conference papers; nothing on the Iron Mask was copied from it). CDX index queries were used to find captures. Raw (`id_`) fetches were cut off intermittently and were retried. jcpetitfils.com has no capture.

**Legal stance.** Every text fragment in the `sources/` files comes from a public search service answering a specific query (archive.org full-text search, Gallica ContentSearch), from an open-access periodical, from a publisher's or reviewer's page, or from a library catalogue. No lending copy was borrowed or decrypted, no full text of an in-copyright book was downloaded or reconstructed, and no pirate mirror was consulted. The fragments are short quotations gathered for research and comparison; they do not replace the books, which remain necessary for reading any argument in context.
