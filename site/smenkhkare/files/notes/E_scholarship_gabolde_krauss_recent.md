## 2. Marc Gabolde (1998 → 2019): "Neferneferuaten = Meritaten; Smenkhkare = Zannanza"

**Verification:** 2009 (a), 2013 (a); 1998, 2001, 2005, 2015, 2019 (c). Where a 1998 page number is given below it is *as cited by* the named intermediary, not checked by me.

### 2.1 Reconstruction (sequence)
1. Akhenaten, 17 years; dies after the year-17 vintage and before that of "year 18" (wine dockets; Gabolde 1998: 137–138, cited WiBiLex 2008; Hornung 2006: 206 gives the same reading with Krauss, MDOG 129, 1997, 227–229). Nefertiti predeceases him (1998: 170–174; 2001: 20f., as cited by Miller 2007 n. 95; Murnane 2001: 17f. and Eaton-Krauss & Krauss 2001: 95 objected that there is no evidence for her death).
2. In year 16–17 Meritaten is raised to coregent in her mother's place (Miller 2007 p. 275 summarising Gabolde 1998; 2001; 2002); Laboury 2002 n. 32 explicitly *rejects* this element of Gabolde's model.
3. After Akhenaten's death the widow/daughter Meritaten is Dahamunzu; Nibhururiya = Akhenaten (Neferkheperure); the Hittite prince Zannanza arrives and reigns briefly as **Ankhkheperure Smenkhkare Djeserkheperu**, husband of the "great royal wife" Meritaten (1998: 213–226, as cited by Miller 2007 n. 23 and HKW 2006 n. 73 — "not probable" — with Eaton-Krauss & Krauss 2001 col. 96 and Murnane 2001: 19f. sceptical; Sadowska 2000: 73–77 also responded).
4. After Smenkhkare's death Meritaten reigns alone as **Ankh(et)kheperure mery-Waenre Neferneferuaten akhet-en-hies** ("beneficial for her husband") — the epithet Gabolde discovered by re-reading the group formerly read "mery-Akhenaten" (1998: 153–157; accepted by Murnane 2001: 16, Eaton-Krauss & Krauss 2001 col. 94, Eaton-Krauss OLZ 98, 2003, 47, Hornung 2006: 207; contested only by Kemp 2016, see §8). Her reign reaches year 3 (Pairi/Pawah graffito TT 139; 1998: 161–162, 184 per Hornung 2006: 207). She prepares a full royal burial (second coffin, canopic coffinettes 266g, canopic stoppers, chest goddesses, pectoral 261p1, statuettes JE 60714/60710, shrines, mask) which Tutankhamun later re-inscribes (1998: 147–226; 2005; 2009 pp. 18–21 of author PDF; Laboury 2002 follows this).
5. Tutankhaten, son of Akhenaten and Nefertiti, born c. year 12 (royal tomb room γ "birth scene" read as Tutankhaten, 1998: 118–124, 2001: 24–27 — rejected by van Dijk 2009 and Eaton-Krauss & Krauss 2001 col. 93 "methodically unsound"), was set aside as a child, then crowned after Meritaten's death; he did not reside at Amarna (1998: 89–90, 195; contra Eaton-Krauss & Krauss 2001: 96).
6. KV55 = Akhenaten, buried by Tutankhamun, aged c. 26–27 at death (Gabolde 2009, author PDF p. 12: "probably 26–27 years old when he died", built on his accession as an adolescent and 17 years' reign); the coffin was Kiya's, re-adapted; band D of the coffin reads only "Waenre"; Dodson's alternative (KV55 = Smenkhkare; coffinettes made for Smenkhkare) is refuted point by point (2009 pp. 3–6, 12–18).

### 2.2 Evidence he relies on (with where I verified it)
- Box Carter 1k / JE 61500 (Akhenaten – Ankhkheperure mery-Neferkheperure Neferneferuaten mery-Waenre – great royal wife Meritaten): read as *two* persons, Akhenaten and Meritaten-as-Neferneferuaten (1998: 178–179 per Dietrich 2024 n. 13). Criticised by Eaton-Krauss & Krauss 2001: 95–96 (per Dietrich), Allen 2009 (read by me: "the first juxtaposition seems clearly to identify Meritaten and Neferneferuaten as two different individuals"), Dodson 2020: 128 ("apparently insurmountable problem"), Kemp 2016 (box "makes it as clear as one could wish that he was married to … Meretaten").
- Stela UC 410 + Cairo JE 64959: palimpsest; underlying text Nefertiti's cartouche + "king's daughter Meritaten"; re-cut with Ankhkheperure mery-Waenre / Neferneferuaten akhet-en-hies (Gabolde 1990 BSEG 14; 1998: 162–165, pl. XXIV; 2005: 281; 2015: 76–77, 80 — per Dietrich 2024 and Kemp 2016 pp. 23–26, who reproduces Gabolde's tracing).
- Hermopolis block 826/VIIIA (Neferneferuaten + Ankhesenpaaten) reconstructed with Akhenaten's titulary above (Gabolde 1990: 45; 1998 pl. XXIVb; accepted by Dietrich 2024 fig. 3).
- North Riverside Palace painted cartouches (Gabolde 1998: 154 fig. 2; Kemp 2016 pp. 20–22).
- Feminine forms and the epithet akhet-en-hies (1998: 153–157).
- Memphis block with Smenkhkare + Meritaten (2015: 72, 76 fig. 12, per Dietrich n. 17).
- Manetho's "daughter Akenkheres" is used by his school for Meritaten (Laboury 2019: 126, read by me: "une trace de son règne semble avoir subsisté dans les archives dont se servit … Manéthon"); Allen 2009: 18 makes the same point ("points to a daughter rather than Nefertiti").

### 2.3 Counter-arguments he answers / his critics
- Against Nefertiti as king: her death before Akhenaten (shabti; absence after year 12/16 — but the Dayr Abu Hinnis year-16 graffito of 2012 removed the "absence" argument; Gabolde 2013 nevertheless keeps Nefertiti dead before Akhenaten, cf. Laboury 2019: 125 "sa dernière mention datée … an 16 … décède alors qu'elle n'a sans doute pas beaucoup plus de 30 ans").
- Against Smenkhkare as KV55 (Dodson, Allen): 2009 pp. 12–18 (age of the body 18–25 per anthropologists vs. his 26–27 for Akhenaten; coffin texts; magic bricks "Osiris king Neferkheperure"; canopic jars Kiya→Akhenaten).
- Against the JAMA 2010 genealogy (Tutankhamun = son of KV55 × KV35YL, full siblings): 2013 argues KV35YL = Nefertiti as a *first cousin* of Akhenaten through the Akhmim family (Yuya sharing 5/16–6/16 alleles with Amenhotep III → Yuya his maternal uncle; KV21A possibly Mutemwiya; KV35EL = Tiye), so that Tutankhamun can still be the son of Akhenaten and Nefertiti (2013: 199–203).

### 2.4 Changes of position
- 1998 → 2009/2013: unchanged on Meritaten and Zannanza; 2013 adds the cousin-marriage model to save Nefertiti as Tutankhamun's mother. 2015 (per Dietrich's and the fr.wikipedia page references) still Zannanza = Smenkhkare and Meritaten = Neferneferuaten. A 2019 academia note (Huber) reportedly summarising a Gabolde lecture in which Nefertiti is the immediate successor could not be verified (academia 403) and no published change of position after 2015 was found.
- His pupil Laboury (2002, 2019) drops the Akhenaten–Meritaten coregency (2002 n. 32) and, in 2019, drops Zannanza/Smenkhkare from the narrative altogether (see §6).

### 2.5 What would refute Gabolde
A securely read attestation of *Neferneferuaten* (with akhet-en-hies or feminine forms) side by side with "great royal wife Meritaten" as two persons — Allen, Dodson, Kemp and Dietrich hold that the box 1k already is that; a datable attestation of Nefertiti alive after Akhenaten's death; or Smenkhkare securely attested before year 17 (a coregency), which would remove the Zannanza slot.

## 3. Rolf Krauss (1978 → 2008): "Smenkhkare = Neferneferuaten (one man); then queen Ankhetkheperure = Meritaten; Nefertiti as regent"

**Verification:** all (c). Krauss 2007 is paywalled (de Gruyter; no abstract in CrossRef/OpenAlex); my knowledge of it comes from four independent summaries: Miller 2007 (same AoF issue, pp. 274–275, nn. 26, 60, 96–101), Belmonte 2013 pp. 426–427 (long paraphrase), Dietrich 2024 (pp. 98–99, n. 11; nn. 14–15 on Krauss 1978), WiBiLex "Echnaton" 2008 §7 (Krauss 2007: 297–307). The other parts of the "Revidierte Überlegungen zum Ende der Amarnazeit" series were not identified by title in any bibliography I obtained (the WiBiLex and Dietrich bibliographies list only part 4); candidates are Krauss, MDOG 129 (1997) and MDAIK 53 (1997) "Nefretitis Ende", but I could not confirm the series numbering — recorded as a gap.

### 3.1 Krauss 1978, *Das Ende der Amarnazeit* (c)
- First collected the feminine markers (pp. 29–35) and held that Smenkhkare and Neferneferuaten are two nomina of one king Ankhkheperure (p. 90), while Ankhetkheperure is a *different* individual (Dietrich n. 14); sequence Smenkhkare (year 1) → Neferneferuaten (year 3) (pp. 90–91, Dietrich n. 15; Helck 2001: 62 concurs). The queen Ankhetkheperure was Meritaten, widow of Akhenaten = Dahamunzu, Nibhururiya = Akhenaten (Belmonte 2013: 423 item 5 "proposed by Krauss in the seventies … now abandoned by its mentor"; Dodson 2020 and HKW confirm the nfr- reading in Krauss's *Amarnazeit*). Rejects the Amenhotep III coregency (pp. 6–9). Gives the Berlin mould 21941 drawing used by Kemp 2016 fig. 8c. (Kemp cites the 1981 printing.)

### 3.2 Krauss 1997 (MDOG 129) (c)
- Wine-docket distribution: years 2–3 of a successor belong to Smenkhkare; year-1 dockets divided between Smenkhkare and his successor (Ankhetkheperure or Tutankhamun); the dockets "cannot rule out an interregnum following the death of Akhenaten and before the accession of Smenkhkare" (Krauss 1997a: 247 per Miller 2007 p. 275); Akhenaten died c. II Akhet (MDOG 129: 238 per Hornung 2006: 208).

### 3.3 Krauss 2007, AoF 34: 294–318 (c) — the sequence "Regentin – König – Königin"
1. **Nefertiti as regent (Regentin)**: at the end of Akhenaten's reign Nefertiti held a regent-like position "without being placed on a par with her husband" (WiBiLex: Krauss 2007, 297–307). Evidence: the unfinished Berlin stelae ÄM 20716 and 17813 with a blue-crowned female figure, the seal impression Berlin ÄM 21331 on which Krauss reconstructs Nefertiti's cartouche beside Akhenaten's (2007: 299–301, figs 2–4 — Dietrich n. 11 finds the reconstruction "quite far from certain"), the Ranefer-house seal impressions, and Nefertiti with blue crown/sceptre. Miller (p. 274) renders Krauss's alternatives: either she was raised to this status toward the end of Akhenaten's reign ("which would not have entailed a full coregency") "or … she ruled during an interregnum of sorts, without assuming full pharaonic status, following his death and before the accession of Smenkhkare". Belmonte's paraphrase adds that in this interregnum she wrote to Suppiluliuma as Dahamunzu, widow of Nibhururiya = Akhenaten, and that Zannanza was murdered (Belmonte 2013: 426).
2. **The king**: Smenkhkare, husband of Meritaten (tomb of Meryre II), succeeds *without coregency* (WiBiLex), later changes his nomen to Neferneferuaten (Ankhkheperure mery-Neferkheperure / Neferneferuaten mery-Waenre) "in honour of Nefertiti", reigns at least into year 3 (dockets + Pairi graffito), and begins the return to Amun (Belmonte 2013: 426; WiBiLex).
3. **The queen**: after his death in year 3 his widow Meritaten, c. 16, reigns as Ankhetkheperure Neferneferuaten akhet-en-hies (Krauss thus accepts Gabolde's epithet and Gabolde's identification of the *queen* with Meritaten, while rejecting Meritaten as coregent of Akhenaten); her funerary equipment is later taken over by Tutankhamun; the "king's son Tutankhaten" block dates to this period; then Tutankhaten (WiBiLex; Belmonte 2013: 426–427). Krauss assigns the Hermopolis "coregency" reliefs to the reign of Ankhetkheperure (Miller n. 26).
- Belmonte's objection (2013: 427): the scheme "needs three rulers of the same name Neferneferuaten" in succession.

### 3.6 What would refute Krauss
Any object naming Neferneferuaten (male forms) *together with* Smenkhkare as two persons; proof that the year-3 graffito belongs to a female ruler (Krauss/Hornung leave this open, HKW p. 207: "the date seems to relate to the king, but the queen is not excluded"); demonstration that Nefertiti was already dead before Akhenaten (Gabolde) or, conversely, that she reigned as full king (Reeves/Dodson).

## 6. Dimitri Laboury (2002; 2010; 2019)
**Verification:** 2002 (a); 2019 chapters (a); 2010 (c).

### 6.1 Laboury 2002 (a) — iconography of "Neferneferuaten, Tutankhamun's predecessor"
- Adopts Gabolde's model "largely" (n. 32: "j'adhère largement au modèle proposé par M. Gabolde") but **rejects the Akhenaten–Meritaten coregency**: it would require a pharaoh to be demoted to queen to marry Smenkhkare and then become king again; "lorsque l'on devient pharaon, on meurt pharaon". - Iconographic corpus: the faces on the KV62 objects usurped by Tutankhamun (statuettes JE 60714, JE 60710; second coffin JE 60670; canopic chest goddesses JE 60686; coffinettes JE 60688; canopic stoppers JE 60687) form a coherent portrait type distinct from Tutankhamun's and from Akhenaten's, compared with Louvre 14.715 (Meritaten as princess); the most "quadrangular" faces (second coffin, canopic set), whose texts are traditionally Osirian, belong to the end of Neferneferuaten's reign (pp. 9–10; n. 26: the coffinettes' original inscriptions were in the name of Ankh(et)kheperure mery-Waenre Neferneferuaten).

### 6.3 Laboury 2019 (a) — the Liège catalogue narrative (Bayer & Laboury, "Les protagonistes du drame" 122–129; Laboury, "Aton vs Amon" 238–243)
Explicit sequence, with Laboury's absolute dates:
1. Amenhotep IV/Akhenaten (name change year 5, c. 1341 BC), 17 years; dies c. 1329 BC "de manière assez inopinée" amid a plague and a military defeat by the Hittites in the north (238–241). Nefertiti: last dated mention year 16; dies aged not much over 30, before her husband (125); she is his first cousin on both sides, Akhmim family, Ay possibly her father (124).
2. **Meritaten**, eldest surviving child (aged 13–14), "à l'instar de son aïeule Hatshepsout … se fait assez rapidement couronner pharaon(ne)", with the complicity of Ankhesenpaaten, setting aside their brother Tutankhaten (c. 6); reign "ne dépassant pas trois années"; she initiates the return to the traditional cults ("il est aujourd'hui parfaitement établi qu'elle initie assez rapidement l'abandon de ce système"); dies before 16; part of her royal funerary equipment reused for Tutankhamun; her reign survives in Manetho's sources (125–126, 241).
3. **Tutankhaten/Tutankhamun**, son of Akhenaten and Nefertiti, born c. year 12 (c. 1335), crowned "vers la fin de l'automne 1327", nine years, dies winter 1318–1317 (127–128); Ay (128–129); Horemheb from c. 1314 (129).
- **Smenkhkare/Zannanza are not mentioned at all** in either chapter; the "Pour en savoir plus" points to Laboury 2010 and Gabolde 2015. Absolute dates (Akhenaten's death 1329, Tutankhamun's accession 1327) leave c. 2–3 years for Meritaten alone, i.e. the Zannanza episode of Gabolde 1998 is silently absorbed or dropped.
- KV55: not discussed in these chapters; Tiye and Akhenaten "rejoints" in the Amarna royal tomb, later moved to Thebes "peut-être" by Tutankhamun (123).

## 7. Hornung, Krauss & Warburton (eds) 2006, *Ancient Egyptian Chronology* (b)

## 8. Barry Kemp (2012 (c); 2016 (a))

## 12. Charlotte Dietrich 2024, CRE 2023 (a)
- Method: coregency requires either a double date (none in the New Kingdom; accession dating makes them impractical) or a depiction of two living, equally royal figures in one scene without hierarchical proportion (pp. 95–97, after Murnane 1977).
- Amarna results (pp. 98–105): Amenhotep III–Akhenaten: no qualifying evidence (Soleb, Sesebi, Kheruef, Huya lintel, 3rd-pylon barque); Smenkhkare: appears with Akhenaten on *one* object only (Cairo JE 62172/Carter 405) and in no scene → "at best circumstantial"; Neferneferuaten: named with Akhenaten on three objects (Munich ÄS 7342; box 1k; UC 410) and shown as a female coregent on the Hermopolis pillar 406/VII + 777/VIIIC (Johnson 2020's join: female with tall crown) + 826/VIIIA (Neferneferuaten + Ankhesenpaaten), plus the anonymous Berlin stelae 17813/20716 and seal 21331 → "the coregency of Akhenaten and Neferneferuaten currently [is] the only Amarnian coregency with substantial and convincing evidence" (p. 105). The order Akhenaten–Neferneferuaten in the box and ivory supports Gabolde's reconstruction of 826/VIIIA (fig. 3).
- Identity: left open; two-ruler model adopted for method's sake; Meritaten's absence from the pillar scene explained either by Gabolde's identification or by her promotion to Smenkhkare's queen (p. 104); sequence Smenkhkare/Neferneferuaten "still up to debate" (n. 18: Allen 2016 and Dodson reversed to Smenkhkare first; "arguments … based on the dipinto in the tomb of Pairy (TT 139) as presented by e.g. Dodson (2020b) have been convincingly rejected by a corrected reading by Reeves (2023)"); Krauss 2007's Nefertiti cartouche on seal 21331 "quite far from certain" (n. 11).

**Eusebius, Greek via Syncellus (Fr. 53, Waddell p. 115):** 8. Orus 36 (or 38); 9. Achenchersês [12]; [Athôris 39 (? 9)]; [Cencherês] 16 ("about this time Moses led the Jews …"); then Acherrês 8, Cherrês 15, Armais 5.
**Eusebius, Armenian (Fr. 53, Waddell pp. 117–119):** Orus …; Achencheres 16 ("in his time Moses …"); Acherres 8; Cherres 15; Armais also called Danaus 5; Ramesses also called Aegyptus 68.
Kemp 2016 p. 30 prints the same J/A/E synopsis (from Gardiner 1961: 444).

### 14.2 What the authors make of it
- **Krauss / HKW 2006** (p. 36, editors; p. 207, Hornung): Akencheres < Ankh(et)kheprure; the "daughter of a king" is the queen Ankhetkheperure whose existence "was archaeologically first confirmed in the 1970s" (i.e. by Krauss 1978); Josephus's *three* Akenkheres = two male rulers (one perhaps a textual corruption) + one female; "12 years and some months" for each may reflect an original 2 years and some months; Akhenaten himself is absent from Manetho, and Horos ≠ Akhenaten (p. 206). Kemp's synopsis also notes the Armenian 16 years. For Krauss the "daughter" fits Meritaten (his queen Ankhetkheperure); he does not use Manetho to identify her further in the pages I could read.
- **Gabolde's school:** Laboury 2019: 126 (a): a trace of Meritaten's reign "semble avoir subsisté dans les archives dont se servit … Manéthon". Gabolde 1998's own treatment (c) not seen.
- **Allen 2009: 18** (a for this paragraph): "for what it is worth, Manetho's tradition that a king of the late Eighteenth Dynasty was succeeded by 'his daughter Akenkherēs' points to a daughter rather than Nefertiti" — used for his (then) Neferneferuaten-tasherit hypothesis, since withdrawn (Dodson 2020: 129).
- **Dodson 2020: 104–105** (b): Manetho follows the Ramesside canon (Amenophis = Amenhotep III, Orus = Horemheb) but then inserts Acenchêrês "his daughter", Rathôtis "her brother", Acenchêrês "his son", Acenchêrês II, Harmais (dittography of Horemheb): "a garbled, misordered remembrance of the Amarna kings. Most interesting is the recognition of there having been a female ruler during the period, with 'Acenchêrês' a passable potential rendering of 'Ankhkheperure', as well as potential distortion of 'Akhenaten'. One king is missing, presumably the ephemeral Smenkhkare". p. 128: support for Meryetaten "has been garnered from Manetho's calling his late Eighteenth Dynasty female ruler her predecessor's 'daughter' — but this now comes up against the apparently insurmountable problem of her being named alongside Neferneferuaten on the KV62 box fragment". Dodson's own Neferneferuaten is Nefertiti, so for him the "daughter" is a garbling.
- **Kemp 2016: 30** (a): "We do not know how Manetho worked … nothing is gained by citing him in support of one theory or another."
- Assessment for the report: the Josephan text is the only one that preserves gender and filiation ("his daughter"); Africanus/Eusebius lose both; the 12-year figures are unusable as they stand; the "daughter" can be read as evidence for a *king's daughter* (Meritaten, or Neferneferuaten-tasherit) against Nefertiti, but Dodson and Kemp show why the argument is weak (garbled order; Nefertiti's identity could be obscured; Manetho's sources unknown).
