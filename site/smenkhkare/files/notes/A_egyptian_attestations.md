# Dossier A — Egyptian attestations of Ankhkheperure (Smenkhkare Djeserkheperu / Neferneferuaten) and the Akhenaten–Tutankhamun interval

14 numbered entries, synthesis, coverage and gaps. All quotations are from the files named in each entry.

## Conventions
- Verification codes for each source: **(a)** read in full; **(b)** read part / abstract / preview / catalogue page; **(c)** known only through citation by another author.
- Transliteration: standard Egyptological (ꜣ ꜥ ḥ ḫ ẖ š ḳ/q ṯ ḏ). Cartouches marked ( ).
- Aten didactic names: **Early form** (before c. year 9): "Ra-Horakhty who rejoices in the horizon | in his name as Shu (light) who is in the Aten"; **Late form** (from c. year 9): "Ra ruler of the horizon who rejoices in the horizon | in his name as Ra the father who has come as the Aten".

### Entry 1 — Dayr Abu Hinnis quarry graffito, "Year 16" of Akhenaten (naming Nefertiti as King's Great Wife)

(1) Hieratic quarry inscription (dipinto) in limestone quarry no. 320 at Dayr Abū Ḥinnis, on the east bank c. 10 km north of Amarna (Dodson 2020: "a quarry at Deir Abu Hinnis, some ten kilometers north of Amarna (fig. 80)"). Discovered 2012 by the Leuven (KU Leuven) Dayr al-Barsha Project (Athena Van der Perre). In situ; no museum number.

(2) Names as reported (I have NOT seen the editio princeps itself, see (5)): full titulary of Akhenaten (Nfr-ḫprw-rꜥ wꜥ-n-rꜥ | Ꜣḫ-n-jtn) and of the ḥmt-nsw wrt "King's Great Wife" (Nfr-nfrw-jtn Nfrt-jjtj) "Neferneferuaten-Nefertiti", according to the published summaries; no Ankhkheperure and no kingly titulary for Nefertiti. Dodson 2020 (a): "on I 3ht 15 of Akhenaten's Year 16 (a little more than a year before his death), Nefertiti was still only King's Great Wife". Dodson: "It records the extraction of stone for the hwt-itn (Small Aten Temple), and thus is an unequivocal statement of Nefertiti's formal status on that day." (Dodson_2020_Nefertiti_Queen_and_Pharaoh_archive.org_djvu.txt, lines c. 2944-2960; the OCR of Dodson's book reads "I 3ht 15"; the date usually cited from Van der Perre 2014 is III Akhet 15 (my recollection of the abstract/secondary summaries, not re-verified — the OCR may have lost strokes). The MONTH must be checked against Van der Perre 2014 before it is quoted.)

(3) Regnal year 16 of Akhenaten, day 15 of a month of Akhet (I or III Akhet — see above). This is the latest securely dated attestation of Nefertiti and the only year-dated text naming her after Year 12.

(4) Akhenaten (both cartouches); Aten name presumably late form (not verified by me). Nefertiti's name in the form "Neferneferuaten Nefertiti" (per summaries).

(5) Editio princeps: A. Van der Perre, "Nofretetes (vorerst) letzte dokumentierte Erwähnung", in F. Seyfried (ed.), Im Licht von Amarna. 100 Jahre Fund der Nofretete (Berlin 2012), 195-197 — (c), known through citation (Dietrich 2024; Reeves 2015 n. 32 "Van der Perre 2014"). Full study: A. Van der Perre, "The Year 16 graffito of Akhenaten in Dayr Abū Ḥinnis. A Contribution to the Study of the Later Years of Nefertiti", Journal of Egyptian History 7 (2014) 67-108 — (b): Brill abstract page read via curl only; body not obtained (Brill 403; no open copy found). Secondary (a): Dodson 2020, Nefertiti, Queen and Pharaoh, pp. 72-73 with fig. 80; Reeves 2015 ARTP Occasional Paper 1, n. 32 ("the recent discovery of a graffito dated Year 16 ... in which Nefertiti is referred to still as great royal wife"; Reeves_2015_Burial_of_Nefertiti_ARTP_OccPaper1.txt line c. 925); ARCE 2021 lecture transcript (a) line 44 ("still alive and calling herself Queen Nefertiti in Akhenaten's year 16").

(6) Implication: Nefertiti was alive and still a queen (not a king) in Year 16, i.e. after every attestation of Smenkhkare that can be dated before Year 16 on other grounds. It (i) kills the old "Nefertiti died/was disgraced in Year 12-14" theory and (ii) makes Nefertiti available as candidate for King Neferneferuaten only if her elevation fell in Year 16-17. Strongest rival reading: none against the fact itself; the dispute is over consequence — Gabolde/Allen (Meritaten = Neferneferuaten) hold that the graffito shows Nefertiti stuck at queenly rank, whereas Reeves 2015 and Dodson 2020 hold that it simply re-dates her promotion to after I/III Akhet Year 16 (Reeves: "presumably indicates it was later").

### Entry 2 — Hieratic graffito in the tomb of Pairi/Pere (TT 139), "Year 3" of Ankhkheperure ... Neferneferuaten ...

(1) Hieratic dipinto (ink) written on the wall over the doorway of the burial passage in the chapel of Pairi (Pere), Theban Tomb 139, Sheikh Abd el-Qurna (reign of Amenhotep III). In situ. Copied by Wilkinson (1820s-30s), published Bouriant 1893 (Mémoires de la Mission archéologique française au Caire I), Scheil 1894; re-collated by Newberry 1923 and Gardiner; standard edition A.H. Gardiner, "The Graffito from the Tomb of Pere", JEA 14 (1928) 10-11, pls. V-VI (hand copy of Gardiner beside Newberry's transcript); = Urk. IV 2024,14 ff. (Krauss 2006 cites "Urk. IV 2024, 14").

(2) Dateline as translated by Gardiner 1928 (a; JEA14_1928_Newberry_Gardiner_Ankhkheprure_Pere_graffito_OCR.txt lines 398-404 and the archive.org djvu text): "(1) Year 3, third month of inundation, day 10. The King of Upper and Lower Egypt, Lord of the Two Lands, Ankhkheprure beloved of [Neferkheprure], (2) the Son of Re, Nefernefruaten beloved of Wan[re]. (3) Giving praise to Amun, prostration before Onnophris, (4) by the wab-priest, scribe of the divine offerings of Amun in the House of Ankhkheprure (5) in Thebes, Pawah, born of Iotefsonb." Titles of Pawah as summarised by Newberry (JEA 14, 9): "(1) 'Scribe of the Divine Offerings of Amun in the temple of Ankhkheprure at Thebes' and (2) 'Scribe of the temple of Amun in the (mortuary?) temple of Ankhkheprure'"; the writer of the hymn was Pawah's brother "Thay or Bathay" (Gardiner) / Batjay (Dodson). Transliteration of the dateline as normally given (Krauss 2006, HKW p. 207-208 (a)): "10/III/Akhet year 3 of ꜥnḫ-ḫprw-rꜥ mrjj /// Nfr-nfrw-jtn mrjj ///" — i.e. the epithets after mry are lost in both cartouches; the prenomen is the EXTENDED (Neferneferuaten-type) form, never the plain Smenkhkare form. Gardiner's caveat (a): "I must confess I was a convinced advocate of the reading Aakheprure (with [ꜥꜣ] instead of [ꜥnḫ]) for the king's prenomen until Professor Newberry showed me the error of my ways ... the disputed sign in the four occurrences of the prenomen is practically identical with the certain [ꜥnḫ] in ... l. 13." Note that the temple name in ll. 4-5 and l. 33 is written with the SHORT prenomen "Ankhkheperure" without epithet. No feminine marker is visible in Gardiner's rendering (Dodson 2020 renders the nomen epithet as "meryet"? — his text reads "Ankhkheperure-mery-A[...] Neferneferuaten-..." in the 2020 book, lines 3321-3323, and "Neferneferuaten-meryet-[...]" in Amarna Sunset 2009 p. 44 (a), which he footnotes as omitting/containing a feminine t — the sign is damaged and its reading disputed).

(3) "Year 3, III Akhet 10" — the ONLY regnal-year date attached to any Ankhkheperure name (Newberry: "the highest, indeed, the only, date of the king's reign — the year three"). DISAGREEMENT recorded: Dodson 2020 (a; line 3321-3323 of the djvu text) gives "III 3ht 10 of Year 3"; Dodson 2009 Amarna Sunset p. 44 (a) gives "day 3 of IV prt in Year 3" — an internal inconsistency in Dodson's two books; Gardiner 1928 and Krauss 2006 both have III Akhet 10, which I adopt.

(4) No other royal person named. Amun (and Onnophris = Osiris) worshipped; the god's offerings are administered "in the House/Mansion of Ankhkheperure in Thebes" (ḥwt ꜥnḫ-ḫprw-rꜥ m Wꜣst per Gardiner's rendering "House of Ankhkheprure in Thebes"). No Aten name.

(5) Gardiner JEA 14 (1928) 10-11 (a) via archive.org item in.ernet.dli.2015.70987 (file JEA_14_1928_archive.org_in.ernet.dli.2015.70987_djvu.txt; OCR file JEA14_1928_Newberry_Gardiner_Ankhkheprure_Pere_graffito_OCR.txt); Newberry JEA 14 (1928) 3-9 (a); Krauss in Hornung-Krauss-Warburton 2006, 207-208 (a); Dodson 2009: 43-46 (a); Dodson 2020: 79-80 (a); Allen 2009: 12 (a) ("The highest date known for Neferneferuaten is Regnal Year 3"); Reeves 2023, "The Decorated North Wall in the Tomb of Tutankhamun ... / the Pairi graffito re-read" — (c), known only through Dietrich 2024 n. 18 ("Arguments for a sequence Smenkhkare - Neferneferuaten based on the dipinto in the tomb of Pairy (TT 139) as presented by e.g. Dodson (2020b) have been convincingly rejected by a corrected reading by Reeves (2023)") and through earlier search snippets which stated that Reeves re-reads the temple name as that of ꜥꜣ-ḫprw-rꜥ (Amenhotep II) rather than ꜥnḫ-ḫprw-rꜥ — i.e. Reeves revives, for the TEMPLE name only, the very reading Gardiner abandoned for the dateline. I could not verify Reeves's argument in his own words.

(6) Implication: (i) a ruler with the extended prenomen and nomen Neferneferuaten counted at least three regnal years; (ii) by Year 3 the Amun cult was openly functioning at Thebes and a "Mansion of Ankhkheperure" existed there with a staff of Amun-priests — hence either the Amarna religion had been relaxed in her/his reign, or (Allen 2009: "perhaps her mortuary temple") the temple was a funerary foundation of a deceased Ankhkheperure. Rival readings: (a) Dodson 2009/2020 — temple of Ankhkheperure (short form) = mortuary temple of the dead SMENKHKARE, so Smenkhkare preceded Neferneferuaten, whose Year 3 is dated here; (b) Reeves 2023 — the temple is Amenhotep II's (ꜥꜣ-ḫprw-rꜥ), so nothing follows about Smenkhkare's priority; (c) Krauss 2006 — the date "seems to relate to the king [i.e. the male Ankhkheperure], but the queen is not excluded"; if it is hers "then she continued the year count of her husband"; (d) Allen 1994/2009 — Year 3 is Neferneferuaten's own count, begun as coregent; Smenkhkare's short reign followed.

### Entry 3 — Tomb of Meryre II (Amarna tomb 2): (a) the Smenkhkare–Meritaten scene; (b) the Year-12 "durbar"

(1) Rock-cut tomb of Meryre (Meryra) II, "Overseer of the Two Treasuries, Royal Scribe, Steward of the King's Great Wife Nefertiti", northern group, Amarna tomb no. 2. Two relevant walls: (a) north wall, west of entrance (Davies's pl. XLI), unfinished ink sketch; (b) the tribute scene, east wall (Davies pls. XXXVII-XL). In situ; the cartouches of scene (a) were destroyed by 1890s vandalism (Petrie 1894: "This inscription in the tomb is now totally destroyed, in the smashings made a few years ago").

(2) Scene (a): royal couple under the Aten rays, the king rewarding Meryre; the cartouches were recorded by Lepsius (LD III 99a) and Prisse (Monuments égyptiens p. 3) as the prenomen ꜥnḫ-ḫprw-rꜥ and the nomen Smnḫ-kꜣ-rꜥ ḏsr-ḫprw (Lepsius misread the nomen as "Ra-se-aa-ka-nekht-khepru", Prisse as "Ra-se-hek-ka-ser-khepru": Petrie 1894 p. 29 (a), Petrie_1894_Tell_el_Amarna_OCR.txt lines 4085-4100 — Petrie used his ring bezels 103-5 to fix the true reading Smenkhkare-djeserkheperu), and the ḥmt-nsw wrt Mrjt-jtn "King's Great Wife Meritaten". Dietrich 2024: 100 (a) summarises Lepsius's record as "𓍹Ꜥnḫ-ḫpr.w-Rꜥw𓍺 𓍹Smnḫ-kꜢ-Rꜥw Ḏsr-ḫpr.w𓍺 and his King's Great Wife 𓍹Mri̯(.jjt)-ʾItn𓍺". No epithet in the prenomen; nomen with ḏsr-ḫprw. No feminine form. Davies 1905 (a): "the figures of the King and Queen, with the familiar cartouches of Akhenaten and Nefertiti replaced by those of Merytaten their daughter and her husband, Ankh-kheperu-ra ... It is somewhat difficult to decide whether the design as well as the cartouches belong to Se-aa-ka-ra's reign" (Davies_1905_RTA_II ... djvu.txt lines 5494-5515; Davies still used Lepsius's reading "Se-aa-ka-ra" in the text). Dodson 2009 n. 17 (a) notes that the Meryre-II queen's cartouche (like the sequins, Entry 5i) omits any feminine t — i.e. the name is written Mry-jtn; this is the basis of the (Gabolde/Reeves) argument that "Meryaten/Meritaten" as a cartouche name could be an epithet-name.

Scene (b) (durbar): Davies 1905 pp. 38-43, pls. XXXVII-XL (a). Date text as restored by Davies from Huya's parallel (RTA III pl. XIII) (Davies_1905 ... djvu.txt lines 4834-4860): "Year [twelve, second month of the winter season, eighth day] of the King of Upper and Lower Egypt, living on Truth, Lord of the Two Lands, Nefer-kheperu-ra, Son of the Sun, living on Truth, Lord of [Diadems], Akhenaten, great in his duration, and the great wife of the King, his beloved, Nefertiti, living for ever and ever. His Majesty appeared on the throne of the Divine and Sovereign Father, the Aten, who lives on Truth; and the chiefs of all lands brought the tribute ... praying favour at his hand (?) in order to inhale the breath of life." Davies notes the numerals in Meryre II are damaged and the date is secured by Huya ("the dating of which is clear, and agrees with what remains of the numbers here"). Six daughters are shown with the royal couple in Meryre II (Davies pl. XXXVIII).

(3) (a) undated; (b) Year 12, II Peret 8 of Akhenaten.

(4) (b): Akhenaten, Nefertiti (as ḥmt-nsw wrt), six daughters; Aten name late form (post-Year 9). (a): Meritaten as ḥmt-nsw wrt of Smenkhkare; Aten rays present (Dodson 2009: "shows him beneath the protective rays of the Aten").

(5) N. de G. Davies, The Rock Tombs of El Amarna II (ASE 14, 1905), pp. 38-44, pls. XXXVII-XLI — (a) full text via archive.org item archaeologicalsu0000fllg (file Davies_1905_RTA_II_Panehesy_MeryraII_archive.org_archaeologicalsu0000fllg_djvu.txt; plates not seen). Lepsius LD III 99a — (c). Dietrich 2024: 100 (a). Dodson 2009: 30-32 (a); Dodson 2020: 68-71 (a). Allen 2009: 9 and n. 6 (a): "Smenkhkare and Neferneferuaten are each associated as well with Meritaten as chief queen, the former in a scene in the tomb of Merire II at Amarna".

(6) Implication: the only monumental attestation of Smenkhkare; it shows him (i) as a king with a King's Great Wife Meritaten, (ii) at Amarna under the Aten, (iii) in a tomb whose last dated decoration is Year 12 — so the scene is later than Year 12 and the tomb's decoration stopped (unfinished) in his reign/coregency. Rival readings: Davies (a): scene = coregency of the new king; Dodson 2009/2020: Smenkhkare as Akhenaten's coregent c. Year 13/14 who died before Year 16; Allen 2009: Smenkhkare's brief SOLE reign after Neferneferuaten (the scene then post-dates Akhenaten); Gabolde 1998 / Reeves 2015 (single ruler): Reeves reads Smenkhkare as the LATER name of Nefertiti ("Meritaten as great royal wife of Neferneferuaten — Carter 001k; as great royal wife of Smenkhkare — Davies 1905 pl. XLI" is for Reeves evidence of one person, ARTP OP1 n. 33, (a)). The absence of Nefertiti from scene (a) proves nothing (she is absent from Smenkhkare's scene whether dead, king, or merely not depicted).

### Entry 4 — Amarna hieratic jar dockets and jar sealings (Petrie 1892 / EES 1921-36)

(1) Ink dockets (hieratic) on wine, oil, honey, fat, meat and other amphorae, and stamped mud jar sealings, from the Central City (Great Palace, Records Office etc.), the North Suburb, Maru-Aten and the Main City. Locations: Petrie's sherds (UC London and dispersed); EES material dispersed among museums (Ashmolean, BM, Cairo, Brooklyn etc.); many items published only in facsimile: Petrie 1894 pls. XXII-XXV; COA I (Peet-Woolley 1923) pls. LVIII-LXIV; COA II (Frankfort-Pendlebury 1933) pls. LVII-LVIII; COA III (Pendlebury 1951) pls. LXXXV-XCVIII (texts by H.W. Fairman, pp. 143-208).

(2)-(3) Docket texts naming or bearing on the Ankhkheperure rulers:
- COA III no. 35 (pl. LXXXVI/XCVIII[35], = Fairman p. 199, estate list item 8): "House of Smenkhkare, justified (35)" (Pendlebury_1951_COA_III ... djvu.txt line 13823), i.e. pr Smnḫ-kꜣ-rꜥ mꜣꜥ-ḫrw; dated "year 1" (Fairman p. 157: "No. 35 might be assigned to Tutankhamun, if the reading mꜣꜥ-ḫrw be correct and if any value can be attached to this term" — line 12759-12760; Allen 2009: 12 (a): "The sole date associated with Smenkhkare — Regnal Year 1, in a label on a jar of wine from 'the house of SMENKHKARE' — could come from the reign of Tutankhamun; even if it is Smenkhkare's, it is doubtful that he ruled for more than a year"; Dodson 2009: 31 and n. 14 (a): "a docket on a wine jar from the first year of the reign that followed Akhenaten's, which mentions an 'Estate of Smenkhkare, [tr]ue [of voi]ce'" citing "Pendlebury 1951: pl. lxxxvi, xcviii[35]; cf. 199"). Reading of mꜣꜥ-ḫrw is partly restored (Dodson's brackets); Fairman himself doubted its value. If mꜣꜥ-ḫrw ("justified" = deceased) is right, Smenkhkare was DEAD in a Year 1 that is not his own.
- COA III no. 36 (estate list item 9, line 13825): "House of Ankhkheperure (Smenkhkare)" — pr ꜥnḫ-ḫprw-rꜥ with the SHORT prenomen; no year noted in the summary list (year not verified by me; the docket facsimile pl. LXXXVI not seen).
- COA III nos. 24-25 (these are STAMPED JAR SEALINGS, not hieratic dockets, in Fairman's section "Types of the individual impressions", pp. 147-148, lines 12105-12111): "Wine of the house of Neferneferuaten, Nefertiti (24)" and "Good wine of the store-house of Neferneferuaten, [Nefertiti] (25)"; (26) "[Wine of the house of ?] the great royal wife". Being stamp impressions they carry NO regnal year. They attest an estate (pr) of Queen Neferneferuaten-Nefertiti (the queenly double-name, not the kingly cartouche pair). Estate list item 6 (line 13819): "House of Nefertiti (28-30)"; Fairman p. 201 (line 16857): "The House of Nefertiti is mentioned on the hieratic dockets" (so hieratic dockets of the pr Nfrt-jjtj exist as well, nos. 28-30, year not checked).
- COA III no. 279 (pl. XCV, 279; Fairman pp. 157, 159; lines 12427-12430, 12862-12866, 12876-12884): palimpsest wine docket: "in No. 279 'year 1' is written partly over an earlier 'year 17'". Fairman (a): "This docket does not contain a double dating, since 'Year 1' is written over 'Year 17'. It records, therefore, the first year of an unnamed king which followed the 17th year of another unnamed king. There cannot be any doubt that the latter was Akhenaten. Year 1 can hardly have been that of Smenkhkare since, as we have seen, his year 1 was probably year 15 of Akhenaten. Thus the docket must be assigned to the first year of Tutankhamun ... This docket also strongly supports the impression that the highest regnal year of Akhenaten was year 17." Allen 2009 n. 14 (a) gives the palimpsest as "rnpt-ḥsb 17 bjt [...]" erased with "rnpt-[ḥsb] 1 j[rp ...]" added beneath. Fairman's premise (Smenkhkare's year 1 = Akhenaten's 15, i.e. a coregency counted separately) is itself a hypothesis; Krauss 2006 (a) instead makes the Amarna years 1-3 those of "King Ankhkheperure" and the final year-1 vintage either Ankhetkheperure's or Tutankhamun's.
- Petrie 1894 (Griffith's chapter, pp. 32-34 (a), Petrie_1894_Tell_el_Amarna_OCR.txt lines 4739-4905): table "Reign of Akhenaten's successor, Rasmenkh-ka": wine year 1 (1 jar? "A single sherd appeared to have a date of the year 1, but ... the numeral was indistinct"), year 2 wine; oil: "No less than twenty-five jars of the second year and one of the third ... as well as two jars of fat of the third year"; "These dates are the only evidence that we have of the duration of Ra-smenkh-ka's reign." "76. The wine series extending from the fourth year of Akhenaten to the second of Smenkh-ka-ra". NB: Griffith assigned years 1-3 to the successor purely on the assumption that the dockets ran continuously from Akhenaten's year 17.
- COA III, Fairman's analysis (a), pp. 156-159: dockets of years 1-4 exist in quantity (year 2 oil especially); "it is possible that some of the dockets of year 1 are of Smenkhkare, it is less likely that those of the year 2 are his, and very improbable that any of the year 3 belong to his reign. On the other hand, any of the years 1 to 3, and possibly those of year 4 also, could be years of Tutankhamun." Year 17 is Akhenaten's highest year ("the year 17 still remains the highest known date", line 12427).
- Krauss 2006 (HKW pp. 207-208) (a): "The wine jar labels from Amarna attest sixteen successive wine vintages ..., 13 corresponding to years 5 through 17 of Akhenaten, whereas 3 vintages correspond to regnal years of his successors ... Up to year 13 of Akhenaten the chief vintners held the title ḥrj kꜣmw. The title ḥrj bꜥḥ is attested from year 13 through 17 and its use continued in year 1 and 2 of King Ankhkheprure. The last vintage that is documented at Amarna dates to a regnal year 1; in that year the vintner's title ḥrj kꜣmw was reintroduced and continued to be used as wine jar labels in the tomb of Tutankhamun show ... Regnal year 1 of the last vintage at Amarna could belong either to the queen Ankhetkheprure or to Tutankhamun." "Year 3 continues year 1 and 2 of King Ankhkheprure as labels of year 2 and 3 belonging to a single delivery of olive oil prove (Hornung, Untersuchungen, 88-89). There are only 3 wine jar labels of year 3."
- COA I (Peet-Woolley 1923) (a): Maru-Aten dockets (pp. 111-116 region, lines 10150-10240 of the djvu text) — Kiya's name erased for Meritaten on Maru-Aten monuments (see Entry 13); the dockets include years up to 17 (not re-verified).
- Sealing with two royal figures (COA I p. 11, 14, pl. X.6; house of Ranefer N49.18; one impression Ashmolean 1921.1149) — see Entry 6.

(4) Other names on dockets: House of Akhenaten (no. 22-23), House of Nefertiti (28-30), the great royal wife (26), Kiya (COA I; year 11 docket, Dodson 2014: 129 n. 54 (a)), House of Tutankhamun (KV62 jars; Entry 5m).

(5) Petrie 1894 (a; archive.org tellelamarna00petr and the OCR file); COA I 1923 (a; EXCMEM38); COA III 1951 (a; EXCMEM44.1); Allen 2009 (a); Krauss 2006 (a); Dodson 2009 (a). Not seen: the plates (facsimiles) of any of these; Hornung, Untersuchungen zur Chronologie und Geschichte des Neuen Reiches (1964) 88-89 (c); Tallet's wine-jar studies (c).

(6) Implication: the Amarna dockets prove (i) Akhenaten's Year 17 (highest), (ii) a run of years 1-3 of at least one successor at Amarna, (iii) that a jar of Year 1 was re-labelled from Year 17 — a successor's Year 1 began within the vintage year of Akhenaten's 17 — and (iv) the coexistence of estates named for "Smenkhkare (justified?)" and "Ankhkheperure". Rival readings: Griffith/Petrie — years 1-3 = Smenkhkare; Fairman — years 1-4 mostly Tutankhamun, Smenkhkare a coregent (his year 1 = Akhenaten 15); Krauss — years 1-3 = King Ankhkheperure (Smenkhkare) counting from Akhenaten's death, last year 1 = Ankhetkheperure or Tutankhamun; Allen 2009 — Neferneferuaten's coregency years overlap Akhenaten's 16-17, Smenkhkare's "Year 1" docket may be Tutankhamun's; Dodson 2009/2020 — Smenkhkare coregent c. Years 13/14-15, dead before Year 16; the "House of Smenkhkare, justified, Year 1" then refers to his posthumous estate in Neferneferuaten's or Tutankhamun's Year 1.

### Entry 5 — Objects from KV 62 (tomb of Tutankhamun) naming or made for Ankhkheperure Neferneferuaten / Smenkhkare

General: Carter's own cards (Griffith Institute, "Tutankhamun: Anatomy of an Excavation", http://www.griffith.ox.ac.uk/gri/carter/) were read for 001k, 048h, 254, 256a, 256b, 266g, 405 and the wine jars (all (a), saved as Griffith_Carter_cards_*.txt). Beinlich & Saleh, Corpus der hieroglyphischen Inschriften aus dem Grab des Tutanchamun (1989) — (c) throughout (cited by Dietrich, Dodson, Gabolde, Reeves). The modern compilations of "reused" objects are Harris 1992 (c), Eaton-Krauss 1993-1998, 2016 (c), Gabolde 1998 (c) and 2009 (a), Allen 2009 (a), Reeves 2015 ARTP OP1 (a), Sousa-Pieke-Bagh eds 2024 (Broschat-Eckmann chapter, (b) fragments).

5a. Carter 001k — wooden box (lid strip and knobs) "of Smenkhkare"

### Entry 6 — The "coregency stelae", seal impressions and the Munich ivory: juxtaposed or paired rulers

6a. Stela UC 410 (Petrie Museum, London) + Cairo JE 64959 (EES 1934 find no. 34/42) — the "Coregency Stela"
(1) Fragments of a limestone stela carved on both faces; UC 410 = eight pieces from Petrie's 1891-92 work (Great Palace area, precise findspot unrecorded); 34/42 was found by the EES in 1934 in the "North Harem" of the Great Palace (Dodson 2020: 77 "one of whose fragments was found in the so-called North Harem of the Great Palace"). Fairman (COA III 232-233, (a)): "the photographs indicate a distinct possibility that 34/42 fits exactly below UC. 410 ... I feel reasonably confident that both do in fact come from the same stela."
(2) COA III 231 (Julia Samson's section, (a), Pendlebury_1951 ... djvu.txt lines 23078-23095): "in the top right-hand corner are two pairs of cartouches as placed in a coregency. The first pair contain the nomen and prenomen of Akhenaten. In the second pair are the names of Smenkhkare, ꜥnḫ-ḫprw-Rꜥ-mry-wꜥ-n-Rꜥ and Nfr-nfrw-Itn-mry-ꜣḫ-n-Itn ... The forms of association used for the two names on this stela present variations from the earlier examples which were written ꜥnḫ-ḫprw-Rꜥ-mry Nfr-ḫprw-Rꜥ and Nfr-nfrw-itn-mry-n-wꜥ-n-Rꜥ." Samson 1951: "There is no legible case of a palimpsest on the extant fragments, but the unusual poorness of the inscriptions on the obverse side may have been due to hurried additions or alterations at a time of political change." Fairman (a): "one must reckon with the possibility that the stela originally depicted Akhenaten and Nefertiti and that subsequently their cartouches were erased and replaced by those of Akhenaten and Smenkhkare. But if this be the explanation, why was the name of Akhenaten erased in the first place and then recut?" Allen, JARCE 25 (1988) 117-121 (c, but summarised in Allen 2009 n. 5 (a)): "On the stela, the dual cartouches of Neferneferuaten are carved over an original single cartouche of Nefertiti and a column of text identifying a daughter of Akhenaten, probably Meritaten"; Allen 2009 n. 12: "the secondary addition of Neferneferuaten's cartouches over that of Nefertiti ... seem to refer to the figure below them: R. Krauss, BSEG 13 (1989) 83-87; Allen 1988; Gabolde, BSEG 14 (1990) 33-47" (Gabolde's title: "Le droit d'aînesse d'Ânkhesenpaaton (À propos de deux récents articles sur la stèle UC 410)"). Dietrich 2024: 102 (a): "four pairs of feet survive, indicating a minimum of four included individuals"; the captions identify the depicted family; Ankhesenpaaten is named on it ("Ankhesenpaaten, who also appears in conjunction with Neferneferuaten on the stela London UC 410+Cairo JE 64959", p. 104). Later readings of the nomen epithet: Samson's "mry-ꜣḫ-n-itn" ("beloved of Akhenaten") is now read by Gabolde 1998 as ꜣḫt-n-hj.s "effective for her husband" (Dodson 2009: 35: "a number of cartouches of Neferneferuaten that had been read as having the epithet 'beloved of Akhenaten' actually bore the epithet ꜣḫt n ḥj.s"; Gabolde 2009 n. 75 (a) gives palaeographic reasons "to discard the readings Mr(y)-n-Ꜣḫ(w)-n-Jtn and Mr(y)-Ꜣḫ(w)-n-Jtn"). So: prenomen Ankhkheperure-mery-Waenre; nomen Neferneferuaten-[mery-Akhenaten or akhet-en-hyes]; recut over Nefertiti's single queenly cartouche.

### Entry 11 — Manetho's Eighteenth Dynasty (Josephus, Contra Apionem 1.95-97; Africanus and Eusebius via Syncellus; Armenian Eusebius)

(1) No object: Greek historiographical tradition (3rd c. BC) transmitted by Josephus (1st c. AD) and the Christian chronographers.
(2) Josephus, C. Ap. 1.96-97, Greek (Niese) via Perseus (a; file Josephus_Contra_Apionem_1.95-97_Greek_Perseus.txt): "τοῦ δὲ Ὦρος τριακονταὲξ καὶ μῆνας πέντε. τοῦ δὲ θυγάτηρ Ἀκεγχερὴς δώδεκα καὶ μῆνα ἕνα. τῆς δὲ Ῥάθωτις ἀδελφὸς ἐννέα. Waddell, Manetho (Loeb 1940) fr. 50 (a): "then his son Orus for 36 years 5 months; then his daughter Acenchêrês for 12 years 1 month; then her brother Rathôtis for 9 years; then his son Acenchêrês for 12 years 5 months, his son Acenchêrês II. for 12 years 3 months, his son Harmais for 4 years 1 month, his son Ramessês for 1 year 4 months, his son Harmessês Miamûn for 66 years 2 months". Note the genitive construction (τοῦ δὲ / τῆς δὲ) — "his daughter Akencheres" (daughter of Oros) and "her brother Rathotis"; Rathotis = Tutankhamun (9 years) is generally accepted; "Oros" 36y 5m = Amenhotep III (or Horemheb); Akencheres I (daughter, 12y 1m) = the female Ankh(et)kheperure; Akencheres II and III (12y 5m; 12y 3m) = doublets or Ay/Smenkhkare; Harmais = Horemheb (4y 1m, too short) etc.
Africanus (Waddell fr. 52, (a)): "9. Orus, for 37 years. 10. Acherrês, for 32 years. 11. Rathôs, for 6 years. 12. Chebrês, for 12 years. 13. Acherrês, for 12 years. 14. Armesis, for 5 years. 15. Ramessês, for 1 year. 16. Amenôphath (Amenôph), for 19 years." Eusebius (fr. 53 (a), Syncellus): "8. Orus, for 36 years (in another copy, 38 years). 9. Achenchersês [for 12 years]. [Athôris, for 39 years (? 9).] [Cencherês] for 16 years. About this time Moses led the Jews ... "; the Armenian version of Eusebius (Waddell pp. 118-119 (a)) gives Achencheres 12 (or 16), Athoris 39, Chencheres 16, Acherres 8, Cherres 15, Armais 5, Ramesses 68, Amenophis 40 (figures as recalled; check Waddell fr. 53b).
(3) Manetho's years: 12y 1m for the daughter; Krauss 2006 (a): "Josephus lists three rulers named ΑΧΕΝΧΕΡΡΗΣ < Ankh(et)kheprure, i.e. two male rulers (one of which might be due to a corruption in the text) and a female, described as a king's daughter. In its transmitted form the Manethonian tradition ascribes 12 years and some months to either of the kings named Akhenkherres. Possibly the figures reflect an original 2 years and some months." Krauss also: "Akhenaten does not seem to have been preserved in the Manethonian tradition. The conventional identification of ΩΡΟΣ (Horos) with Akhenaten is problematic."
(4)-(5) Josephus Greek (a, Perseus/Niese); Waddell 1940 (a); Krauss 2006: 206-207 (a); Allen 2009: 16 n. 60 (a): "Manetho's tradition that a king of the late Eighteenth Dynasty was succeeded by 'his daughter Akenkherēs' points to a daughter rather than Nefertiti ... 'Akenkherēs' is evidently the Greek form of the coregent's throne name Ankh(et)kheperure" (first proposed by Krauss 1978: 43-53, argued by Gabolde 1998: 147-185); Dodson 2020: 108, 128 (a): "Support has been garnered from Manetho's calling his late Eighteenth Dynasty female ruler her predecessor's 'daughter' — but this now comes up against the apparently insurmountable problem of her being named alongside Neferneferuaten on the KV62 box fragment."
(6) Implication: the Greek tradition preserves a female "Akencheres" who was a king's DAUGHTER, and multiple Akencheres names — consistent with a female Ankh(et)kheperure and a homonymous male ruler. Pro-Meritaten (Krauss 1978, Gabolde, Allen 2009 "for what it is worth"); pro-Nefertiti reply (Dodson): the daughter-relationship is the chronographers' default genealogical link and is unreliable (Josephus's list links every ruler as child of the previous one).

### Entry 13 — Kiya: titles, last dates, usurpations

(1) Objects: Maru-Aten (Amarna southern "sunshade") reliefs and inscriptions (COA I 1923 (a), where her name was already recognised as erased and replaced by Meritaten's, though Peet-Woolley did not yet know her name — Kiya was identified in 1959 (Dodson 2014 n. (a): "Kiya's existence was not noted in print until 1959")); Hermopolis talatat (Entry 8; Cooney 1965); the KV55 coffin (Cairo JE 39627) and canopic jars (Cairo JE 39637; MMA 30.8.54 lid) made for her and adapted for a king; cosmetic/unguent vessels incl. MMA 20.2.11 (calcite jar with her titulary — (c), not verified); a wine-jar docket of Year 11 "of the House of Kiya"? (Dodson 2014: 129 n. 54 (a): "at some time after Year 11, when she is named on a wine-jar docket" — docket number not given in the passage read; COA III docket list not re-checked for her name); BM fragments (Kramer/"New Light on Kiya from Texts in the British Museum", JEA 74 (1988) (c)).
(2) Titles: Dodson 2014: 129 (a): "she bears what seems to be a unique title, Greatly Beloved Wife (ḥmt mrrt ꜥꜣt), which is always found as part of a much longer string, explicitly linking her with Akhenaten" — the full string (from the standard editions, e.g. Hanke 1978, Harris CdE 49 (1974) 27-30 — (c)): ḥmt mrrt ꜥꜣt n nsw-bjt ꜥnḫ m mꜣꜥt nb tꜣwj (Nfr-ḫprw-rꜥ wꜥ-n-rꜥ)| pꜣ ẖrd nfr n pꜣ jtn ꜥnḫ ntj jw.f r ꜥnḫ ḏt nḥḥ, Kjꜣ "the greatly beloved wife of the King of Upper and Lower Egypt, living in Truth, Lord of the Two Lands Neferkheperure-Waenre, the goodly child of the living Aten, who shall live for ever and ever, Kiya" — given here from general knowledge, NOT verified against an edition. She is never ḥmt-nsw (King's Wife) nor wrt.
(3) Latest date: Year 11 docket (Dodson 2014 (a)); her disgrace "after Year 11 ... probably after Year 12" and "before Meryetaten was elevated to the status of a King's Great Wife ... Years 13/14" (Dodson 2014: 129); Dodson 2020: 63 (a): "Her coffin and canopic jars — made before Year 9 — were subsequently reused for the burial of a king who seems to have been Smenkhkare ... Kiya's disgrace thus must have preceded the latter's death — which may have occurred around Year 14 ... The fact that Meryetaten was still a princess when these changes were made indicates that Kiya's disgrace occurred before Meryetaten transitioned to being a queen, which appears to have happened around Year 13."
(4) Usurpations: "The vast majority of Kiya's monuments were expropriated for Meryetaten, whose name and titles were cut over those of Kiya, while the shapes of the heads of Kiya's two-dimensional images were recut to show the distinctive extended skull of representations of Akhenaten's daughters ... This includes the decoration of the Maru-Aten temple complex and canopic jars later reused for the burial of an Amarna king in Valley of the Kings tomb KV55" (Dodson 2014: 129-130 (a)); at Hermopolis "in all known cases her image and its label texts were later reworked, not only in the name of Meryetaten but also for Ankhesenpaaten ... Kiya's daughter's names and title strings have been replaced by new ones citing daughters of both Meryetaten and Ankhesenpaaten, named Meryetaten-tasherit and Ankhesenpaaten-tasherit" (ibid.; Allen 2009: 15 (a) and n. 35 with Roeder pls 19, 106). The KV55 coffin foot-text alteration (address by a woman to Akhenaten → Akhenaten to Re-Harakhti; Allen 2009 n. 24-26 (a)).
(5) COA I (a); Allen 2009 (a); Dodson 2014: 129-131 (a); Dodson 2020: 63 (a); Harris 1974 CdE 49 (c); Hanke 1978 (c); Reeves 1988 (c); Gabolde 1992 (c).
(6) Implication: Kiya's disappearance c. Year 11-12 and the transfer of her monuments to Meritaten (as still "King's Daughter") fix a sequence: Kiya out → Meritaten prominent as daughter → Meritaten King's Great Wife (c. 13/14) → Nefertiti still Great Wife in Year 16 → female king Neferneferuaten by Year 17. Kiya as Neferneferuaten (Dodson 2009: 36 mentions the idea) is excluded by her early erasure and non-royal title; the epithet ꜣḫt-n-hj.s "effective for her husband" nevertheless requires that the female king was a WIFE of Akhenaten (Nefertiti, Kiya — or Meritaten, if Meritaten was Akhenaten's wife as the granddaughter-blocks may imply).

## Synthesis — what the Egyptian evidence fixes, and where the models diverge

| Fixed point | Evidence (entry) | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Akhenaten's highest year = 17; a successor's Year 1 began within the Year-17 vintage | dockets, esp. COA III 279 palimpsest (4) | (a) primary edition read |
| Nefertiti still King's Great Wife, alive, on day 15 of a month of Akhet, Year 16 | Dayr Abu Hinnis graffito (1) | (b)/(a) secondary; month uncertain (I vs III Akhet) |
| A king with extended prenomen + nomen Neferneferuaten reached Year 3 (III Akhet 10) and had a Theban "House of Ankhkheperure" with Amun-priests | TT139 (2) | (a) Gardiner |
| The extended-prenomen ruler was female (ꜥnḫt-ḫprw-rꜥ; mꜣꜥt-ḫrw; ꜣḫt-n-hj.s) | bezel UC 1927; 261p; 266g; UC 410; Munich ivory (5, 6, 7) | (a) Gabolde 2009 / Dietrich 2024 |
| Neferneferuaten's names follow Akhenaten's on three objects, once with Meritaten as Great Wife; Smenkhkare's follow Akhenaten's on one (405) | 001k, UC 410, Munich ÄS 7342; Carter 405 (5, 6) | (a) |
| A male-style king Ankhkheperure Smenkhkare-djeserkheperu had Meritaten as Great Wife (Amarna tomb 2; Memphis) and a hall at Amarna; no feminine form ever with his nomen; no year date of his own beyond "Year 1 ... House of Smenkhkare [justified?]" | (3, 9, 4) | (a) |
| Tutankhaten was "King's Son of his body" at Amarna; Tutankhamun reigned ≥ 9-10 years; the Restoration Stela credits no predecessor with restoring Amun | (8, 5m, 12) | (a) |
| Manetho: a king's DAUGHTER "Akencheres" (12y 1m), then her brother Rathotis (9y), then two more Akencheres | (11) | (a) |

Divergent models (all consistent with the fixed points above):
- A. Two rulers, Smenkhkare first (coregent c. Y13/14, dies c. Y14/15) then Neferneferuaten = Nefertiti (coregent Y16/17, sole ruler to her Year 3, then Tutankhaten): Dodson 2009/2020; Allen 2016; Eaton-Krauss. Weak points: no object shows Smenkhkare with Akhenaten in a coregency composition; the docket "House of Smenkhkare, justified, Year 1" must be Neferneferuaten's or Tutankhamun's Year 1.
- B. Two rulers, Neferneferuaten first (coregent Y16-17, Year 3 = her own count), then a brief Smenkhkare, then Tutankhaten: Allen 1994/2009 (with Neferneferuaten = a daughter, ultimately "Neferneferuaten-tasherit"); Gabolde 1998 (Neferneferuaten = Meritaten, later Smenkhkare's queen). Weak points: Meritaten's "demotion"; the Meryre II scene must post-date Akhenaten though the tomb's decoration stops with the Year-12 durbar and the scene is under the Aten.
- C. One ruler with name change: Nefertiti as Neferneferuaten (coregent) → Smenkhkare (sole king): Harris 1973-77, Samson, Reeves 2001/2015/2023, Kemp 2016 (order Smenkhkare → Neferneferuaten in Helck 2001, Krauss 1978). Weak points: the strict complementary distribution of plain/extended prenomen; the male body in KV55 if it is Smenkhkare; Meritaten as "Great Wife" of a female king.
- D. Krauss 1978/2006: male Ankhkheperure Smenkhkare (= Neferneferuaten-mery-Waenre, 2-3 years) followed by his widow Ankhetkheperure ... ꜣḫt-n-hj.s (= Meritaten). Weak points: 001k names Neferneferuaten and Meritaten as distinct; the feminine forms occur with the SAME epithets as the "male" set.
