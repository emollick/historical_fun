no case at hand in which the presiden
tial aspirant who made speeches was
not defeated by an overwhelming ma-,
jority, and forever afterwards relegated
to obscurity.
The most noted case of this kind was
in 1862, when Winfield Scott, by long
odds the best soldier tbe United States
had ever uniformed up to that date, was

istracy of the nation. He stayed at

Poor old Scott went out on the Btump

lor an oratorical effort, but had been

THE STATUTE MANDATORY.
A number of eminent lawyers who
were consulted yesterday, asserted that
