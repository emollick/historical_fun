How He Came to be a Candidate for the
Parkersburg Postoffice and Hotc the
Senator Came to Endorse Him.
Special dispatch to the Wheeling Register.
Washington, March 14.—Mr. John S.
Camden, who whs to day appointed post
master at Parkersburg, is a brother of tren
ator Camden and a well-known citizen and
active Democrat. He was eudorsed for the
position by the united West Virginia dele
gation. Senator Camden naturally felt some
delicacy in recommending his brother for
the place and when, on March 10, he filed
the endorsement and papers in the case, he
enclosed a communication for the President
in which he said "In joining in this rec
ommendation T desire to place on file the
following explanation. Mr. Camden is my
brother and is the first near relative that I
have ever recommended for appointment.—
In 1884, when President Cleveland was first
elected, I was a member of the Senate, and
without my knowledge Mr. Camden became
perhaps the most strongly endorsed appli
cant for postmaster. At that time I advised
him that being in the Senate I could not
recommend his appointment, and at my re
quest his application was withdrawn. At
the last election (1892) he was urged for the
nomination for sheriff of his county. 1
suggested to him that if the Democrats were
successful in electing a President there
would be no reason why he should not be
come an applicant for postmaster, with good
chances for success, as I at. that time had no
expectation of embarrassing him by being
again in the Senate. Ho no doubt adopted
the suggestion made by me and declined to
become a candidate for the nomination for
sheriff. Under these circumstances I do
not think it would be fair to him, since I
happened to be in the Senate, to withhold
my recommendation, as I believe be is le
gally the choice of the patrons of the office
and in fact, so far as I am advised, there is
no other application aud no opposition to
the appointment.”
