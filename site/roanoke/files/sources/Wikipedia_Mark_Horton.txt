From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

British archaeologist (born 1956)

[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Mark_Horton.jpg)Horton in 2010

**Mark Chatwin Horton** , [FSA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Society_of_Antiquaries_of_London "Fellow of the Society of Antiquaries of London"), (born 15 February 1956) is a British maritime and historical [archaeologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaeologist "Archaeologist"), television presenter, and writer.

## Academic career

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Mark_Horton_\(archaeologist\)&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Academic career")]

Horton attended [Peterhouse, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peterhouse,_Cambridge "Peterhouse, Cambridge"), graduating and receiving a doctorate. He is Professor of Archaeology and Cultural Heritage at the [Royal Agricultural University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Agricultural_University "Royal Agricultural University")[1] in [Cirencester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cirencester "Cirencester"), and Emeritus Professor at the [University of Bristol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bristol "University of Bristol"). One of his former students is the archaeologist and television presenter [Sam Willis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Willis "Sam Willis"). He is part of a project to establish the Cultural Heritage Institute in the former [Great Western Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Western_Railway "Great Western Railway") carriage works at [Swindon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swindon "Swindon"), offering research and masters training from 2020.[2]

He has conducted excavations in [Zanzibar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zanzibar "Zanzibar"), Egypt, the Caribbean, North America, Central America and France, as well as sites in Britain. His chief publications are on the Swahili site of [Shanga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shanga,_Pate_Island "Shanga, Pate Island"), [Kenya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenya "Kenya"),[3] between 1980 and 1986 and more recently sites on the islands of Zanzibar and Pemba, notably Tumbatu, Ras Mkumbuu, Mtambwe Mkuu and Chwaka.

His other excavations include the Scottish [Darien scheme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darien_scheme "Darien scheme") (1698–1700) in [Panama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panama "Panama"); the [Cistercian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cistercian "Cistercian") Abbey of Grosbot (Charente, France); the [Bishop's Palace, Wells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bishop's_Palace,_Wells "Bishop's Palace, Wells"), Somerset; a medieval farmstead at Carscliffe, [Somerset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somerset "Somerset"); Fishmongers Cave, [Alveston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alveston "Alveston"), Gloucestershire, and he worked at [Berkeley Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berkeley_Castle "Berkeley Castle") (Gloucestershire) and [Repton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Repton "Repton"). In 2008-19 he undertook survey and excavation work in the Kherlen Valley in [Mongolia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongolia "Mongolia"). Between 2011 and 2019 he worked with the Sealinks Project, undertaking excavations on Pemba, Zanzibar, Mafia, [Anjouan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anjouan "Anjouan"), [Sri Lanka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sri_Lanka "Sri Lanka") and [Madagascar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madagascar "Madagascar"). A project in East Pemba combined archaeological investigations with ethnography and anthropology.[4][5]

He also has an interest in [Isambard Kingdom Brunel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isambard_Kingdom_Brunel "Isambard Kingdom Brunel") and directed the digitisation of the engineer's sketch books and letters at Bristol University library, which project was grant-aided by the Arts and Humanities Research Council in 2003.[6]

He is currently Pro Vice-Chancellor, Research & Enterprise at the [Royal Agricultural University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Agricultural_University "Royal Agricultural University").[7]

He was elected a Fellow of the [Society of Antiquaries of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Society_of_Antiquaries_of_London "Society of Antiquaries of London") on 7 May 1992.[8]

## TV career

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Mark_Horton_\(archaeologist\)&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: TV career")]

Horton's first television appearances sprang from his academic work in East Africa. He contributed to a programme by Time Life Television _Lost Civilisations – Africa_ in 1996 and to a [Channel 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Channel_4 "Channel 4") television programme on the Atlantic slave trade in 1998.

He was closely involved in the inception of the long-running [Channel 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Channel_4 "Channel 4") television series _[Time Team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_Team "Time Team")_ and the third episode was filmed in his home town of [Much Wenlock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Much_Wenlock "Much Wenlock"), [Shropshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shropshire "Shropshire"), in 1994. He has appeared on several subsequent programmes including [Hylton Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hylton_Castle "Hylton Castle"), [Sunderland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Sunderland "City of Sunderland"), in 1995, and [Aston Eyre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aston_Eyre "Aston Eyre"), [Shropshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shropshire "Shropshire"), in 1998.[9] In 2000 he acted as tiles specialist on _Time Team Live_.[10] Having invited _Time Team_ to investigate the bones found by cavers in a cave in the village of [Alveston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alveston "Alveston"), [Gloucestershire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gloucestershire "Gloucestershire"), he appeared in the programme on this site shown in 2001.[11] In 2008, further work on the site was included in a National Geographic / Channel Five documentary, _Julius Caesar and the Druids_.[12]

He was a co-presenter on two series of the BBC Two production, _Time Flyers_ 2002 – 2003. In 2004 he presented BBC Scotland's programme _Darien: Disaster in Paradise_ , which was highly commended in the archaeological film category at that year's British Archaeological Awards.

Between 2005 and 2016 Horton was one of the team of presenters on the programme _[Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coast_\(TV_series\) "Coast \(TV series\)")_ , exploring the coastline of Britain.[13][14] He also presents occasional pieces for BBC1 _Inside Out_ , West and South West Regions.[15] He was the archaeological consultant on the TV drama _[Bonekickers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonekickers "Bonekickers")_ , shown on BBC One in 2008.

In 2017, he appeared in an episode of the [Science Channel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_Channel "Science Channel") documentary _Mysteries of the Missing_ (series 1, episode 6), investigating the possible relocation of the 16th-century English settlement of [Roanoke Colony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roanoke_Colony "Roanoke Colony") in Virginia from Roanoke to [Croatoan Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hatteras_Island "Hatteras Island"). He is a regular contributor and presenter on Science Channel _[What on Earth?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/What_on_Earth%3F_\(American_TV_program\) "What on Earth? \(American TV program\)")_.

## Interests

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Mark_Horton_\(archaeologist\)&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Interests")]

Horton is a keen sailor and enjoys dinghy-sailing on the [River Severn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Severn "River Severn") and restoring his historic 26-foot (7.9 m) yacht _Mignonette_ , a Lone Gull design of [Maurice Griffiths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maurice_Griffiths "Maurice Griffiths") built in 1946–47.[16] He lives on the banks of the Severn in a sixteenth-century house associated with [Sir Francis Drake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_Francis_Drake "Sir Francis Drake").[17]
