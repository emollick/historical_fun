From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

2020 non-fiction book by Scott Dawson

The Lost Colony and Hatteras Island [](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:The_Lost_Colony_and_Hatteras_Island.jpg)  
---  
Author| Scott Dawson  
Genre| Non-fiction  
Published| 2020  
Publication place| United States  
  
**The Lost Colony and Hatteras Island** is a 2020 non-fiction book by Scott Dawson, a researcher from [Hatteras Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hatteras_Island "Hatteras Island"). The book argues that the colonists of the [Roanoke Colony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roanoke_Colony "Roanoke Colony") were not lost, but were taken in by the [Croatoan tribe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatan "Croatan") on Hatteras Island and assimilated into their society. Dawson developed his hypothesis through archaeological fieldwork and historical research conducted alongside archaeologist [Mark Horton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Horton_\(archaeologist\) "Mark Horton \(archaeologist\)") of the [University of Bristol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bristol "University of Bristol") and others affiliated with the Croatoan Archaeological Society. The book received coverage from outlets including _[The New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times "The New York Times")_ and _[The Virginian-Pilot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Virginian-Pilot "The Virginian-Pilot")_ , though some scholars questioned its conclusions, arguing that the physical evidence could be explained by trade rather than settlement.

## Synopsis

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=The_Lost_Colony_and_Hatteras_Island&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Synopsis")]

The book attempts to dismantle the long-standing mystery of the [Roanoke Colony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roanoke_Colony "Roanoke Colony") by arguing that the colonists were taken in by the [Croatoan tribe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatan "Croatan") on [Hatteras Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hatteras_Island "Hatteras Island") and assimilated into their society.

## Background

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=The_Lost_Colony_and_Hatteras_Island&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Background")]

Dawson, a researcher from Hatteras Island, worked with archaeologists, geologists, botanists and other researchers,[1] including archaeologist [Mark Horton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Horton_\(archaeologist\) "Mark Horton \(archaeologist\)") from the [University of Bristol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bristol "University of Bristol"), to collect evidence for the colony's fate.[2] They established the Croatoan Archaeological Society,[1] and conducted archaeological fieldwork and historical research at sites like Buxton, Frisco and Hatteras.[3] Evidence of English activity on Hatteras like guns, mixed architecture,[4] and large-scale [blacksmithing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blacksmith "Blacksmith") during the 16th century was presented by them as proof that the colonists had moved there.[5][6]

The accounts of English explorers like [Arthur Barlowe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Barlowe "Arthur Barlowe") and [John Lawson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Lawson_\(explorer\) "John Lawson \(explorer\)") provided a basis for Dawson's hypothesis,[7] including claims that blue-eyed people were found living among the Croatoans years later.[8]

## Reception

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=The_Lost_Colony_and_Hatteras_Island&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Reception")]

The book's claims were reported by multiple newspapers at the time of its publication, including _[The Virginian-Pilot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Virginian-Pilot "The Virginian-Pilot")_[9] and _[The New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times "The New York Times")_.[2] Peter Vankevich of the _[Ocracoke Observer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ocracoke_Observer "Ocracoke Observer")_ wrote that "Dawson’s book is a good history/archeology primer. It is well-written and accessible for the lay reader."[10]

However, some scholars rejected its claims due to a lack of solid proof, arguing that English material artifacts could have reached Hatteras Island through trade instead of settlement.[11]
