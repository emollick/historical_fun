## Was Hay in Washington on 21 November 1864?
Yes, so far as the record shows. He returned from City Point at 7 a.m. on Tuesday 15 Nov. and was writing for Lincoln in Washington on 23, 25, 27 and 28 Nov. and 1 Dec.; no absence between 15 Nov. and 18 Dec. is recorded in the 1908 diary extracts, Thayer, or Basler. Thayer 1:218: "During the ensuing months we have only sparse records of Hay's life."

## What Hay was writing that week
The Spencer letter is the key document: on 25 Nov. Hay says Lincoln "fully intended" to write a New York banquet note himself and that Hay therefore "did not prepare a letter for him," but that Lincoln was "crowded out" by "the crush." Burlingame (unedited *Life* vol. 2 p. 3854) reads this as showing that Lincoln, "unusually preoccupied," also lacked time for the Bixby letter, so that "For the president's signature Hay wrote a letter of condolence." The letter equally shows (a) that Hay distinguished between letters Lincoln wrote himself and letters Hay "prepared" for him, and (b) that Hay could tell a correspondent that a letter had not been prepared at all; it says nothing about Bixby. Bullard (pp. 134-135) characterizes the Hay of 1864 as "the untried young man of 1864" whose known compositions for Lincoln (Boker, Rowland) show "no glimmer of Lincoln."

## Hay's habits of record
Hay pasted a clipping of the Bixby letter into two scrapbooks (Brown University, donated 1954 per Burlingame 1999 or "late 1950s" per Burlingame 1995 p. 70; Library of Congress vol. 54, donated 1952, which also holds clippings of Lincoln's letters to Phillips (21 Nov. 1864), Wyman (11 Dec. 1861), Loomis (12 May 1864) and the New York Committee (2 Dec. 1863)) (Burlingame 1995 pp. 70-71; Emerson 2008 pp. 94-96 and nn. 40-43 describe the LC scrapbooks OV 7 and OV 40 as full of Lincoln material including photostats of the Gettysburg Address and Second Inaugural). He kept no diary entry, letter or memorandum of the Bixby letter, and Robert Lincoln reported that Hay "had never seen any draft or copy of this letter" (Towers to Tweedale, 22 Apr. 1908, Emerson 2008 n. 68).
