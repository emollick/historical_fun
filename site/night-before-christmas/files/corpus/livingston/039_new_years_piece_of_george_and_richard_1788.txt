title: The New Year's Piece of George and Richard (1788) - verse portion
author: Henry Livingston Jr.
date: 1788
source: Country Journal and Poughkeepsie Advertiser (Poughkeepsie Journal), 1 Jan 1788 (FDR Library); verse embedded in a prose "Scene from the Comedy called the Printers Prentices"; not in MS; site: highly probably Henry's by style. URL: https://www.henrylivingston.com/writing/poetry/carrieraddresses/pj1788.htm (fetched via https://web.archive.org/web/20250522013733/https://www.henrylivingston.com/writing/poetry/carrieraddresses/pj1788.htm)
form: anapestic tetrameter

attribution: doubtful
notes: Comic. Only the 14-line verse speech is kept; the surrounding prose dialogue is omitted. Attributed on style (echoes of the 1787 address).

And now our good patrons pray melt into pity
Nor expect at our hands a more dolorous ditty;
For our muse, all exhausted, declares on her honor
We ought not in conscience ask any more from her;
She blows too her fingers, and pleads the cold weather,
And vows her ideas are frozen together.
But still we believe not a syllable true
But that the fly baggage has New-Year in view;
And means with her sisters, those tight pretty lasses,
To romp on the top of the airy parnassus;
Or else with Apollo, the graces, and Venus,
Sly Cupid, fierce Mars, and the jolly Silenus,
To tipple stew'd Quaker at Bacchus's hall,
And there in fandango out caper us all. ---
