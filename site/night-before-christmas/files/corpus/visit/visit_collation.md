# Collation of "A Visit from St. Nicholas": Troy Sentinel 1823 / Hoffman 1837 / Poems 1844

Line-by-line comparison of the three witnesses in this folder, with three further witnesses in the appendices. All texts have 56 lines; line numbers are the same in every witness (no lines are added, dropped or reordered in any of them).

## 2. Summary of the verbal variants (1823 → 1837 → 1844)

| l. | 1823 | 1837 | 1844 | comment |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 6 | sugar plums danc'd **in** their heads | sugar-plums danced **through** their heads | sugar-plums danced **in** their heads | 1837 alone reads 'through'; 1844 returns to the 1823 reading |
| 10 | I **sprung** from the bed | I **sprang** | I **sprang** | verb form changed from 1837 on |
| 21 | "Now! Dasher, now! Dancer, now! Prancer, **and** Vixen, | "Now, Dasher! now, Dancer! now, Prancer! **now,** Vixen! | "Now, Dasher! now, Dancer! now, Prancer **and** Vixen! | 1837 adds a fourth 'now'; 1844 restores 'Prancer and Vixen' |
| 22 | on! **Dunder and Blixem**; | on! **Donder and Blixen**— | on, **Donder and Blitzen**! | the reindeer names change twice; 'Blitzen' first appears in 1844 |
| 25 | As **dry leaves before** the wild hurricane fly, | As **leaves that before** | As **dry leaves that before** | 1844 conflates both earlier readings |
| 35 | A bundle of toys **was flung** on his back, | he **had flung** | he **had flung** | changed from 1837 on |
| 40 | the beard **of** his chin | the beard **on** his chin | the beard **of** his chin | 1837 alone reads 'on' |
| 53 | He **sprung** to his sleigh | He **sprang** | He **sprang** | as l. 10 |

Spelling-level differences: l. 1 thro' → through (1837, 1844); l. 7 Mama → mamma (1837) → Mamma (1844); l. 36 peddler → pedlar (1837, 1844); l. 50 jirk (1823, 1837) → jerk (1844); l. 44 'bowl full' (1823, 1837) → 'bowlfull' (1844); elided preterites danc'd, call'd, dress'd, tarnish'd, laugh'd, fill'd, turn'd (1823) are progressively expanded (1837: called, dressed, filled, turned but tarnish'd, look'd, laugh'd; 1844: dressed, tarnished, laughed, but look'd, fill'd).

Things that do **not** vary: 'St. Nicholas' (ll. 4, 28, 32) and 'St. Nick' (l. 18) are identical in all three; the closing wish is **'Happy Christmas to all'** in every witness of the poem (1823, 1830, 1837, 1840, 1844; the holograph page ends at l. 24) — 'Merry Christmas' never occurs in the poem; the only 'merry Christmas' in the record is in Holley's 1823 editorial preface ('that they may have many a merry Christmas'). The reindeer names Dasher, Dancer, Prancer, Vixen, Comet, Cupid are stable; only the seventh and eighth vary.

Punctuation habits: the 1823 newspaper prints an opening quotation mark at the start of every line of the reindeer speech (ll. 21-24) and leaves the final line unquoted; 1837 and 1844 quote only the first line of the speech and put the last line in quotation marks (1837 with '!', 1844 with '.'). 1844 alone italicizes the reindeer names and the last line and sets 'St. Nicholas' (l. 4) in small capitals; 1823 capitalizes 'Toys' (ll. 28, 35); 1844 keeps that capital, 1837 lowers it.

## 3. Relations between the witnesses (observation, not a stemma)

* 1837 (Hoffman) is not a straight reprint of 1823: it introduces 'through', 'sprang', 'he had flung', 'pedlar', 'Donder ... Blixen', 'now, Prancer! now, Vixen!', 'leaves that before', 'through their heads', 'beard on his chin'. Bryant 1840 reproduces the 1837 text almost exactly (Appendix B), so 1840 is a witness to Hoffman, not an independent text.
* 1844 (Moore's own edition) agrees with 1837 against 1823 in 'through', 'sprang', 'he had flung', 'pedlar', 'Donder', and the quoted last line, but returns to 1823 in 'danced in their heads', 'Prancer and Vixen', 'dry leaves', 'beard of his chin', and introduces 'Blitzen', 'jerk' (already in 1830 and 1840) and 'bowlfull'.
* The 1830 broadside (Appendix A), printed at the Sentinel office, already has 'sugar-plums danced in', 'Mamma', 'sprang', 'pedlar', 'jerk', 'bowlfull', the quoted last line and 'now, Prancer! now, Vixen!', while keeping 'thro'', 'Dunder and Blixem', 'dry leaves before', 'was flung'; it is the only witness with 'held just in his teeth'.
* Moore's late holograph (Appendix C) follows the 1844 text in every verbal reading of ll. 1-24 except that it writes 'sprung' (the 1823 form) at l. 10 and doubles 'what' ('what what was the matter').

Holograph transcription (ll. 1-24, from the Commons image https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:A_Visit_From_St._Nicholas,_by_Clement_C_Moore.jpg; the reindeer names are underlined in the manuscript; l. 1 is written over two lines):

```
'Twas the night before Christmas, when all through the house
Not a creature was stirring, not even a mouse;
The stockings were hung by the chimney with care,
In hopes that St. Nicholas soon would be there;
The children were nestled all snug in their beds;
While visions of sugar-plums danced in their heads;
And Mamma in her 'kerchief, and I in my cap,
Had just settled our brains for a long winter's nap;
When out on the lawn there arose such a clatter,
I sprung from the bed to see what what was the matter.
Away to the window I flew like a flash,
Tore open the shutters and threw up the sash.
The moon, on the breast of the new-fallen snow,
Gave the lustre of mid-day to objects below,
When, what to my wondering eyes should appear,
But a miniature sleigh, and eight tiny rein-deer,
With a little old driver, so lively and quick,
I knew in a moment it must be St. Nick.
More rapid than eagles his coursers they came,
And he whistled, and shouted, and called them by name;
"Now, Dasher! now, Dancer! now, Prancer and Vixen!
On, Comet! on, Cupid! on, Donder and Blitzen!
To the top of the porch! to the top of the wall!
Now dash away! dash away! dash away all!"
```
