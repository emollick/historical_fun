# Nashville Democratic Clarion, 20 October 1809: the first newspaper notice of Lewis's death — FRAGMENTS ONLY (SUMMARY + phrases quoted by secondary authors)

CITATION (primary): The Democratic Clarion (Nashville, Tenn.), Friday 20 October 1809 (microfilm: Tennessee State Library and Archives, Nashville, roll 113; Nashville Public Library, Nashville Room). Not digitized in Chronicling America (Library of Congress) as of the fetch date; no open scan found (archive.org has no title match).

TRANSCRIPTION: FRAGMENT. The Clarion's text was NOT obtained. What the secondary authors report: (A) the Clarion's story followed the Neelly account but added that Lewis "also suffered knife or razor wounds to the throat and 'hams'"; the same issue carried an advertisement placed by Neelly (dated 3 Oct. 1809, about a horse thief, Van Meter); (C) quotes the Clarion's eulogy: "he [Lewis] was a pupil of the immortal Jefferson - by him he was reared ... was instructed in the tour of the sciences ... was introduced to public life ... [was] to command". Chuinard (WPO 17:4, Nov. 1991, p. 5) and Cutright (WPO 12:1, 1986) summarize later garbled reprints ("cut his arm and neck and ham with a razor").
NOTE: The National Intelligencer's 27 Nov. 1809 notice (saved separately) prints an "Extract of a letter from a gentleman, dated Nashville, Tennessee, 18th October, 1809" whose wording matches Brahan's 18 Oct. letters; that Washington text is probably NOT the Clarion's.

---- UPDATE ----
The complete text of the Clarion's 20 Oct. 1809 notice (obituary + eulogy + Gray's Elegy lines) was afterwards obtained as reprinted, datelined "NASHVILLE, Oct. 20.", in the Kentucky Gazette (Lexington), 31 Oct. 1809, p. 3, col. 1 (Chronicling America sn82014783), and is transcribed from the page images in sources/1809-10-31_kentucky_gazette_reprint_of_clarion_20oct1809.md. That file supersedes this one for the wording of the death account. Note the actual wording: "cut his neck arm and ham with a razor" (not "throat"). Neelly's Van Meter advertisement (Gale) and the Nashville microfilm citations remain only here.
