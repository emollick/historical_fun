# E. Building materials in Sennacherib's inscriptions (RINAP 3): agurru, kupru, ittû, pīlu, gaṣṣu

## 1. Method and sources
- Corpus: ORACC RINAP 3 (Grayson & Novotny 2012/2014), 261 texts (Q003475–Q004072 plus 1xxx), taken from the ORACC open-data JSON dump `rinap-rinap3.zip` (Wayback snapshot 2022-09-21; corpus generated 2022-07-20), which contains every lemmatised word with its citation form. The archived RINAP 3 Akkadian glossary (`cbd/akk/onebigfile.html`, same snapshot) gives the same counts. Translations are RINAP's, taken from the archived ORACC text pages (`web.archive.org/web/2024id_/https://oracc.museum.upenn.edu/rinap/rinap3/Qxxxxxx/`, 2022–2025 snapshots) for every text with an attestation of agurru, kupru or pīlu (55 pages fetched; list at the end). Luckenbill 1924 (OIP 2, ISAC PDF) was used as a control. Text numbers are RINAP 3 numbers ("no. 17" = Q003491, the King Prism; nos. 22/23 = Chicago/Taylor prisms; nos. 39–50 bull colossi; 195–202 Assur bricks; 213 Tarbiṣu; 223 Bavian; 226–227 Jerwan). Line labels are ORACC's. Full tables of all 150 attestations (25 + 13 + 112) are in §7.
- Glossary counts (RINAP 3, whole corpus): **agurru [baked brick] 25** (written a-gur-ri, a-gur₂-ri, SIG₄.AL.UR₃.RA); **kupru [bitumen] 13** (always ESIR.UD.A); **pīlu [limestone] 112** (pi-i-li, {na₄}pi-i-li, pe-e-li, {na₄}pi-li…); **ittû/iṭṭû "bitumen": 0 (word absent from RINAP 3)**; **gaṣṣu "gypsum": 0 (absent)**. Related: libittu [mudbrick] 20 (SIG₄), tibku [course of bricks] 33, tamlīu [fill/terrace] 38, nalbanu/nalbattu [brick mould] 3, lābinu 3 (only in *lābin appi*), utūnu [kiln] 6 (all at Assur), kāru [quay] 12 (the "quay dock" and the Quay Gate — never a quay *built*), kuppu [cistern/spring] 11, būrtu [well] 8, mušēṣû 5 (only in the gate name "Mušlālu"), pitqu 43 (only of *cast* metal, "pitiq …"), abāru 1 (not "lead"; metal lead never occurs). A possible second bitumen word, *qīru*, occurs once as KI-i-ri (see §2.3).

## 2. agurru "baked brick" — the 25 attestations grouped

### 2.1 Glazed bricks decorating the South-West Palace (14 attestations, one formula)
Formula: *… ina agurri uqnî [ZU₂ = ṣurru "obsidian"] (u) uqnî ussīma sellum nēbeḫi u gimir pasqīšin* — no. 17 (King Prism, Q003491) vi 42: `i-na SIG₄.AL.UR₃.RA {na₄}ZU₂ {na₄}ZA.GIN₃` … RINAP (unit vi 37): "I decorated them with silver and copper knobbed nails. **I adorned the arches, friezes, and all of the copings with baked bricks (glazed in the color of) obsidian (and) lapis lazuli.**" Same in no. 15 vi 54 (`qe₂-[reb-ši-in u₂-šal-me i-na a-gur₂-ri]`), no. 16 vi 66 (`qe₂-reb-ši-in u₂-šal-me i-na a-gur₂-ri`), no. 39 line 37 (`… u₂-šal-me i-na a-gur-ri {na₄}ZU₂ {na₄}ZA.GIN₃ us-si-ma`), no. 41 4', no. 43 29, no. 44 47, no. 46 130 (`… [SIG₄].AL.UR₃.RA {na₄}ZU₂ {na₄}ZA.GIN₃ us-si-ma se-el-lum ne₂-be₂-hi u₃ gi-mir pa-as-qi₂-šu`), no. 49 28. Luckenbill OIP 2 p. 107 vi 42–44: "With burnt-brick, KA-stone, and lapis-lazuli, I adorned the walls, cornices and all of their copings." CAD A/1 162 (agurru e, "glazed") translates the same passage "I decorated their (the barakku rooms') corbels, friezes and all their …-s with red(?) and blue glazed bricks". → Decorative glazed brick on the upper parts of walls; nothing structural. (This is the textual counterpart of Viggiano's decorative use.)

### 2.2 The "bridge" opposite the Citadel Gate — *agurru* here means limestone slabs (7 attestations)
No. 4 (Rassam Cylinder, 702 BC) line 90: `i-na me₂-eh-ret KA₂.GAL MURUB₄ URU i-na a-gur₂-ri {na₄}pi-i-li pe-ṣe-e a-na me-ti-iq be-lu-ti-ia u₂-šak-bi-is ti-tur-ru` — RINAP: "I had a bridge constructed opposite the Citadel Gate (by packing down) paving stones of white limestone for my lordly procession(s)." Parallels no. 7 4', no. 8 16', no. 16 viii 1 (`i-na a-gur₂-ri {na₄}pi-i-li pe-ṣe-e`, unit vii 85: "I had a bridge constructed opposite the Citadel Gate with paving stones of white limestone for the passage of my lordly chariot"), no. 136 r i' 9'. Luckenbill read "a bridge (built) of burnt brick and white limestone" (OIP 2 p. 102 l. 90; p. 154 ll. 9–10), but CAD A/1 163 s.v. agurru mng. 2 "paving stone, tile (of stone), slab" cites exactly this line ("I had a ramp of slabs of white limestone built at the access to the inside gate of the city for my solemn processions, OIP 2 102:90") alongside Tiglath-pileser I's "ina agurri ša pīli peṣê … almi" and "ina agurri ša atbari … I laid slabs of basalt all around". The same verb *ušakbis* is used of the Jerwan aqueduct "(by packing down) white limestone" (no. 226 line 8). → Not baked brick.

### 2.3 Water channels of the Ḫusur under the city wall (2 attestations) — the one hydraulic use, with a possible *qīru* "bitumen"
No. 16 (Cylinder D, 696 BC) vii 81–84: `aš₂-šu₂ mu-ṣe-e {id₂}hu-su-ur qe₂-reb URU ma-lak A.MEŠ e-liš u₃ šap-liš / i-na SIG₄.AL.UR₃.RA KI-i-ri ma-ši-i / ti-tur-ri ab-ta-ni ša₂-pal-šu₂` (no. 8 15' restored from it). RINAP: "So that there would be outflow from the Ḫusur River in the city (and) the passage of water upstream and downstream, I built aqueducts beneath it (the city wall) with baked bricks, …" RINAP on-page note (no. 8 n. 9): "KI-i-ri '…': Read as either qí-i-ri or ki-i-ri, 'bitumen' and 'kiln (for lime and bitumen)' respectively. ma-ši-i '…': The meaning of this word is unknown … ti-tur-ri 'aqueducts': Following Frahm (Sanherib pp. 64, 83, and 86) and Bagg (Assyrische Wasserbauten pp. 196–198), titurru probably refers to aqueducts, and not to a bridge, in this passage." → Culverts/aqueduct-channels of baked brick, possibly with bitumen (*qīru*), where the Ḫusur passes the wall. This is the only baked-brick water-work Sennacherib claims at Nineveh.

### 2.4 Broken (1): no. 18 (BM 127845+) vii 14 `še-hi-ni-tu-šu₂ ša a-gur₂-ri [...]` — RINAP: "(As for) its …, which [...] baked bricks, I had (it) expertly made through the craft of [well-trained] master builder[s]" (in a building passage after the palace description; referent lost).

### 2.5 Outside Nineveh (8 attestations): Assur and Kilizu, "baked bricks from a pure kiln"
- No. 196 (Assur, Bīt-tikāti brick) 3: `ina a-gur₂-ri UDUN KU₃-ti eš-šiš u-še-piš-ma u-zaq-qir₆ hur-ša₂-niš` — "With baked bricks from a (ritually) pure kiln, I had the tikātu-house of the courtyard (where) the pedestals of the Igīgū gods (stand) in rows made anew and I raised (it) as high as a mountain." (= no. 197.)
- Nos. 199, 200, 201, 202 (Assur bricks): "With baked bricks from a (ritually) pure kiln, I had the Step Gate of the Palace in Baltil (Aššur) built anew [and raised it as high as a mountain]."
- No. 195 (Assur, Samītu brick) 5: `ina a-gur-ri NA₄.MEŠ ma-aʾ-diš u₂-si-im` — "greatly embellished the frieze of the battlemented parapet of Ešarra with baked bricks (and) stone" (CAD: "with glazed bricks").
- No. 219 (Kilizu brick) 3: "Sennacherib … had the outer wall of the city Kilīzu built with baked bricks (`i-na a-gur₂-ri u₂-še-piš`)."
Note: the Assur akītu-house, the Ešarra courtyard, the Step Gate and the princes' houses have *limestone* foundations (nos. 168–184, 205, §4.3); all-baked-brick construction there is confined to the bīt tikāti and the Step Gate.

## 3. kupru "bitumen" — all 13 attestations are one passage: the bedding under the palace-terrace extension
There is exactly one context. Two recensions:
- Early (nos. 2, 3 [Bellino, 702], 4 [Rassam, 702], 15 [Cyl. C, 697], 16 [Cyl. D, 696]): no. 3 line 49: `qe₂-reb ka-tim-ti a-sur-rak-ki-ša₂ šap-la-nu GI.MEŠ e-la-niš NA₄.MEŠ KUR-i dan-ni it-ti ESIR.UD.A ak-si-ma A.ŠA₃ ul-tu ma-a-me u₂-še-lam-ma na-ba-liš u₂-ter` — "In the hidden depths of its subterranean waters I bonded together, with bitumen, reeds below (and) strong mountain stone above, then I raised (that) area out of the water and converted (it) to dry land. I filled in and measured a terrace of 700 large aslu-cubits along (its) longer side …" (No. 1, the First Campaign Cylinder of 702, has the same act *without* bitumen: "I bonded together strong mountain stone sixty (nindanu) along (its) longer side (and) thirty-four (nindanu) along (its) shorter side, then I raised (that) area out of the water".)
- Later (no. 17 [King Prism, 694/693] v 88–vi 3; nos. 46, 49, 50): `i-na 1/2 IKU ma-lak A.MEŠ 4 {na₄}pi-i-li GAL.MEŠ / it-ti ESIR.UD.A ak-si-ma GI.MEŠ a-pe / u₃ ku-pe-e u₂-šat-ri-ṣa UGU-šu₂-un` — "In a one-half ikû (stretch) of the water's (natural) course, I bonded together, with bitumen, four large limestone (blocks) and spread marsh reeds and rushes over them. As an addition, I took a plot of land that was 340 cubits long (vi 1) (and) 289 cubits wide from the Ḫusur River and from the plain of the city (and) added (it) to the dimensions of the former terrace. In its (the terrace's) entirety, I raised its superstructure 190 courses of brick high." Luckenbill p. 105: "(I placed) four great blocks of limestone and covered (them) with bitumen; reeds of the cane-brake and rushes I spread over them."
- Bull-colossi version (nos. 39, 42, 43, 44): no. 43 line 15–16: `… šap-la-nu GI.MEŠ e-la-niš ESIR.UD.A it-ti {na₄}pi-i-li GAL.MEŠ dun-nu-niš ak-si` — "In its subterranean waters I very firmly bonded reeds below (and) bitumen above with large (blocks of) limestone."
Bitumen is therefore used **only** as the bonding/bedding of limestone blocks (and reeds) in the water-logged former bed of the Tebiltu, before the terrace extension was heaped up. It is never combined with *agurru*, never used for walls, quays, canals, the terrace body, the garden, or the wells. Note the sequence reeds–bitumen–stone (or stone-in-bitumen with reeds and rushes on top).

## 4. pīlu "limestone" — what it is used for (112 attestations)
4.1 **Palace terrace base**: "In order to prevent the foundation of the terrace from being weakened over the passage of time by cresting flood(s), I surrounded its base with large limestone (slabs and thereby) reinforced its base" (no. 17 vi 7–10 `tem-me-en tam-li-i la e-ne₂-ši … {na₄}pi-i-li GAL.MEŠ ki-su-u₂-šu [u₂-ša₂-as-hi-ra u₂-dan-ni-na šu-pu-uk-šu₂]`; nos. 1, 2, 3, 4 (`as-kup-pat {na₄}pi-i-li rab-ba-a-ti a-sur-ru-šu₂ u₂-ša₂-as-hi-ra u₂-dan-ni-na šu-pu-uk-šu₂` "I surrounded its damp course with large limestone slabs (and thereby) reinforced its base"), 15, 16, 18 ("In order to [prevent] its foundations fr[om being weakened by rain] and snow, [I had] its base [built] with [large] limest[one]"), 39, 42, 43, 44, 49, 138).
4.2 **Palace rooms**: "I surrounded their (the palace rooms') lower courses with slabs of breccia (and) alabaster, and large limestone slabs (and) made (them) an object of wonder" (no. 17 vii 41–43; nos. 1, 2, 15, 16, 39–46); bull colossi and sphinxes of white limestone from Balāṭāya and Tastiate (passim); "a palatial wing of limestone and cedar in the style of the land Ḫatti" (no. 22 vi 53; no. 34 64, 76, 82); horse troughs (no. 132); sphinxes for Tašmētu-šarrat's wing (no. 40).
4.3 **City walls**: inner wall: `ṣe-er {na₄}pi-i-li tem-me-en-šu₂ ad-di-ma 40 SIG₄ u₂-kab-bir` — "I laid the foundation of its great wall, Badnigalbilukurašušu, … upon limestone and made (it) 40 bricks thick. I raised its superstructure 180 courses of brick high" (no. 17 vii 65–68; nos. 8, 15, 16; no. 18 vii 1'–8': "made (it) 40 bricks thick, measured by [my] large brick mold … I enlarged its battlements to (a width of) 39 bricks, [then] I raised its superstructure up high, to its copings, to (a height of) 200 courses of brick, (each of) whose thickness was one-third of a cubit"). Outer wall: "I opened up a foundation pit for the outer wall, Badnigerimḫuluḫa, …, then I dug down forty-five nindanu and made (it) reach the water table. I bound together strong mountain stone in the water below and above I expertly carried out its construction with large limestone (blocks) up to its copings" (no. 17 viii 6–11 `… e-la-niš a-di pa-as₂-qi₂-šu₂ i-na {na₄}pi-i-li GAL.MEŠ u₂-nak-kil ši-pir-šu₂`; nos. 8, 15, 16, 18) — an all-stone outer wall, no bitumen, no baked brick.
4.4 **Aqueduct at Jerwan** (nos. 226, 227): `… UGU na-ah-li hu-du-du-ti ša {na₄}pi-i-li pe-ṣi-i u₂-šak-bi-is ti-tur-ru A.MEŠ šá-tu-nu u₂-še-ti-iq ṣe-ru-uš-šu₂` — "I had an aqueduct constructed (by packing down) white limestone over deep wadis (and thereby) enabled those waters to flow over it." Stone only.
4.5 **Assur**: akītu-house "With limestone, stone from the mountains, I laid its foundation … I completed it from its foundations to its crenellations with stone from the mountains and raised it as high as a mountain. I had two canals dug around each of its sides, and (then) I had it surrounded with a lush garden, an orchard with fruit, and placed a splendid plantation around it" (no. 168 30b–35; cf. 171–175: "from buildings for the living to tombs befitting the dead (made) from limestone, stone from the mountains, with which none of the kings of the past … had used"); Ešarra courtyard (169, 172), Step Gate (178), houses of the princes (181–184, 205) — foundations "with limestone, stone from the mountains".

## 5. The terrace, the palace platform, the city wall and the garden: what Sennacherib says they were made of
- **Palace terrace (tamlû)**: filled/heaped in **courses of brick** (tibku): 160 courses (no. 1 line 78 `2 UŠ 40 ti-ib-ki … tam-la-a u₂-mal-li-ma`; "Upon them, I filled in a terrace to a height of 160 courses of brick"), then "I added 20 courses of brick to the former (terrace) and (thus) I raised (it) to a (total) height of 180 courses of brick" (nos. 2–4, 138), 190 courses (nos. 15–17, 39, 42–46, 49), 200 courses "measured by my large brick mold" for the Nebi Yunus ekal māšarti (no. 34 line 62 `tam-la-a uš-mal-li 3 UŠ 20 ti-ib-ki i-na na-al-ban-ia`; nos. 22, 23). The bricks are the ones the deportees made: "I made them carry baskets (of earth) and they made bricks (`il-bi-nu SIG₄`)" (no. 17 v 54–56; nos. 1–4, 16, 42–44, 50) — SIG₄ = *libittu*, mudbrick. Foundation deposits "160 courses of brick within the terrace … deep down in its foundation" (nos. 2–4, 15, 138). Base ringed with large limestone slabs (§4.1); bedding of the riverbed extension: limestone blocks bonded with bitumen, reeds and rushes (§3). **No agurru, no kupru, no gaṣṣu in the terrace itself.**
- **City wall**: foundation on limestone, 40 (mud)bricks thick, 180/200 brick courses high (§4.3); outer wall of stone founded at the water table (§4.3); Ḫusur culverts of baked brick (+ *qīru*?) beneath it (§2.3); Bavian: "I had its (inner) wall and its outer wall, which had never been constructed before, built anew and I raised (them) as high as mountain(s)" (no. 223 line 5b).
- **Garden**: "I planted alongside it (the palace) a botanical garden (kirimāḫu), a replica of Mount Amanus, which has all kinds of aromatic plants (and) fruit trees, trees that are the mainstay of the mountains and Chaldea, [together with cotton trees] collected inside it" (no. 17 vii 53–57; nos. 1–4, 15, 16, 42, 43, 46, 138); "Beside the city, in a botanical garden (one) pānu (in size and) a garden (one) pānu (in size) for a game preserve …" (nos. 15, 16 viii). Water: "In order to be able to draw water by bucket every day, I had bronze wire chains and bronze cables made and, instead of poles, I had tree trunks and date palm(s) (of copper) placed over wells" (no. 17 vii 45–49; nos. 40, 43, 46; the Dalley "screw" passage, see RINAP n. 49). **No building material of any kind is given for the garden**; no terrace, vault, pier, brick or bitumen is associated with it.
- **Quays/canals**: *kāru* occurs only as "the quay dock" where the boats sank under the colossi and as the "Quay Gate"; canals are "dug" (ḫerû) and cut with iron picks through rock (Bavian, no. 223); the Jerwan crossing is limestone (§4.4). No baked-brick or bitumen lining is ever claimed.

## 6. Answer to the questions
1. *Does Sennacherib ever describe his palace terrace, the palace foundations, or the garden with baked brick or bitumen?* Bitumen: only for the foundation bedding in the old Tebiltu channel (limestone blocks bonded with bitumen, with reeds and rushes) — 13 attestations of one passage. Baked brick: never for the terrace or its foundations, never for the garden; at Nineveh only as glazed decoration of arches/friezes/copings and for the Ḫusur culverts under the wall (the "bridge" of *agurri pīli peṣê* being limestone slabs per CAD and RINAP).
2. *What were the terrace, platform and city wall made of?* Terrace and platform: brick courses (mudbrick made by deportees) heaped to 160→180→190 (SW Palace) and 200 (Nebi Yunus) courses, on a bedding of limestone + bitumen + reeds where it overlay the river bed, with a ring of large limestone slabs around the base. Inner wall: limestone foundation, 40 bricks thick, 180–200 courses high; outer wall: stone, founded at the water table.
3. *What did he build with baked brick and bitumen?* Nothing "in baked brick and bitumen" together. Baked brick: glazed wall decoration; Ḫusur culverts (with *qīru* "bitumen"? — reading uncertain, RINAP leaves it untranslated); at Assur the bīt tikāti and the Step Gate ("from a pure kiln"); Kilizu's outer wall. Bitumen: the terrace bedding only. Gypsum (gaṣṣu) and ittû never occur.

## 7. Bearing on Strabo 16.1.5 / Diodorus 2.10.5
- Strabo's "vaults on hollow cube-shaped piers of baked brick and asphalt" and Diodorus' "double layer of baked bricks bonded in gypsum, over a layer of reeds in bitumen, with lead sheets" describe the *ina kupri u agurri* construction idiom that Nebuchadnezzar (and Nabopolassar, Neriglissar, and Esarhaddon at Babylon) use in their own words for quays, walls and terraces (CAD A/1 161: "I built two reinforced quay walls of baked bricks laid in bitumen … between them I piled up a mass of baked bricks mountain high", Nbk.; "I raised the foundation of the terrace from ground-water level with kiln-fired bricks laid in bitumen", Esarh. Babylon). Sennacherib never uses that idiom for anything, and for his own terrace and garden he uses a different one (brick courses; limestone slabs; limestone-in-bitumen bedding). So the classical formula does discriminate at the level of *what the two kings say*: it matches Nebuchadnezzar's building vocabulary and does not match Sennacherib's.
- Two cautions. (i) Silence: Sennacherib does not describe the garden's substructure at all, so his texts cannot exclude a brick-and-bitumen vaulted platform beside the palace; they only show he did not advertise one, whereas he did advertise the bitumen bedding, the limestone facing and the brick courses of the terrace. (ii) The idiom is not unknown in Assyria: Adad-nārārī I and Aššur-bēl-kala repaired the Tigris quay wall at Assur "with baked bricks set in bitumen" (CAD A/1 161, AOB 1 72:27ff.; AKA 148 v 27), and Sennacherib's Ḫusur culverts may combine baked brick and *qīru*. But archaeologically (Viggiano 2025, file E_viggiano.md) Neo-Assyrian baked brick at Nineveh is facing, paving (with bitumen coating), drains and decoration, never mass masonry; and the one detail of Diodorus that resonates with Sennacherib — reeds laid in bitumen at the base — is in Sennacherib a bedding in a river bed under mudbrick, not a layer over vaulted galleries under a double course of baked brick in gypsum.
- Net: on the building-material description the classical tradition points to a Babylonian-style structure (as Koldewey's "Vaulted Building" and Nebuchadnezzar's inscriptions embody it) rather than to anything Sennacherib describes at Nineveh; the argument is one of idiom and probability, not proof, because the garden substructure at Nineveh is undescribed and unexcavated.
