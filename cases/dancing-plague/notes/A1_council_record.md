## 0. One-paragraph result

The contemporary Strasbourg record of 1518 that survives today consists of (a) three original documents in Strasbourg and Bas-Rhin archives — the deliberations of a three-man council commission of **Friday 23 July 1518** with an *Instruction* for the persons to be sent to St Vitus (AVES III 200/15, formerly "G U P 200"; printed in full by Martin 1914 as Anlage I–II), a mandate signed by Sebastian Brant of **2 August 1518** (AVES 1 MR 3 f. 72r; not transcribed in anything I could retrieve), a **letter of the magistrate to Bishop Wilhelm of 25 July 1518** (ADBR 12 J 1495; only a 2018 French translation retrieved) and undated notices/mandates to the brotherhoods (III 200/15; printed by Martin) — and (b) extracts made in the 17th–18th c. by Jacob II Wencker from the lost XXI protocols/Memoriale of 1518 (the so-called *Annales de Brant*, burned 1870), transmitted through 19th-c. copies and printed by Dacheux (BSCMHA XV 1892 no. 3443; XIX 1899 no. 4398). Independent contemporary witnesses are the Augsburg chronicler Wilhelm Rem (a notice of 1518) and, by report, the Imlin family chronicle and Gebwiler. The contemporary layer gives: onset "um Margarethen" / "8 days before Magdalene" (c. 13–15 July) with one woman; "uff fünfzig personen" dancing day and night; a physicians' verdict of a *natural* illness ("hitzig geblüt" in Jung's résumé; "astral conjunction and heat" in the 25 July letter); the transport of the sick in three groups on hospital and cathedral-works wagons to St Vitus at the Hohlenstein above Saverne with sung masses, small offerings and red shoes (the shoes only in Specklin/Wencker); a ban on dancing and drumming until Michaelmas (29 Sept) with an exception for weddings and first masses with string music; orders that families and brotherhoods keep and calm their sick; and on **Tuesday 31 August 1518** a hundredweight of wax to be sent as a candle to St Vitus with a sung mass and three side-masses. **No contemporary document mentions a single death.** Deaths first appear in Daniel Specklin's *Collectanea* (c. 1580: "Viel tantzten sich zu tode"), in Kleinlawel's rhymed chronicle (1625: "Viel sind Todt drüber blieben") and in Wencker's chronicle (1637: "Etliche stürben dran"). "Hundreds" of dancers appear only in the undated verses printed by Schilter in 1698 ("Viel hundert") and in the 17th-c. chroniclers (Imlin "mehr denn 400"; Schadaeus/Duntzenheim "mehr als 200"; Specklin "über 100").

## 2. The sources one by one — author, date of composition, manuscript/edition history

### 2.1 The surviving originals (Strasbourg and Bas-Rhin archives)

- **AVES (Archives de la Ville et de l'Eurométropole de Strasbourg) Série III 200/15** — formerly cited as "Archiv der Stadt Strassburg G U P 200" (Martin 1914 pp. 115, 121–124). Contents according to Martin and the 2018 lenders' list: (i) the council commission's deliberations "Vff Fritag post Marie Magdalene" (= 23 July 1518; Martin's *Anlage I*, undated in the year but assigned to 1518 by the archive from the names of the magistrates); (ii) the "Instruction der armen dantzen[den] Personen so zu Sant Vit geschickt. Veneris post Magdalene etc. XVIII" (Martin's *Anlage II*; the year is written only "etc. XVIII", which is how the false "1418" of Schilter → Hecker → Häser arose); (iii) six identical undated notices ("Als sich yetzt die kranckheit des dantzens wider erhept …"), apparently for distribution to the guilds (presumably the "note du magistrat aux confréries" of the 2018 lenders' list); (iv) an undated mandate ("Als dann die kranckheit mit dem dantzen sich leider nit enden will …") with a correction in the original. Martin: "Das Original (Anlage II) liegt noch im Archiv der Stadt Strassburg (GUP 200) und hat auch nur etc. XVIII. Nach der Charakteristik der Schriftzüge ist 1518 anzunehmen" [IMG p. 115].
- **AVES 1 MR 3, f. 72 r** — "Extrait du registre des Mandats et Règlements de la Ville de Strasbourg. Interdiction des danses publiques par le magistrat de Strasbourg, signée Sébastien Brandt (Strasbourg, 2 août 1518)" (2018 press kit, illustration caption; lenders' list: "Source manuscrite du 2 août 1518. Feuillet signé Sébastien Brant, 1 MR3, f° 72 r"). Text not retrieved. Its content is presumably what the Extractus summarises as the ban on dancing until Michaelmas (§3.1).
- **ADBR (Archives départementales du Bas-Rhin) 12 J 1495** — "Source manuscrite du 25 juillet 1518 : lettre du magistrat à l'évêque Wilhelm" (2018 lenders' list); French translation of two passages in the teachers' dossier p. 11 (§3.5). German text not retrieved.
- **AMS X 140** — dance prohibition of 15 June 1547 (2018 lenders' list); later, not used.

### 2.2 The lost XXI protocols and Wencker's *Extractus* ("Annales de Brant")

The protocols of the Council of XXI for 1518 no longer exist: "Comme les Protocoles des XXI n'existent aux Archives de la Ville qu'à partir de 1539 …" (Dacheux, BSCMHA XIX p. 35 [OCR]). What survives are extracts made by **Jacob II Wencker** (city archivist, d. 1743; dates [UNVERIFIED (from memory)]) from the protocols and from the *Memorialia* (notebooks) of the city secretaries, among them the "Memoriale Petri Buotz de a° 1518". Dacheux (XV p. XCVII [OCR, Google scan]): "Aux archives de la ville on conservait également autrefois en deux exemplaires un manuscrit désigné généralement sous le nom d'Annales de Brant, qui malheureusement fut transporté … à la bibliothèque publique; … tous ont péri dans l'incendie de 1870. Ces prétendues Annales de Brant étaient en réalité l'œuvre de Jacques II Wencker et portaient le titre suivant: *Extractus ex prothocollis D. D. XXI, necnon quibusdam ex annalibus Seb. Brandti, auctore Jacobo Wenckero*. L'un des deux exemplaires, peut-être l'original, formait un volume. Le second exemplaire, en deux volumes, était une copie, car il portait la note suivante: *Descripsit Maria Barbara Meyer, Lahrensis ancilla, 1763*." Dacheux XIX p. 36 [OCR]: "Bien que le titre porte le nom de Brant, il est certain que les Annales sont l'œuvre de Jacques Wencker … Brant y est complètement étranger, sauf que pour les années antérieures on a tiré partie de ses carnets, Memorialia, Gedenkbüchlein, comme on l'a fait pour ses successeurs." Authority of the text: "exactement celle des Registres conservés aux Archives et qui renferment les procès-verbaux des séances des Conseils" (ibid.).

Two printed witnesses of the 1518 entries:
- **Dacheux 1892 (BSCMHA XV)**: 19th-c. extracts from the papers of L. Schnéegans, Cunitz and Rœhrich (each entry marked *Pp. Schn.*, *Pp. C.*, *Pp. Rh.*), sometimes giving two versions of the same folio ("Autre texte"). Folio numbers of the lost volume are printed in the margin (fol. 158–174 = 1517–1519).
- **Dacheux 1899 (BSCMHA XIX)**: the full copy of the religious-affairs entries 1517–1536 made by the librarian **Andreas Jung** "pour son propre usage et tandis que le manuscrit original restait encore déposé à la Bibliothèque"; "Généralement M. Jung a transcrit textuellement; rarement il semble avoir résumé" (p. 36 [OCR]). Jung's marginal notes are printed in small type; his résumés are enclosed between ‖ ‖ signs. **The 1518 dance entry (no. 4398) is one of the cases where Jung only résuméd** — see §3.2.

### 2.3 Daniel Specklin's *Collectanea* (→ Reuss 1888–89)

Daniel Specklin (Strasbourg architect, 1536–1589; Martin p. 119: "der 1536 in Strassburg geboren wurde, also seine Nachrichten noch von älteren Leuten haben kann, die den Tanz erlebten"). His preparatory *Collectanea* (2 vols., c. 402 + 492 fols. [from my earlier reading of Reuss's introduction; the exact figures are OCR-only]) passed at his death to his brother-in-law, the printer Lazarus Zetzner, were bought in 1615 by the pastor Oseas Schad for 30 florins ("Empta a … Lazaro Zetznero, quindecim viro Reipublicae Argentinensis, 1615, triginta florenis" — Reuss, XIII p. 173 [OCR]), passed to the city archives and then to the city library, and burned in 1870. Reuss reconstructed them from: the papers of Schnéegans, Silbermann, Jung, Rœhrich, Piton, an 18th-c. anonymous excerptor ("Exc. Sp."), Wencker's *Collectanea* in the archives, and "Gloses ajoutées à un Kœnigshoven, éd. Schilter (Bibliothèque municipale) = K.-S. G." (XIII pp. 176–177 [OCR]). **The dance entry no. 2216 rests on "K.-S. G., p. 1087" and "Pp. Rh."** — i.e. on a gloss written into a printed Schilter 1698 at p. 1087 (the very pages where Schilter printed his own note "Vom Veitz-Tantz", pp. 1085–1090 per Martin p. 114 n. 1) plus Rœhrich's papers. Date of composition: Specklin was writing in the 1570s–80s (the dossier says "vers 1580").

### 2.4 Jacob Wencker's chronicle of 1637 (→ Dacheux 1892)

Manuscript chronicle of the Wencker family, lost 1870; Dacheux printed fragments from Schnéegans's extracts (BSCMHA XV). The dance entry no. 3007 is marked "Sp. n° 2216. — Pp. Schn.", i.e. Dacheux/Schnéegans regarded it as derived from Specklin; it adds details (guild halls named, "hellensteg", people falling before the image, the note that everything ceased once refuted "aus Gottes wort") — Martin p. 119: "Wencker hat diesem Bericht 1637 hinzugefügt …".

### 2.5 Duntzenheim's chronicle (→ Jung 1830 → Dacheux 1897)

Known only as a German résumé by Andreas Jung (*Beiträge*, 2. Theil, p. 59), reprinted as no. 3981 in BSCMHA XVIII (1897) p. 12 [IMG]. Author/date of the Duntzenheim chronicle: not established here (the family Duntzenheim were Strasbourg patricians; [UNVERIFIED (from memory)] a "Chronik" attributed to Batt von Duntzenheim, 16th c.).

### 2.6 The Imlin family chronicle (→ Witkowski 1879 → Martin 1914)

"Die sogenannte Imlinsche Familienchronik der Strassburger Landesbibliothek bringt als Zeugnis eines Zeitgenossen" (Martin p. 119 [IMG], citing Witkowski, Allg. Zs. f. Psychiatrie 35, 1879). Whether the 1518 entry is really contemporary could not be checked; the wording ("meinw g[nädigen Herrn]") is that of a 16th-c. Strasbourg burgher's notebook.

### 2.7 Seventeenth-century chronicles (Schadaeus, Kleinlawel, Goldmeyer) via Boersch 1836 → Martin

Martin p. 116 [IMG]: the medical historians' account "gründet sich auf Schilter und auf das, was Boersch zusammengetragen hat, der die Angaben aus der (1870 verbrannten) handschriftlichen Chronik von Schadaeus (Oseas Schad, 17. Jahrhundert) und den im 17. Jahrhundert gedruckten von Goldmeyer und Kleinlawel vermehrte." Martin adds: "Die Goldmeyersche Chronik erwähnt nach Angabe der Strassburger Landesuniversität den Veitstanz von 1518 nicht."

### 2.8 Schilter 1698 and the "1418" error

Schilter's note to Königshoven (Strassburg 1698, pp. 1085–1090) quotes six verses "aus Chron. M.S. Argent. p. 318" under the heading "St. Veits Tantz An. 1418" and prints (incompletely, with misreadings — Martin) the *Instruction* and the commission protocol. The manuscript chronicle he used cannot be identified; Martin p. 114: "Wie mir die Strassburger Landesbibliothek mitteilt, ist die angeführte Chronik beim Bibliotheksbrande 1870 zugrunde gegangen. Ich glaube, es geschah schon früher, da keiner der Schriftsteller vor 1870, die nach handschriftlichem Stoff suchten, ausser Schilter ihrer gedenkt" [OCR]. Hecker 1832 took 1418 from Schilter; Boersch 1836 first printed 1518, then recanted to 1418 and inserted "vierzehnhundert" into his French translation of the Instruction; Häser then added "14.." to the German text "etc. XVIII" — "und so entstand in der Ratsverfügung die gefälschte Jahreszahl 1418" (Martin p. 115 [IMG]).

### 2.9 Wilhelm Rem, Augsburg

Wilhelm Rem, Augsburg merchant-chronicler (c. 1462–1529 [UNVERIFIED (from memory)]); his "Cronica newer geschichten" covers 1512–1527 and was written up year by year. The Strasbourg notice stands in the 1518 section between an entry on the Louvain Collegium trilingue and the entry on the Augsburg diet of June 1518 (CDS 25 p. 88 [IMG]). Martin p. 114 calls Rem a "Nürnberger Chronist" — an error; the volume is *Chroniken der schwäbischen Städte: Augsburg* 5.

### 2.10 Martin 1914

Alfred Martin (physician-folklorist) — the first to use the original G U P 200 documents; he prints them (pp. 121–124), corrects the date to 1518, identifies the Saverne site (Hohlenstein/"Vixberg") and reviews the whole German tradition (Zürich 1418/1428/1452, Cologne/Liège 1374, Kölbigk 1021 etc. in the second instalment pp. 225–239 [OCR only]).

## 3. Every 1518 dancing passage, quoted in full, with translation and citation

### 3.1 Wencker's *Extractus* ("Annales de Brant"), no. 3443 — BSCMHA 2e sér. XV (1892) pp. 239–240 [IMG: `../sources/bscmha-xv-1892/images/p239.jpg`, `p240.jpg`; IIIF `pourlaconser2v15soci$363`, `$364`]

Printed text (marginal year 1518; the first paragraph is from Rœhrich's papers, the "Autre texte" paragraphs from Schnéegans's):

> **3443.** (*St. Veits-Tanz.*) — Als diss jars um Margarethen ein schwäre erschreckliche krankheit mit St. Vitstanz erhub, also dass uff fünfzig personen damit behafft tag und nacht tantzeten dass jämmerlich zu sehen war, wurden dieselben alle in der Stattcosten verwahrt und zu dem lieben heil. St. Vit im Hohenstein bei Zabern geführt und fast erledigt. Da setzten unsere herren uff und lissen ein gebot üssgon, dass niemand tantzen soll bis Michaelis in der ganzen Statt und burgbann, bei 30 sch. pf. und kein brucken schlagen; wol möchte man bey brautläuften und ersten messen mit saitenspiel tantzen nach eines jeden conscienz.
> Darnach uff 4a post Laurentii ist erkannt den zünfften zedel zu schicken dass jedermann sin kind und die sinen verwart und die bruderschafften ir brüder in ihren kosten undersuchten oder zu den heyligen schickten. — 5a Mariae Magdalenae. Item als etlich frauen und knaben den bössen dantz tantzen, soll die termen (?) abstellen und heimlich seitenspiel haben.
> Item die verordneten herren bringen an des tantzens halb. Erk. in Unser Frauen capell mittwoch ein herlich amt halten, porro erkant die tantzent im Spittal zu dem heiligen schicken und ein opfer von der ganzen Statt wegen dem heiligen bringen. Die leichtfertigen der Statt ein zitlang verwisen, darauf der hh. bedacht des opfers halb zu St. Viten, namlich ein zentner wachs zu einer kerzen mit dem rentmeister schicken, und ein erlich ampt und 3 nebenmessen lassen lesen von wegen des Raths und gemeiner Statt wegen. — Pp. Rh.
> [margin: Fol. 172, 1518] *Autre texte:* Herren bringen ihren bedacht des opffers halben zu St. Viten, dass man ein wäccen bild eines centners schwer mit dem rentmeister oder kornmeister zu St. Viten schicke für ein opffer, oder aber dasselbig wächserin bild auf einen altar im Münster stelle und da ehre bis die neu capell ausgemacht, dann einen altar in derselben capellen in St. Viten ehren weyhen lassen. Erkt. den centner wachs zu einer kerzen machen und zu dem lieben heyligen mit dem rentmeister schicken, und ein singent erlich ampt und 3 neben messen lassen lesen von wegen des ganzen Raths und gemeiner Statt wegen. Dienstag post Adolphi. — *XXI.* — Pp. Schn.
> [margin: Fol. 36a] *Ad art. S. Veits-Tanz:* von disem tantz findt man in miner *Cronicken* ad longum. — *Reg. de a°. 1518.* *En marge:* «Not. Dr Sebast. Brandt *Chronicon*.»
> [margin: Fol. 172a] *Autre texte:* Vorgeschlagen ein wehssen bild eins zentners swer zu S. Vit schicken, oder aber dasselbig wehssen bild uff ein altar im Münster stellen, und do eren bitz die neu cappel üssgemacht, dann ein altar in derselben capellen in S. Viten ehre wihen lossen. Erkt. zu einer kerzen machen lassen und zu dem lieben heiligen schicken und ein singent erlich ambt und dry neben messen lossen lesen von wegen des ganzen Raths und gemeiner Statt. Dienstag post Adolphi 1518. — *XXI.*
> *S. Veits-Tanz:* fol. 159b, 161b, 171a et b, 172a, 174b.

Translation:

> **3443.** (St Vitus's dance.) — As in this year around St Margaret's day a grave, terrible sickness with St Vitus's dance arose, so that up to fifty persons afflicted with it danced day and night, which was pitiful to see, these were all kept at the city's cost and taken to the dear holy St Vitus in the Hohenstein [recte Hohlenstein] near Zabern [Saverne] and [were] nearly [all] relieved (*fast erledigt*). Then our lords resolved and issued a decree that nobody shall dance until Michaelmas [29 September] in the whole city and its jurisdiction (*burgbann*), on pain of 30 shillings pfennig, and [that nobody shall] beat *brucken* [sic; Martin reads *beucken* = kettledrums]; but at weddings and first masses one might well dance with string music, according to each one's conscience.
> Thereafter, on Wednesday after St Lawrence [11 August 1518] it was resolved to send notes to the guilds that everyone should keep his child and his household under guard, and the brotherhoods look after their brothers at their own cost or send them to the saints. — Thursday [the feast] of Mary Magdalene [22 July 1518]. Item: as some women and boys are dancing the evil dance, [one] shall stop the *termen* (?) and have string music privately (*heimlich*).
> Item: the deputed lords report about the dancing. Resolved: to hold a solemn service in Our Lady's chapel on Wednesday; further resolved to send those dancing in the Hospital to the saint and to bring an offering to the saint on behalf of the whole city. To banish the frivolous (*leichtfertigen*) from the city for a while; thereupon the lords' consideration about the offering to St Vitus, namely to send a hundredweight of wax for a candle with the treasurer (*rentmeister*), and to have a solemn service and three side-masses read on behalf of the council and the whole community. — Pp. Rh.
> [Fol. 172, 1518] Other text: The lords bring their consideration about the offering to St Vitus: that one send a wax image weighing a hundredweight with the treasurer or the grain-master to St Vitus as an offering, or else set the same wax image on an altar in the cathedral and honour it there until the new chapel is finished, then have an altar consecrated in the same chapel in honour of St Vitus. Resolved: to make the hundredweight of wax into a candle and send it to the dear saint with the treasurer, and have a sung solemn service and three side-masses read on behalf of the whole council and the whole community. Tuesday after Adolphi [31 August 1518]. — XXI. — Pp. Schn.
> [Fol. 36a] On the article "St Vitus's dance": of this dance one finds [an account] at length in my chronicle. — Register of the year 1518. In the margin: "Note: Dr Sebastian Brant's Chronicon."
> [Fol. 172a] Other text: Proposed: to send a wax image weighing a hundredweight to St Vitus, or else to set the same wax image on an altar in the cathedral and honour it there until the new chapel is finished, then have an altar consecrated in the same chapel in honour of St Vitus. Resolved: to have it made into a candle and sent to the dear saint, and have a sung solemn service and three side-masses read on behalf of the whole council and the whole community. Tuesday after Adolphi 1518. — XXI.
> St Vitus's dance: fol. 159b, 161b, 171a and b, 172a, 174b [= the six places in Wencker's Extractus that mention the dance].

Notes on the text. (a) "um Margarethen": St Margaret = 13 July in most German calendars (20 July in the Roman); Martin's bracket "[22. Juli]" (p. 116) is a slip (that is Magdalene). (b) "brucken schlagen": Dacheux prints *brucken*; Martin (p. 116) prints "beucken [Pauken]" — the sense "drum-beating" is secured by Rem ("paugkenschlagen"), Specklin ("trummen und pfeiffen"), Imlin ("drummeln vnd pfiffen") and the physicians' advice in the 25 July letter (no "timbales et fifres"). (c) "termen (?)": queried by Dacheux, unexplained by Martin ("Was mit termen gemeint ist, weiss man nicht", p. 121); my conjecture: a copyist's misreading of *trummen* ("stop the drums and have string music in private"), which would make the 22 July decision the first restriction of drums. (d) "5a Mariae Magdalenae" = feria quinta = Thursday, 22 July 1518 — i.e. this entry precedes the commission of Friday 23 July; the extract is not in chronological order. (e) "Dienstag post Adolphi": St Adelphus (Adolphus) of Metz, venerated at Neuwiller, 29 August [UNVERIFIED (from memory) as to the Strasbourg calendar], hence Tuesday 31 August 1518. **Martin's bracket "[29. September]" (p. 122) and his conclusion that the epidemic lasted "mindestens bis nach Adolphi, dem 29. September 1518" is an error** — Adolphi is not Michaelis; the exhibition's chronology (from a 2018 press report read but not saved — treat as unverified) likewise puts the candle decision on 31 August. (f) "in miner Cronicken ad longum": a first-person note in the 1518 register that a full account exists "in my chronicle", glossed as Brant's *Chronicon* — evidence that a detailed contemporary narrative (Brant's lost chronicle, mentioned by Wencker in his *Res Alsaticae*, Dacheux XV p. XCVIII [OCR]) once existed. (g) "die tantzent im Spittal": by the time of this entry there were dancers being kept in the Hospital.

### 3.2 Wencker's *Extractus*, fol. 171, no. 4398 — Jung's copy, BSCMHA 2e sér. XIX (1899) p. 42 [IMG: `../sources/bscmha-xix-1899/images/p042.jpg`; IIIF `pourlaconser2v19soci$68`]

> [margin: Fol. 171] **4398.** *S. Veitstanz.* ‖ In mehreren Sitzungen wurde von Veitstänzern gesprochen: die Aerzte erklärten es für eine natürliche krankheit, die von hitzigem geblüt herkomme. Indessen «die armen leute» wünschten, daß man für sie messen lese u. s. w. Der Rhat schickte deswegen zu dem bischöflichen *Vikarius*, der antwortete:
> *Ein aufgeklärter bischöfl. Vikar.* — Dass dis im unnot zu sin dünkt, sondern diwyl die Artz anzeigen, daß es eine natürlich krankheit sy, daß man auch natürlich mittel mit in versuche, aber domit nit nichts beschehe, woll er alle Predikanten beschickhen und in bevelhen dass sie öffentlich in *cancellis* Gott ermaneten zu betten und anzurieffen, daß er sin gnad und barmherzigkeit uns sende. — Weniger aufgeklärt, oder vielleicht gedrängt von dem abergläubischen volke, verordnet indessen der Rat religiöse übungen; die kranken wurden zu dem h. Vitus geschickt, und da sie gesund zurückkehrten, diesem ein bild von wachs, ein centner schwer, zuerkannt. ‖ *Résumé de M. Jung.* — Cfr. n° 3443.
> [small type:] Wencker, *Chronik*, II, fol. 23, fol. 36. — Cfr. n° 3443. / Freitag p. Medardi, 11. juni. Ein päpstl. Legat hier. — Suppl., fol. 4b. / Mittwoch Vigil. Joh. Bapt., 23. juni. Leutpriester am Alt S. Peter klagt. — Id., fol. 4b vel a.

Translation:

> 4398. St Vitus's dance. ‖ In several sessions the St Vitus dancers were discussed: the physicians declared it a natural illness which comes from hot blood. However, "the poor people" wished that masses be read for them, etc. The council therefore sent to the episcopal vicar, who answered: [Jung's heading: "An enlightened episcopal vicar."] — That this seemed to him unnecessary, but since the physicians indicate that it is a natural illness, that one should also try natural remedies on them; yet so that nothing at all be left undone, he would summon all the preachers and order them to admonish [the people] publicly from the pulpits (*in cancellis*) to pray to God and call upon him to send us his grace and mercy. — Less enlightened, or perhaps pressed by the superstitious populace, the council nevertheless ordered religious exercises; the sick were sent to St Vitus, and when they came back healthy, a wax image weighing a hundredweight was awarded to him. ‖ Résumé by Mr Jung. — Cf. no. 3443.

Note. Everything between ‖ ‖ is **Jung's 19th-century résumé**, not the wording of the protocol; only the vicar's answer ("Dass dis im unnot zu sin dünkt … uns sende") is in the language of the source. Therefore the formula "hitzig(es) geblüt" is attested only at third hand (lost protocol → Wencker's Extractus fol. 171 → Jung's résumé), although the underlying verdict "natürlich krankheit" is confirmed verbatim in the vicar's reply and independently by the 25 July letter (§3.5). Note also that the episcopal vicar's answer contradicts the common modern statement that the Church/council "rejected" the physicians: the vicar accepted the natural diagnosis and merely added public prayers.

### 3.4 The council documents in AVES III 200/15 ("G U P 200") as printed by Martin 1914

#### 3.4.1 The six identical notices to the brotherhoods (undated) — Martin p. 121 [IMG `../sources/martin-zvfv-24-1914/images/p121.jpg`; IIIF `zeitschriftfrv24verbuoft$135`]

> Im Stadtarchiv zu Strassburg finden sich sechs gleichlautende, undatierte Bekanntmachungen, die wohl zum Verteilen an die Zünfte bestimmt waren: „Als sich yetzt die kranckheit des dantzens wider erhept unnd zu besorgen von tag zu tag meren möcht, do habent unser herren rete und XXI. erkandt, das eyner yeden bruderschafft buchssenmeistere, die jhennen so inn irer bruderschafft sindt und mit solcher krankheit beladen werden, versorgen, und versehen, das die nyder getüschet oder zu dem heiligen sant Viten gefürt werden, und inen keynerley seytten oder fröydenspiel gebruchen, cleynötter oder hübsche cleyder uff oder anthün, ouch sie nirgent lossen, uff den gassen louffen, dann welche brüderschafft oder büchssenmeistere solchs verbrechent, die wöllent unser herren darumb straffen, unnd ir ernstliche hut daruff setzen, des wiß sich menglich zu halten.“ (Archiv der Stadt Strassburg G U P 200.)

> Translation: "As the sickness of dancing has now arisen again and it is to be feared that it may increase from day to day, our lords the councils and the XXI have resolved that the box-masters [alms-box keepers] of every brotherhood shall provide for those in their brotherhood who are burdened with such sickness, and see to it that they are quieted down (*nyder getüschet*) or taken to the holy St Vitus, and use no string or merry music for them, put no jewels or fine clothes on them, and also nowhere let them run about in the streets; for whichever brotherhood or box-masters violate this, our lords will punish for it and keep an earnest watch upon it; let everyone know to conduct himself accordingly."

Comment: "wider erhept" (arisen *again*) shows a relapse after the first group had been sent to Saverne and reported "fast erledigt" — i.e. a second wave, presumably early August. Martin (p. 121): "Hier wurde nun die ursprünglich vom Rat angewandte Methode, durch Musik und Tanz zu heilen, gänzlich verboten, dafür sollten die Kranken niedergetuscht oder nach St. Veit geschickt werden."

#### 3.4.2 The later mandate (undated) — Martin p. 122 [IMG `p122.jpg`; IIIF `$136`]

> „Als dann die kranckheit mit dem dantzen sich leider nit enden will, do haben unser herren rät und XXI erkant daz ein jeder burger sin kind, der massen behafft, und gesind die daz vermögen, inn iren husern behalten und zutuschen soll; wo aber ein dienstknecht also behafft wurt, sollen die brüderschaftmeister sins hantwercks, inn der bruderschaft costen, denselben verwaren oder zu dem heiligen Sant Viten (‚dem heiligen Sant Viten‘ durchgestrichen und dafür steht ‚den heiligen, wo inen geliebt‘) schicken, domit sie nit also öfflich uff der gassen, zu beswernis anderer menschen, sich des dantzens annemen, dann welcher den sinen nit also verwaret, den wöllen unser herren darumb heffteklich straffen.“ (Archiv der Stadt Strassburg G U P 200.)

> Translation: "As the sickness with the dancing unfortunately will not end, our lords the councils and the XXI have resolved that every burgher shall keep his child, [if] afflicted in that manner, and [his] servants — those who can afford it — in their houses and quiet them down; but where a servant is so afflicted, the brotherhood-masters of his craft shall, at the brotherhood's cost, keep him or send him to the holy St Vitus ('the holy St Vitus' struck out and replaced by 'the saints, wherever they please'), so that they do not thus publicly in the streets, to the annoyance of other people, take up dancing; for whoever does not thus keep his own, our lords will punish severely for it."

Comment: content and the corrected plural "den heiligen" match the Extractus's decision of Wednesday 11 August 1518 ("den zünfften zedel zu schicken dass jedermann sin kind und die sinen verwart und die bruderschafften ir brüder in ihren kosten undersuchten oder zu den heyligen schickten"). ### 3.5 Letter of the magistrate to Bishop Wilhelm [of Honstein], 25 July 1518 — ADBR 12 J 1495 (German original not retrieved; French translation in the teachers' dossier p. 11, `../sources/misc/dossier_enseignant_1518.txt`)

> « (…) Le Magistrat de Strasbourg à l'évêque Wilhelm : Vous nous avez écrit au sujet de l'inhabituelle maladie qui vient de se déclarer, pour que nous fassions un rapport. Ça a commencé avec une femme, puis avec d'autres personnes qui se sont mises à danser. Nous avons gardé ces personnes quelques jours et interrogé les médecins, qui nous ont dit qu'il s'agissait d'une maladie naturelle due à une conjonction astrale et à la chaleur du moment. Elle touche néanmoins à sa fin. Ceux qui ont été atteints ont été surveillés et on les a fait conduire chez saint Guy [sans doute am Hohlenstein, au-dessus de Saverne]. »
> « Conseils donnés par les médecins au Magistrat — Les médecins nous ont aussi conseillé de ne pas leur donner de vin, mais de « l'eau [décoction ?] d'orge » et du pain ou de la bouillie d'orge et d'avoine et autres choses semblables... et de leur faire de la musique, mais pas avec des timbales et des fifres, mais uniquement avec des cithares, violons, luths ou orgues positifs. »

> English (from the French): "You wrote to us about the unusual illness that has just broken out, asking for a report. It began with one woman, then with other persons who started to dance. We kept these persons for some days and questioned the physicians, who told us that it was a natural illness due to an astral conjunction and the heat of the season. It is nevertheless coming to an end. Those affected have been watched and we have had them taken to St Vitus." — "The physicians also advised us not to give them wine but barley-water and bread or porridge of barley and oats and similar things … and to make music for them, but not with kettledrums and fifes, only with citherns, fiddles, lutes or positive organs."

Comment: the bishop had written first, asking for a report; the reply is dated two days after the commission. It confirms independently (i) onset with one woman, (ii) the physicians' *natural* diagnosis (here "astral conjunction and heat" — which is compatible with, and probably the same verdict as, the "hitzig geblüt" of Jung's résumé), (iii) an optimistic assessment ("touche à sa fin") on 25 July, (iv) a dietary regimen (no wine; barley water; barley/oat bread and porridge — an anti-"heat" regimen), and (v) the medical origin of the string-only music rule that reappears in the council's ban ("mit saitenspiel … nach eines jeden conscienz"; "kein brucken/beucken schlagen"). The 2018 press kit also quotes, without giving the document, a sentence attributed to Sebastian Brant: « Nous sommes frappés par un grand malheur avec ces personnes malades qui ne s'arrêtent pas de danser… » (`fievre_danse_dossier.txt` line 231–233).

Vits tantz; kame vil leute 1518 an, denen mans fluchte, lieff aber auch viel schelmenwerck mit under, da mans anfieng aus Gottes wort zu widerlegen, liess alles nach. — Sp. n° 2216. — Pp. Schn.

> Translation: "St Vitus's dance, a wondrous cure. — At that time a dancing arose of young and old, who danced day and night, so that at Strasbourg they were given the Carpenters' and the Tanners' guild-hall, and special people were engaged who danced with them as much as needed. Several died of it. On the horse-market a platform was put up, they were let dance to drum and pipes, but it helped nothing at all. Many were sent on wagons to St Vitus at the *hellensteg* behind Zabern; others ran there themselves and fell down dancing before the image. Then a priest said a mass over them, whereupon they were given little crosses and red shoes on which, above and below, a † was drawn with chrism and sprinkled with holy water in St Vitus's name; [it] helped many and brought a large offering, which is why it is called St Vitus's dance; in 1518 it came upon many people upon whom it had been wished [as a curse], but much roguery also ran in with it; when one began to refute it from God's word, everything ceased."

### 3.10 The Imlin family chronicle, via Witkowski 1879 — Martin p. 119 [IMG `p119.jpg`; IIIF `$133`]

> „Anno 1518 jar 8 tag vor S. maria magdalena hub ein frauw an zu dantzen, vnd dan[a]ch weren woll 6 tag da leiss sey meinw γ [gnädigen Herrn] nach St. Vit gehn Zabern füren da war sey still, vnd die will sey noch uff dem weg da fing noch mehr an zu dantzen bei dem γ [Magistrats-] stall also daz in 4 tagen bey 34 frauw vnd man waren, da verbatten meinw γ [gnädigen Herrn] die drummeln vnd pfiffen vnd fürt ein teil uff die gewererstub [Zunftstube der Gerber] die and uff die Zimmerleutstub, vnd an dem and tag da leiss man sey alle nach S. > Translation: "In the year 1518, 8 days before St Mary Magdalene [c. 14 July] a woman began to dance, and thereafter [it] lasted a good 6 days; then my gracious lords had her taken to St Vitus at Zabern; there she became quiet. And while she was still on the way, more began to dance by the [magistrates'] stable, so that in 4 days there were about 34 women and men; then my gracious lords forbade the drums and pipes and took one part to the Tanners' hall, the others to the Carpenters' hall, and on the next day they had them all taken to St Vitus and [there they] danced, and in 4 weeks there were more than 400 dancers."

### 3.11 Wilhelm Rem (Augsburg), *Cronica newer geschichten*, 1518 — CDS 25 (1896) p. 88 [IMG `../sources/cds-25-rem-1896/images/p088.jpg`; IIIF `bub_gb_RICIqSRvM78C$109`]

> Wie zů Straspurg vil leut sant Veitz tantz ankam.
> Anno dni. 1518 im sumer da kam es zů Straspurg fast vil leutt sant Veitz tantz an, daß ain tag wol bei 15 menschen ankam¹. es weret fast lang, also verbott man das tantzen und pfeiffen und paugkenschlagen.

(Marginal "[Bl. 47a]" begins at the next entry; Roth's note 1: "Im Jahre 1518 trat der Veitstanz mit großer Heftigkeit in Straßburg auf. S. > Translation: "How St Vitus's dance came upon many people at Strasbourg. — In the year of the Lord 1518, in summer, St Vitus's dance came upon very many people at Strasbourg, so that in one day it came upon a good 15 persons. It lasted very long; so dancing and piping and drum-beating were forbidden."

Value: an outside, contemporary notice (Augsburg, where the imperial diet of summer 1518 drew news from everywhere) that independently attests the summer of 1518, an incidence of "about 15 a day", a long duration, and the triple ban on dancing, piping and drumming. It says nothing of deaths, St Vitus pilgrimage or numbers in the hundreds.

### 3.12 Later rhymed and narrative reports (all [2ND], via Martin pp. 114–116 [IMG])

- Verses printed by Schilter 1698 from a lost manuscript chronicle ("Chron. M.S. - Hieronymus Gebwiler (contemporary humanist; ed. Stenzel 1926 pp. 74–75): not retrieved; the teachers' dossier gives only a moralising excerpt in French ("Il faudrait se souvenir de ces événements pendant mille ans, et limiter les danses, en particulier les danses obscènes et indécentes …").

### 6.2 What the contemporary record contains

1. **Onset and timing.** Around St Margaret's day (Extractus) / eight days before Magdalene (Imlin; Schadaeus) — i.e. 13–15 July 1518 — with a single woman; the council's first recorded discussion is Thursday 22 July; the commission met Friday 23 July and ordered three wagons to stand ready "uff die subent ure" (at seven o'clock); the letter to the bishop of Sunday 25 July already speaks of the illness as ending. A relapse ("wider erhept") followed; the dance ban of 2 August ran to Michaelmas (29 Sept); on Wednesday 11 August families and brotherhoods were ordered to keep their sick; on Tuesday 31 August the council was still voting the thank-offering (a hundredweight of wax). Rem: "es weret fast lang". The 2018 exhibition's "resorbed in about two months" is consistent with this. **Martin's "until after 29 September" rests on his misdating of Adolphi.**
2. **Numbers.** The only contemporary total is the Extractus's "uff fünfzig personen" (which may be the preamble of the 2 August mandate); Rem gives an incidence "wol bei 15" a day. All larger figures (over 100; over 200; over 400; "viel hundert") are from chroniclers writing between the 1570s and 1698, and Imlin (if contemporary) is the exception with ">400" — but that text is known only through Witkowski/Martin and could not be checked.
3. **Symptoms and behaviour.** The council documents describe only: dancing "tag und nacht", "jämmerlich zu sehen"; people "beladen" with the sickness who must be "nyder getüschet" (calmed/held down) or would "uff den gassen louffen"; one woman who "uffgehüpfft" and ran after a bagpipe; a boy who began after watching the dancers ("hab zugesehen und hüt morgen angefangen dantzen"); some persons "nit so geschickt" to walk round an altar and pay a pfennig. Nothing about foaming, screaming, broken ribs, bleeding feet, exhaustion to death, hallucinations or convulsions (those belong to later literature). The one medical detail — the physicians' regimen against "heat" (no wine, barley water, barley/oat food) and the advice that only string instruments (no drums/fifes) be played — points to a humoral reading of over-excitement.
4. **Council measures, in order:** (i) 22 July: stop the *termen*(?)/drums, string music in private; (ii) 23 July: commission; parents to keep children or pay; poor dancers in three troops to St Vitus with masses; (iii) 25 July: report to the bishop with the physicians' verdict and regimen; (iv) 2 August: mandate (Brant) — general ban on dancing till Michaelmas, no drumming, exception for weddings and first masses with strings; (v) undated notices: brotherhood box-masters responsible for their sick — calm them or take them to St Vitus; no music, jewels, fine clothes, no running in the streets; (vi) 11 August: notes to the guilds — every burgher to keep and calm his afflicted child or servants at home; brotherhoods to keep journeymen at their cost or send them "to the saints, wherever they please"; (vii) undated: solemn mass in Our Lady's chapel; dancers in the Hospital sent to the saint; offering from the whole city; "leichtfertige" banished for a time; (viii) "in several sessions": physicians' verdict, poor people's wish for masses, the vicar's reply; (ix) 31 August: wax image of one hundredweight debated (send it, or set it on a cathedral altar until the new chapel was finished); resolved: candle + sung mass + three side-masses on behalf of council and city.
5. **Cause attributed.** Contemporary: the physicians — "natürlich krankheit" from hot blood / an astral conjunction and the heat of the season; the council also treated it as something a saint could remedy ("zu dem lieben heyligen"). Later: a curse ("denen man St. Veits tantz fluchte", Specklin/Wencker) and fraud ("schelmenwerk"); Wencker adds that Gospel preaching ended it.
6. **Deaths.** See §7.

### 6.3 What it does not contain, and discrepancies to flag

- No contemporary death, no name of the first woman (Paracelsus's "Frau Troffea" is a 1530s invention, per the 2018 dossier), no "Frau Troffea", no "400 dancers" (except Imlin), no "platforms on the markets with hired dancers" (that is Specklin/Wencker) — but note the council *did* pay musicians/servants and *did* put dancers on guild-halls, so the later picture is not baseless.
- Martin p. 232 [OCR]: „Auch beim Strassburger Veitstanz kommt in keinem der zeitgenössischen Berichte der Name Veitstanz vor, und ich vermute, dass man sich scheute, den Namen auszusprechen oder niederzuschreiben.“ This is contradicted by his own material: the Extractus's 1518 entry has "ein schwäre erschreckliche krankheit mit St. Vitstanz" and Rem (1518) has "sant Veitz tantz". What is true is that the surviving originals (23 July protocol, Instruction, notices, mandate) never name the disease — they say "die kranckheit des dantzens", "der armen menschen halb so izt dantzen" — while calling the shrine "Sant Vit"/"Sanct Vit zum Holenstein".
- Place-name of the shrine: "Holenstein" (original dorsal note), "Hohenstein" (Extractus copy), "holen stein" (Specklin), "hellensteg" (Wencker 1637), "Rotenstein" (Schilter's misreading, repeated in the 2018 teachers' dossier with "[recte Hohlenstein]").
- Readings: "brucken" (Dacheux) vs "beucken" (Martin) for drums; "termen (?)"; "Gladi Bocklin" (Martin) vs "Claude/Claus Böcklin" (2018); Martin's brackets "[22. Juli]" for Margarethen and "[29. September]" for Adolphi are wrong; Martin calls Rem a Nuremberg chronicler (he is Augsburg).
- Jung's no. 4398 is a résumé; its "hitzigem geblüt" must not be quoted as the protocol's words.
- Dating: the 1518 originals carry only "etc. XVIII"; the assignment to 1518 rests on the hand, the magistrates' names and the concordance with the Extractus (Martin p. 115) — now beyond doubt given Rem's dated notice and the ADBR letter dated 1518.
- The Extractus's index line shows six folios in Wencker's volume touching the dance (159b, 161b, 171a–b, 172a, 174b): the printed extracts (3439, 3443, 4398) do not exhaust what Wencker had; fol. 161b and 174b are not represented by any printed entry.

## 7. Answers to the key questions

**Q1. Earliest dated record and its start date.** The earliest *dated* document is the commission protocol of **Friday after Mary Magdalene = 23 July 1518** with its *Instruction* "Veneris post Magdalene etc. XVIII" (AVES III 200/15; Martin 1914 Anlage I–II, §3.4.3–4), followed by the **letter to the bishop of 25 July 1518** (ADBR 12 J 1495, §3.5) and the **mandate of 2 August 1518** (AVES 1 MR 3 f. 72r, §3.6). The earliest council *decision* recorded (through Wencker's Extractus) is of **Thursday 22 July 1518** ("5a Mariae Magdalenae. Item als etlich frauen und knaben den bössen dantz tantzen …", §3.1). The onset itself is given as "um Margarethen" (Extractus; St Margaret = 13 July) and "8 tag vor S. maria magdalena" (Imlin; = 14 July), with one woman dancing 4–6 days before being sent to Saverne — hence the conventional "mid-July 1518" (Martin: 15 July).

**Q2. Numbers of dancers per layer.** Contemporary council layer: "uff fünfzig personen" (Extractus 3443, probably the mandate's own words); no figure in the originals of 23 July, in the six notices, or in the mandate; Rem (1518): "fast vil leutt … ain tag wol bei 15 menschen". Later/unverifiable: Imlin (via Witkowski) 1 → 34 → >400; Duntzenheim (via Jung) 1 → 34 → >200; Schadaeus (via Boersch) 1 → 34 → >200; Specklin (c. 1580) "über 100 … auff einmal"; Wencker 1637 "viel"; Schilter's verses "Viel hundert". The 2018 exhibition (Clementz) settles on "quelque cinquante personnes".

**Q3. Does ANY contemporary record — or Specklin — mention deaths?** No contemporary record does: not the 23 July protocol, not the Instruction, not the six notices, not the mandate, not the Extractus entries 3443/4398 (which speak of the sick being "fast erledigt" and "gesund zurückkehrten"), not the 25 July letter as translated, not Rem, not Imlin, not Duntzenheim/Schadaeus as summarised. **Specklin does**: „Viel tantzten sich zu tode.“ ("Many danced themselves to death.", BSCMHA XIV p. 308 [IMG]). So do Kleinlawel 1625: „Biß das sie fielen in ohnmacht, / Viel sind Todt drüber blieben“ (via Martin p. 116) and Wencker 1637: „Etliche stürben dran.“ ("Several died of it.", BSCMHA XV p. 148 [IMG]). Boersch attributed the deaths to "Goldmeyer und Kleinlawel"; Martin found that Goldmeyer does not mention 1518 at all. The earliest death statement is therefore Specklin's, written some 60 years after the event, in a compilation that Reuss reconstructed from an 18th-c. gloss and Rœhrich's notes.

**Q4. The council's sequence of actions with dates.** 22 July (Thu): restrain the *termen*(?)/drums, string music in private. 23 July (Fri): commission Böcklin–Hoffmeister–Herlin; parents to keep or pay; poor dancers in three troops with riders and servants on three wagons (Hospital 1, Frauenhaus 2) to St Vitus; sung masses at Saverne, led round the altar, pfennig offerings from the alms money. 25 July (Sun): report to Bishop Wilhelm; physicians' verdict and regimen. 2 Aug (Mon): mandate signed by Brant — no dancing till Michaelmas in city and Burgbann, fine 30 sh., no drumming, weddings/first masses with strings excepted. Undated (early August?): six notices — brotherhoods' box-masters to calm their sick or take them to St Vitus, no music/jewels/fine clothes, no running in the streets. 11 Aug (Wed): notes to the guilds — burghers keep and calm their own; brotherhoods keep journeymen at their cost or send them "to the saints, wherever they please" (St Vitus struck out in the original). Undated: solemn mass in Our Lady's chapel; dancers now in the Hospital sent to the saint; city offering; loose persons banished. "In several sessions": physicians and vicar consulted. 31 Aug (Tue): wax image/candle of one hundredweight with the Rentmeister; sung mass + three side-masses. 29 Sept: end of the ban. (All from §3.1, §3.2, §3.4–3.6.)

**Q5. Origin of the "hitzig blut"/physicians' verdict.** It comes from Wencker's Extractus fol. 171 as **résuméd by Andreas Jung** and printed by Dacheux in 1899 (BSCMHA XIX p. 42, no. 4398): "die Aerzte erklärten es für eine natürliche krankheit, die von hitzigem geblüt herkomme" — Jung's paraphrase; the only verbatim words are the vicar's: "diwyl die Artz anzeigen, daß es eine natürlich krankheit sy, daß man auch natürlich mittel mit in versuche". Independently, the magistrate's own letter of 25 July 1518 (ADBR 12 J 1495; 2018 French translation) says the physicians called it "une maladie naturelle due à une conjonction astrale et à la chaleur du moment" and prescribed a cooling diet and string music only. So "natural illness" is contemporary and secure; the specific formula "hot blood" is attested at third hand; "astral conjunction + heat" is the formulation of the original letter (in translation).

**Q6. What the record says about symptoms.** Almost nothing clinical: dancing day and night, pitiful to behold; sick persons who must be held down/calmed ("nyder getüschet", "zutuschen") or who run in the streets; a relapsing epileptic woman who "hopped up" and followed a bagpipe; a schoolboy who started after watching; some too unfit to walk round an altar unaided; a physicians' regimen implying "heat" (no wine, barley water, barley/oat food, gentle string music). Later chroniclers add falling down exhausted, fainting and death (Specklin, Kleinlawel, Wencker), not eating for days (Schilter's verses), and cures by red shoes, chrism crosses and masses at the Hohlenstein (Specklin, Wencker).
