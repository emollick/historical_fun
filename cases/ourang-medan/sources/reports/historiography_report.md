# Ourang Medan — historiography: what prior investigators established, with what evidence

Working copies of the fetched texts (`fetched/`) are not included in this folder.

## 0. Bottom line

1. The investigative literature is thin and almost entirely non-academic: one long-form journalistic investigation (Theo Paijmans, *Fortean Times* FT402, Feb 2021), one podcast synthesis (Skeptoid #756, Dec 2020), one self-published e-book (Alexander Butziger, 2014), two blog posts that made the key digital finds (Butziger, Nov 2013; Estelle Hargraves, Dec 2015), Roy Bainton's 1999 *Fortean Times* piece (later retracted by him in 2017), and Wikipedia. No peer-reviewed or museum treatment exists.
2. What is firmly established (with primary evidence in hand): the story was written by Silvio Scherl-Scherli of Trieste and first printed in *Il Piccolo* (Trieste) on 16 Oct 1940 (Paijmans 2021, via the Trieste library); it was syndicated by AP into British papers on 21–22 Nov 1940 (Hargraves 2015); it was resold to *Elsevier's Weekblad* (Netherlands) on 17 Jan 1948, p. 16, with follow-ups 14 Feb and 28 Feb 1948 (Paijmans 2021); the three *De locomotief* (Semarang) parts of 3 Feb, 28 Feb, 13 Mar 1948 are colonial reprints, not the origin; the May 1952 US Coast Guard *Proceedings* item is an unsigned editorial ("We Sail Together"), not a report and not a letter; the 1959 "CIA letter" is an unsolicited letter *to* the CIA from a member of the public whose wording is lifted from Frank Edwards (1956) and the *Proceedings*.
3. A Dutch printing earlier than *De locomotief* 3 Feb 1948 *has* been reported: (a) Paijmans (2021) located *Elsevier's Weekblad* 17 Jan 1948 (not on Delpher); (b) the **Leekster courant 24 Jan 1948** ("Mysterie, dat de zee nimmer zal prijsgeven", p. 1) is in Delpher — it was only digitised/published on Delpher on **9 July 2024** — and it has been quoted by **CNBC Indonesia (22 May, 25 May, 7 June 2025) and delik.tv (8 June 2025)**, which however mislabel it an "Indonesian" newspaper and do not notice that it is an explicit digest of *Elsevier's Weekblad* ("aldus een verslaggever van Elsevier's Weekblad"). No English-, Dutch- or German-language investigation (any Wikipedia, Bainton, Hargraves, Butziger, Skeptoid, Paijmans, silentthrill, de173, Yacht.de 2026) cites it. So: not new as a bare citation; new as an analysed source (its priority in Delpher, and its dependence on Elsevier).

### 1.2 Roy Bainton (marine writer, ex-merchant seaman)
- "Cargo of Death", *Fortean Times*, Sept 1999, p. 28 (Skeptoid cites FT126; Paijmans cites "FT125:28-31, Aug 1999" — issue number discrepancy). Full text: Wayback copy of neswa.org.au, https://web.archive.org/web/20070205223141/http://www.neswa.org.au/Library/Articles/A%20cargo%20of%20death.htm (`fetched/bainton_1999_cargo_of_death.txt`).
  What he says he checked: Lloyd's Shipping Registers ("no mention of the case"); Hocking's *Dictionary of Disasters at Sea 1824–1962* (not there); *Sea Breezes* (editor Capt. Andrew Douglas; a plea for information printed); wrote to the Admiralty, the Registrar of Shipping and Seamen, the National Maritime Museum (all said "go to Amsterdam"); "Searching the Dutch Shipping records in Amsterdam … no mention of the ship at all. There was a Medan, but she had been scrapped before World War II"; "my enquiries to the Maritime Authority in Singapore drew a blank". Says the *Proceedings of the Merchant Marine Council* was "a frequently mentioned document (which I have so far been unable to trace)". Names of the two ships (City of Baltimore, Silver Star, Grace Lines) came from **Prof. Theodor F. Siersdorfer (Essen)**, "on the case for 45 years", who relied on **Otto Mielke's 32-page booklet (1954)**; Siersdorfer also mentioned **Alvar Mastin**, a German in Hull who in the 1950s wrote to Grace Lines for the Silver Star's crew list and log "and was met with a stony silence". Bainton notes Lloyd's shows the Silver Star "at the time … had changed owners and had another name … Santa Cecilia". He first heard the yarn in 1961 in the mess room of the tramp steamer *Port Halifax*. Earlier fortean literature he names: Jessup 1955, Gaddis 1965, Damon Wilson *Big Book of the Unexplained* 1998, Robert V. Hulse in *Fate* 1953 (via the Borderland Sciences website). Conclusion 1999: speculation about a smuggled nerve-gas/cyanide-nitroglycerine cargo (Geneva Protocol, Speer on Tabun, Unit 731) and a governmental cover-up; explicitly labelled "pure speculation".
- *The Mammoth Book of Unexplained Phenomena* (2013): per Paijmans and silentthrill, restates Lloyd's and Amsterdam searches, "more than 30 years" of research, Siersdorfer's 45 years without a trace.
- Interview: *Thinking Sideways* podcast, "The Ourang Medan", 24 Oct 2013 (Wayback of thinkingsidewayspodcast.com, `fetched/thinkingsideways_wb2019.txt`; the live domain is now a gambling spam site).
- **Retraction:** "Roy Bainton Writes", roybainton.com, 29 Aug 2017 (`fetched/bainton_blog_2017.txt`): "For 20 years I became the world's 'go to' internet scribe for this nautical chestnut"; Saga magazine "sent me to Amsterdam's Maritime Museum to track the Ourang Medan down. The archives yielded nothing"; Butziger's 2014 book "excavated deep beneath my staggered research and proved what I'd suspected all along – the story was a worn-out threadbare hoax propagated by (among others) the Nazis", leaving him "with much justifiable egg on my face". (The "Nazis" part is Butziger's thesis, not evidence.)

### 1.3 Otto Mielke (1954) and Theodor Siersdorfer
Mielke, *Das Totenschiff in der Südsee. Dampfer "Ourang Medan"*, Anker-Hefte "Seefahrt in aller Welt" no. 1 (de.wikipedia) / no. 2 (Paijmans), Arthur Moewig, Munich 1954, 32 pp.; reprinted 1979 as *Vom Schicksal verdammt. Tankmotorschiff "Esso Bolivar" – Dampfer "Ourang Medan"* (Paijmans n. 13). Introduced: SOS on 27 June 1947 picked up by City of Baltimore and the Grace Line steamer Silver Star; boarding 28 June 1947; an Asian crew; a lifeboat reaching Taongi on 12 July 1947 with one survivor, "Jerry Rabbit"; cargo of Zyankali and nitroglycerine (summaries in de173, silentthrill, SZ 2019, Bainton 1999). Mielke, a former Kriegsmarine sailor who wrote pulp sea stories, gives no sources. Siersdorfer (Essen) never published; he is known only through Bainton. Nothing by Siersdorfer is retrievable.

### 1.4 Alexander Butziger (US thriller writer; "Alex" of Bermuda Triangle Central)
- Blog post "Ourang Medan", Bermuda Triangle Central, 9 Nov 2013, http://bermudatrianglecentral.blogspot.com/2013/11/ourang-medan.html; Goodreads mirror. Found on Gallica: *Sept-Jours* (Vichy France) no. 45, 7 Sept 1941, p. 9, "Après Vingt Mois — Le Mystère de l'Ourang-Medan Est Éclairci", referring back to no. 13, 29 Dec 1940 ("Les sauveteurs trouvèrent un navire immobile et un équipage de cadavres…", p. 6). Conclusion: Nazi/Vichy propaganda — a thesis nobody else has endorsed with evidence (the Italian 1940 origin refutes it).
- E-book *The Ourang Medan: Conjuring a Ghost Ship* (Kindle, July 2014, ASIN B00LKIXB98). Not read by me (paywalled); contents inferred from reviews, Bainton 2017 and de173.

### 1.6 Estelle Hargraves ("Estelle", The Skittish Library, Liverpool) — verified
- "The Myth of the Ourang Medan Ghost Ship, 1940", 29 Dec 2015, https://skittishlibrary.co.uk/the-myth-of-the-ourang-medan-ghost-ship-1940/ (`fetched/skittish_2015.txt`). Byline is "Estelle"; the surname is confirmed by her Reddit username *Estellehargraves* (r/UnsolvedMysteries, "Pretty sure I've solved the mystery of the Ourang Medan Ghost Ship", Dec 2015/Jan 2016; Wayback copy `fetched/reddit_wb.txt`) and by Skeptoid's reference "Hargraves, E. (2015, December 29)". She is an amateur ("I just upload things from my old book collections and interesting things I've found in the archives"), not a Fortean Times author.
- Evidence: British Newspaper Archive scans of *Yorkshire Evening Post* 21 Nov 1940 and *Daily Mirror* 22 Nov 1940 (both AP, dateline Trieste, "Thursday"); the 1940 version: steamship, SE of Solomon Islands, SOS "Beg ships with shortwave wireless get touch doctor. Urgent" / "Probable second officer dead … SOS urgent assistance warship" / "crew has…", source a merchant marine officer of the (unnamed) rescuing ship, no survivor, no lurid faces. She notes the ship isn't named in 1948 either ("The detail of the Silver Star was thrown in later"), that the Silver Star had become *Santa Cecilia* by 1948, and that Trieste is the link to Scherli. Her working assumptions ("the story first appeared in De locomotief … 3–28 February 1948"; "1952 was the earliest known mention in English") were Wikipedia's state in Dec 2015 and are superseded.
- Conclusion: "a modern fabrication, an urban legend"; Scherli "didn't get quite the notoriety he sought the first time round … tried again" in 1948.
- Comment thread adds: "HD" (Apr–May 2016) found Scherli on Ancestry passenger/crew lists (born 1901, Trieste, MS Vulcania) and cited a Dutch forum; "Salvador" (Jan 2017) cited Bainton's "Medan scrapped before WWII" and Plimsoll data for a Canadian Silver Star built 1943; "Hans de Kraker" (Feb 2017) retrieved the YEP page himself and raised the CIA letter; Estelle replied the CIA correspondent "seems to quote verbatim from the original article" in the *Proceedings*.

### 1.8 Theo Paijmans, "Death Ship: The Final Voyage of the Ourang Medan", *Fortean Times* FT402 (Feb 2021), pp. 32–37 — the most thorough prior investigation
Texts: archive.org "fortean-times-08.2021/Fortean Times 02.2021" (`fetched/ft_2021_02_djvu.txt`, lines 5917–6720) and the standalone item https://archive.org/details/paijmans-ourang-medan (`fetched/paijmans_2021_djvu.txt`, uploaded May 2024). Paijmans is a Dutch writer/FT regular. His documented chain (his endnotes):
1. **Elseviers Weekblad, 17 Jan 1948, p. 16, "Brand in Luik IV. Raadselachtige Ondergang van de Ourang Medan"** — first-person eyewitness narrative, two photographs (ship on its side; dead officer face-down), 400 nm SE of the Marshall Islands, mid-June 1947, "22 dead sailors". Follow-ups: **14 Feb 1948, p. 4, "De geheimzinnige Ramp der Oerang Medan. De enige overlevende"** (introduces Scherli by name: "The author, Silvio Scherli, is now in Trieste. He vouches for the authenticity…"; the Franciscan missionary on Taongi; 15,000 crates from a Chinese port) and **28 Feb 1948, p. 19, "Het mysterie der Ourang Medan opgelost"** (the cargo list: sulphuric acid, liquid nitroglycerine; the German castaway buried "by the old palm").
2. *De locomotief* 3 Feb, 28 Feb, 13 Mar 1948 ("a few newspapers in the Dutch colonies were first to repeat the story, even while expressing doubts"); *De West* (Paramaribo) 16 Apr 1948 "Schip vergaan" (says the crew were Indonesian).
3. *Obersteirische Volkszeitung* (Leoben, Austria) 24 Jul 1948; Ennio Garieri, "Il Mistero dell'Ourang Medan", *L'Illustrazione del Popolo*, 31 Oct 1948, p. 5; **American Weekly (Hearst Sunday supplement), Oct 1948, "Secrets of the Sea"** — found in the Times Union (Albany), Baltimore American, Chicago Herald-American, Oregonian — which says the Dutch weekly's story was "apparently told by an Italian officer on a ship that had answered the Ourang Medan's distress call" and that Elsevier's editor "had bought the story and photographs of the Italian officer and the latter had disappeared".
4. *Proceedings of the Merchant Marine Council*, May 1952, p. 107; "Mystery ships", *Leicester Chronicle*, 17 Jan 1953, p. 18; Robert V. Hulse, "Death at Sea", *Fate*, June 1953, pp. 73–74 (cites the *Proceedings*); Mielke 1954; "Panorama", *Amigoe di Curaçao*, 7 Jan 1955 (pesticides angle); Frank Edwards, "The Ship of Death", *Winnipeg Free Press*, 27 June 1964; Jessup 1955; Jessup in the *Liverpool Echo*, 30 Aug 1955 ("Kidnapped to Space?"); Gaddis 1965; Sanderson, *Uninvited Guests* (1967) and *Invisible Residents* (1970); Persinger & Lafrenière 1977; Steiger & Whritenour, "Unidentified Underwater Saucers", *SAGA*; CIA letter 1959; Bainton 1999/2013; Russian: Lev Skryagin, *Voice of the Sea* (1973), Nikolai Vnukov's novel *Sverre calls for help* (1978: a Nazi gas torpedo), A. Leshukonskii, *Tainy XX veka* no. 19, 2014, pp. 26–27 (credit Mikhail Gershtein); Peter H. Block, "Das Totenschiff", *SchiffClassic* 5/2019, pp. 29–35 (gives the castaway a name).
5. Backwards: *7 Jours* 29 Dec 1940 (with "the photo of the dead officer that was to be published eight years later in the Dutch weekly") and 7 Sept 1941 (13 Nov 1939; American destroyer); **Silvio Scherl-Scherli, "Il Mistero dell'Ourang-Medan", *L'Illustrazione Italiana*, 8 Dec 1940, p. 846**; YEP 21 Nov, Daily Mirror 22 Nov, **Hampshire Telegraph 29 Nov 1940 "Story of Weird Sea Tragedy"**; three Vienna papers: *(Neuigkeits-)Welt-Blatt* 20 Oct 1940 "Drama im Stillen Ozean", *Illustrierte Kronen-Zeitung* 20 Oct 1940 "SOS vom Meutererschiff", *(Österreichische) Volks-Zeitung* 29 Oct 1940 "Das Geheimnis des Totenschiffes" — one of which names *Piccolo di Trieste* as its source; **"Il mistero dell'Ourang-Medan", *Il Piccolo delle Ore Diciotto* [the evening edition], 16 Oct 1940**, obtained from the Trieste library/archives, byline Silvio Scherl-Scherli, same two photos; here the finder is "the American torpedo boat W716"; the US Coast Guard told him (email, 21–25 Aug 2015) that no such boat existed in 1939 and the coding was not in use.
6. Scherli: "a number of Italian forteans dived into the minutiae of Italian journalism — but to no avail"; the one document found is Scherli's letter of 7 Feb 1954 to Paul Hahn (American Tobacco Co.) on Trieste letterhead offering "research material on lung cancer" for $780 (UCSF Industry Documents, id lnwp0215).
7. Also: a real *Medan* (Rotterdamsche Lloyd, launched 13 Aug 1910, sold 24 June 1930, scrapped Kobe 18 July 1930).
Conclusion: Scherli "crafted a template attractive enough to resell to different newspapers and magazines for eight long years", altering the finder ship and the year; photos probably staged/obtained in wartime Trieste; the tale recycles the *Mary Celeste*. (Skeptoid 2020 and Wikipedia do not cite this article; silentthrill 2021 and Yacht.de 2026 do so only indirectly.)

### 1.9 The May 1952 *Proceedings of the Merchant Marine Council* — read in full
Wayback copy of the USCG PDF: https://web.archive.org/web/2016id_/https://www.uscg.mil/proceedings/archive/1952/Vol9_No5_May1952.pdf (`fetched/proceedings_may1952_wb.pdf`, text in `proceedings_may1952.txt`). "We Sail Together" begins on p. 106; it is an **unsigned editorial essay** on sea mysteries — the Spanish *Fell Antonia* (Ceylon, 1890s), a nameless Dutch derelict found by the *Phoebus* in 1907 ("Netherland Board of Trade files"), a Tasmanian schooner-ketch in the Lacepede Islands, the schooner *Star*, then: "Perhaps one of the most perturbing sea dramas occurred in February 1948. Radio silence was broken with an urgent S.O.S. from the S.S. Ourang Medan, a Dutch vessel, then proceeding through the Straits of Malacca… 'S.O.S. from Ourang Medan * * * we float. All officers, including the Captain, dead in chartroom and on the bridge. Probably whole of crew dead * * *.' … 'I die.' … found adrift approximately 50 miles from her indicated position … Sprawled on their backs, the frozen faces upturned to the sun with mouths gaping open and eyes staring, the dead bodies resembled horrible caricatures. Even the ship's dog was found dead … a fire was discovered in No. 4 hold … an explosion followed and the Ourang Medan sank. To this day no explanation has been offered" — then the *Titanic* and a plea for safety measures. No author, no source, no rescuing ship named. It is **not a report and not a letter to the editor**; the claim that it "was based on a letter to the editor" appears nowhere in the literature and nothing in the item supports it.

### 1.10 The 1959 "CIA letter" — read in full
CIA FOIA reading room: https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/cia-rdp80r01731r000300010043-5 (PDF https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/docs/CIA-RDP80R01731R000300010043-5.pdf; archive.org mirror LETTERTOSanitizedFROMCHMARCKJR80R01731R0003000100435; text in `fetched/cia_marck_1959_djvu.txt`). Dated 5 Dec 1959; "Executive Registry"; "Approved For Release 2003/05/23"; addressee redacted; the closing block reads "C. H. Marck, Jr. / Assistant to the Director — CENTRAL INTELLIGENCE AGENCY / Washington, D.C." — i.e. the *addressee's* title, which several sites misread as Marck's job. Marck refers to his letter of 29 May 1958 "concerning crews disappearing from ships", says "I have just read a weird story concerning the Dutch vessel S.S. Ourang Medan", asks "do you think 'something from the unknown' is involved?", retells the story (early Feb 1948; Malacca Strait; "Dutch and British listening posts"; "Rescue ships from Dutch Sumatra and British Malaya"; "only fifty miles from the position given"; "the faithful 'sparks' … his hand still on the sending key"; "a small terrier … teeth bared in anger or agony"; No. 4 hold), quotes "a report of the PROCEEDINGS OF THE MERCHANT MARINE COUNCIL", and links it to "fiery spheres" in medieval chronicles. New observation: his wording is a pastiche of **Frank Edwards** (*Strangest of All*, 1956, pp. 70–72: "Dutch and British radio centers", "Rescue craft from Sumatra and Malaya", "teeth bared in anger, or agony", "In the report filed with the Proceedings…") and the *Proceedings* ("50 miles", "No. 4 hold") — so his "source" was fortean retellings, not any official record. It is evidence of public interest only.

## 3. Hypotheses in the literature and their proponents

1. **Pure fabrication by Silvio Scherli, resold and embellished 1940–1959** — De locomotief's own editors (13 Mar 1948 disclaimer); grenswetenschap.nl commenters (June 2012); Hargraves (2015: "somebody's pants on fire"); Skeptoid (2020); Paijmans (2021: a "template" resold for eight years; staged photos; Mary Celeste pastiche); Bainton (2017, conceding hoax); Yacht.de (2026). This is the consensus of everyone who has looked at primary sources.
2. **Wartime propaganda (Vichy/Nazi)** — Butziger 2013/2014 only (Bainton 2017 repeats it uncritically). Refuted by the Italian origin.
3. **Hoax by a journalist / "50-year-old April Fool by a bored tabloid hack"** — floated by Bainton 1999; effectively merged with (1) once Scherli was identified as a freelance journalist/radio operator.
4. **Sailors' yarn (Seemannsgarn) that acquired details over time** — Bainton 1999 (heard it in a mess room in 1961); Welle/SZ 2019; de.wikipedia. Compatible with (1): Scherli's printed story fed the oral tradition, not vice versa.
5. **Real chemical-cargo accident** (sulphuric acid + nitroglycerine + potassium cyanide → hydrogen cyanide; seawater intrusion) — the story's own explanation from 7 Jours 1941 / Elsevier & De locomotief 1948 / Mielke 1954; taken seriously by Bainton 1999, the German popular press (SchiffClassic 2019), and countless retellings.
6. **Smuggled Japanese/German nerve agent (tabun) with Allied connivance; Unit 731; scuttling** — Bainton 1999 (speculation), an unsourced paragraph in en.wikipedia, SZ 2019; no evidence.
7. **Carbon-monoxide poisoning from a smouldering boiler fire** — Gaddis 1965.
8. **Methane / hydrogen-sulphide upwelling from the seabed** — Dutch popular sites (grenswetenschap 2012), Forde 2015 (listing), many clickbait pieces.
9. **UFO / "something from the unknown" / paranormal** — Jessup 1955 (Liverpool Echo 1955), C. H. Marck Jr. 1959, Steiger & Whritenour (*SAGA*), Soviet fiction (Vnukov 1978: a Nazi gas torpedo).
10. **Garbled memory of a real 1939–40 incident with a genuine survivor who resurfaced in 1948** — only in blog comments ("HD", Skittish Library, 2016); no evidence; contradicted by the 1948 texts presenting a June 1947 event.
11. **A real ship "Medan" (1910–30) lent the name** — Bainton 1999; Paijmans 2021 (coincidence, not explanation).

## 4. Has anyone reported a Dutch item earlier than De locomotief 3 Feb 1948?

**Yes, twice, in different senses — but nobody has done the analysis.**

- **Paijmans (FT402, Feb 2021)** documented that the Dutch story began in the metropolitan weekly ***Elsevier's Weekblad* on 17 Jan 1948 (p. 16)**, with the second and third instalments on 14 and 28 Feb 1948; De locomotief (3 Feb, 28 Feb, 13 Mar) simply reprinted them a fortnight later. Win Brooks' American Weekly piece (Oct 1948) had already named "Elsevier's Weekly, January 1948" as the source, and es.wikipedia/Ghost-in-my-Machine/Mental Floss repeat that, but only Paijmans located the issues. Elsevier's Weekblad is not on Delpher (KB SRU DTS query: 0 hits), so Delpher-based researchers never see it; en/de/it Wikipedia still call De locomotief the first 1948 printing.
- **Leekster courant, 24 Jan 1948, p. 1, "Mysterie, dat de zee nimmer zal prijsgeven"** (Delpher, Groninger Archieven, identifier MMRHCG04:235133008:mpeg21:a00006; the run "Jrg. 3, nr. 1 (3 jan. 1948) …" was published on Delpher on **9 July 2024**). The OCR text (saved) is a condensed rendering of the Elsevier article and says so: "…aldus een verslaggever van 'Elsevier's Weekblad'". It gives the SOS with position "20 gr. Westerbreedte 179 gr. Oosterlengte", "midden Juni 1947", 400 nm SE of the Marshalls, 22 dead, a dead dog, the "pre-war" description of the ship (5,000 tons, one funnel, forty years old, ten changes of name and flag, a Chinese troopship 1935–37). It is the earliest Dutch-language item in Delpher (KB SRU "Ourang Medan" returns 10 records: Leekster 24 Jan; De locomotief 3 Feb ×2, 28 Feb ×4, 13 Mar ×2; Amigoe 7 Jan 1955; spelling variants "Oerang", "Orang-Medan", "Ourang-Medan", "Medam" return nothing earlier; "Orang Medan" adds De West 16 Apr 1948). Nobody in the Western literature cites it — it was digitised after Paijmans, Skeptoid and Yodin's list — **but CNBC Indonesia (22 & 25 May, 7 June 2025) and delik.tv (8 June 2025) quote it**, mislabelled as an Indonesian paper and without noticing its Elsevier dependence or its Delpher priority. So the correct claim for our report is: "first to identify the Leekster courant as the earliest Delpher-indexed Dutch printing and as a digest of Elsevier's Weekblad 17 Jan 1948", not "first to find it".
- Other Dutch periodical hit: Delpher DTS returns *De schalmei* (a periodical), April 1948 — after De locomotief; not examined.
- Dutch web: no Dutch investigator has published beyond the 2012 grenswetenschap thread (which found De locomotief) and popular summaries (historianet.nl). No "Marhisdata note" and no "Dick Kooiman" could be found.

## 5. Claims in the literature that are unsupported or wrong (summary)
- "Earliest known reference is the May 1952 Proceedings" (id/pt Wikipedia, Hargraves 2015 as premise, Bainton 1999 couldn't even find it) — wrong.
- "De locomotief 3 Feb 1948 is the first printing / first Dutch printing" (en/de/it Wikipedia, Skeptoid) — wrong (Elsevier 17 Jan 1948; Leekster 24 Jan 1948; and 1940 printings).
- "De locomotief, 2 Feb 1948" (Ghost in My Machine, Yacht.de) — wrong date (3 Feb).
- "The CIA letter is a CIA memo / shows CIA investigation / was classified top secret" — wrong; it is a citizen's letter; it even quotes the Proceedings by name.
- "C. H. Marck Jr., Assistant to the Director, CIA" as author (many sites) — misreading of the addressee block.
- "The Proceedings item was based on a letter to the editor" — unsupported (unsigned editorial).
- "Nazi/Vichy propaganda" (Butziger; Bainton 2017) — contradicted by the Italian original.
- "Hargraves found the 1959 Export Trade report" (Skeptoid) — she quoted Wikipedia's Google-Books snippet.
- Grokipedia's "Leicester Chronicle 21 Nov 1940" and "Bainton researched in the 2010s" — fabricated/confused.
- "Bainton established the Silver Star's identity with high probability" (en.wikipedia) — the literature disagrees on which ship (Santa Cecilia/Santa Juana of Grace Line vs. Silver Star Park, Canadian, 1943) and none places a "Silver Star" near the Marshalls in June 1947.
- Bainton's FT issue: FT125 (Paijmans) vs FT126/Sept 1999 (Skeptoid, neswa copy) — cite as "Fortean Times, Sept 1999 (FT126), p. 28" with the caveat.
- Skeptoid's "the Silver Star Park … renamed the Santa Cecilia in 1945" vs Hargraves "by 1948 renamed SS Santa Cecilia" vs de173 "Santa Cecilia (built 1940) → Silver Star → Santa Juana" vs silentthrill "Santa Cecilia #3 renamed Santa Juana in 1947" — mutually inconsistent; unresolved.
