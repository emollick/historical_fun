# Ourang Medan — Trove / Papers Past / NewspaperSG (+ Pacific Islands Monthly, KB-Delpher Indies press) research report

Companion file: `sources/search-logs/newspapersg_digitalnz_search_log.md` (every query + hit count). Working copies of the per-article files (`articles/`, URL at top), the OCR of the Dutch/Indies items (`kb/`), the NLB clipping images (`clips/`, PNG), the raw search HTML/JSON (`sg_raw/`), the 192 Pacific Islands Monthly issues (`pim/`, full OCR) and the Papers Past/Evening Post 1940 battery (`pp_1940_battery.txt`) are not included in this folder.

## 1. Bottom line

1. **No contemporary (1940 or 1947–48) report of an "Ourang Medan" / "Orang Medan" exists in NewspaperSG** (Straits Times, Singapore Free Press, Malaya Tribune, Morning Tribune, Sunday Tribune, Straits Budget, Pinang Gazette, etc.). Full-text OCR search for `"ourang medan"` returns **3 hits, all from 2022** (Straits Times "Unsolved mysteries of South-east Asia" series). The word "ourang" alone gives 48 hits 1844–2022, **none between 1940 and 1949**, none relevant before 2022. Searches for "Ourang" in 1940–1949 alone: **"No records found"**.
2. **No ship named "Silver Star" appears in the Singapore/Malayan press in 1947–48.** `"silver star"` 1947–48 gives 278 hits, every one a racehorse (Singapore/Penang/Selangor race cards), the US Silver Star decoration, or similar; zero in a shipping context; `"s.s. silver star"`, `"silver star" steamer/freighter/vessel/tanker` = 0. `"City of Baltimore"` 1947–48 = 1 hit (a Damon Runyon story). Nor is there any "death ship", "ghost ship", "crew dead", "all officers dead", "I die"+SOS, or Dutch-freighter-with-dead-crew story in the Singapore press June 1947–March 1948.
3. **No November–December 1940 pickup of the AP "death ship" story was found** in NewspaperSG (Straits Times, SFP, Malaya Tribune, Morning Tribune, Pinang Gazette all present in the index for Nov 1940), in the Wellington *Evening Post* (the only 1940 title searchable through the DigitalNZ API), in *Pacific Islands Monthly* (Nov 1940–Feb 1941 issues, full OCR), or in the Dutch/Netherlands-Indies press (KB Delpher: `"ourang medan"` 1940–41 = 0).
4. **Trove could not be searched.** trove.nla.gov.au, nla.gov.au and catalogue.nla.gov.au are all behind an Anubis proof-of-work bot gate (article pages, search pages and the `/newspaper/rendition/...txt` OCR endpoint alike; a page fetch receives an explicit Anubis "Access Denied"). I did not attempt to defeat the gate or to use anyone's API key. ~20 web-search-engine probes (Bing and DuckDuckGo direct were junk/CAPTCHA) surfaced **no Trove article page containing "Ourang Medan"** (or "Orang Medan"/"Ourang Median") for any year, and no Nov–Dec 1940 "death ship"/"mystery S.O.S." Trove page. This is a *non-result*, not a negative: Trove must be searched with a key or a browser (see §7).
5. **Papers Past: negative, with a big caveat.** The website is behind an Imperva/Incapsula JS challenge (not circumvented). The DigitalNZ public API (no key needed) does search Papers Past OCR, but its index for 1940–45 is essentially the *Evening Post* (Wellington) plus the *Ellesmere Guardian*, and it contains **no records at all for 1946 onwards**. Evening Post 1940: `"ourang medan"`, `"orang medan"`, `ourang`, `"death ship"`, `"crew dead"`, `Medan ship`, `Trieste ship SOS`, `"shortwave" doctor ship` = 0; other 1940 hits are all genuine war-news items. Papers Past's other 1940 titles (Press, ODT, Auckland Star, NZ Herald, etc.) and everything after 1945 remain **unsearched**.
6. **Bonus (Netherlands-Indies press, reachable via the KB's public SRU endpoint):** the story's earliest 1948 appearance in the Dutch-language world found is **Leekster Courant, 24 Jan 1948** ("Mysterie, dat de zee nimmer zal prijsgeven"), credited to a reporter of *Elsevier's Weekblad* — i.e. *before* De locomotief (Semarang) 3 Feb 1948, which prints the same Elsevier text. In all Jan–Mar 1948 Dutch/Indies texts the incident is **mid-June 1947, c. 400 nautical miles SE of the Marshall Islands, Pacific** — the rescuing ship is unnamed ("wij"); **"Silver Star", "City of Baltimore" and the Strait of Malacca do not occur** in any of the ten OCR'd articles (grep-checked; OCR is imperfect). De locomotief 28 Feb 1948 makes the "sole survivor" a man landed on Taongi (Marshall Islands) and cared for by a Franciscan missionary; the headline OCR reads "De Enige Overlevende, Silvio Scherli, Vertelt zijn Verhaal" (the sole survivor, Silvio Scherli, tells his story); 13 Mar 1948 gives the cargo as "15.000 kisten en vaten zwavelzuur, nitro-glycerine in vloeibare vorm en nog andere chemicaliën" loaded in China, a passport of "Alfred Schutzwald uit Hamburg" issued in Shanghai, and closes with the editors' doubt versus "de verklaring van de auteur, Silvio Scherli, te Triest, die in zijn verzekering omtrent de authenticiteit volhardt". A 1955 Curaçao paper (Amigoe di Curaçao, 7 Jan 1955) summarises a *Panorama* magazine retelling in which the cargo is potassium cyanide (cyaankali) turning into prussic acid — still "ergens op de Stille Oceaan".

## 4. The Singapore press, June 1947–March 1948, and the "Silver Star"

- Nothing in NewspaperSG between 1 June 1947 and 31 March 1948 describes a Dutch (or any) ship found with its crew dead, a ship exploding under tow, an "I die" SOS, or an American ship "Silver Star"/"City of Baltimore" in the Strait. The index for this period is dense (Straits Times, Singapore Free Press, Malaya Tribune, Morning Tribune, Sunday Tribune, Straits Budget, Pinang Gazette are all present), and it *does* carry the genuine small dramas of the Strait (Hemland collision, Moebai SOS — §5), so silence here is meaningful: had an "Ourang Medan" incident occurred on Singapore's doorstep it would be here.
- **"Silver Star" shipping check:** 278 full-text hits 1947–48; title tally = race cards ("Today's Race Card" ×15, "TODAY'S CARD" ×9, Penang/Selangor/KL race programmes, "Straits Times Selections", etc.), US Silver Star decorations to RAF/RN officers, "News from the Mews". Not one hit is a shipping column, arrival/departure list, or ship report; `"s.s. silver star"` = 0. The only "silverstar"-type shipping item is Hoegh's *Silvercloud* (1947) and a Hoegh-Knutsen service (Nov 1948). Delpher (Indies Dutch press) also has 0 for "silver star" Jun 1947–Mar 1948.
- The 1948 retelling was **not** picked up by the Singapore press: 0 hits for Elsevier / De locomotief / Marshall Islands death-ship / "ourang" in 1948 (or any year before 2022).

## 5. Real incidents in Asian waters 1947–48 that could be a kernel (Singapore press)

None is a "dead crew found aboard" story; these are the closest genuine items:

1. **Sir Harvey Adamson, April 1947 (Andaman Sea).** Straits Times, 8 May 1947, p.1, "SHIP WRITTEN OFF": *"RANGOON, Wednesday. — The loss of the 1,030-ton ship Sir Harvey Adamson, which disappeared in the Andaman Sea off the Burma coast three weeks ago with 250 persons aboard, was written off today as another unsolved mystery of the sea.—A.P."* (transcribed from the clipping; the OCR abstract reads "1,050-ton"). URL: https://eresources.nlb.gov.sg/newspapers/digitised/article/straitstimes19470508-1.2.9. The only 1947 "unsolved mystery of the sea" in the region, and A.P.-distributed — but a vanishing, not a death ship.
2. **Unidentified ~50-ton steam craft collides with Swedish MV Hemland and vanishes, Strait of Malacca, 2.25 a.m. Friday 3 Sept 1948.** Malaya Tribune, 6 Sept 1948, p.2, "MYSTERY SHIP COLLIDES, VANISHES IN MALACCA ST." and Morning Tribune, 6 Sept 1948, p.1, "SHIP VANISHES AFTER CRASH — MYSTERY IN MALACCA STRAITS — SAILS OFF IN DARKNESS—NO SIGNALS (Tribune Shipping Reporter)". Full transcription not included in this folder; key lines: *"We saw that a steam-driven craft of about 50 tons, carrying no lights, had brushed against our side. She did not reply to our signals asking for her name … Naval, Customs and Police authorities have been alerted to search for the mystery ship."* Post-dates the Jan–Mar 1948 story, so not a source, but shows how a genuine Strait "mystery ship" was reported.
3. **Small Dutch freighter Moebia/Moebai, disabled and drifting off Sumatra, sends SOS (last week of Nov 1948); answered by the 7,651-ton Dutch freighter Molenkerk "40 miles from the northern entrance to the Straits of Malacca", towed to Penang; nine Dutchmen aboard, no casualties.** Morning Tribune, 1 Dec 1948, p.2, "Dutch Ship Feared Seizure, Sent SOS" (Capt. Zwanenburg interviewed); Straits Times 1 Dec 1948 p.7 and Straits Budget 2 Dec 1948 "SHIP TOWED OFF HOSTILE COAST".
4. **Chinese ship Pu To mined off Hong Kong, 36 dead** (Singapore Free Press 6 Oct 1947) — an explosion/casualty story, no mystery.
5. Later "death ship" of the region: Straits Times 15 Dec 1953 p.1, "Indonesians find a 'death ship'": *"JAKARTA, Mon. AN Indonesian patrol vessel has discovered a Philippines vessel with a crew of two emaciated men and three corpses drifting between the Celebes and the Philippines … The corpses were those of two women and a child who had died of starvation.—Reuter."*.
No poisoned-crew / chemical-cargo / drifting-junk-with-dead-crew story was found for 1947–48.

### 7.1 Leekster Courant (Leek, Netherlands), 24 Jan 1948 — "MYSTERIE, DAT DE ZEE NIMMER ZAL PRIJSGEVEN" (credited "aldus een verslaggever van 'Elsevier's Weekblad'")
URL: https://resolver.kb.nl/resolve?urn=MMRHCG04:235133008:mpeg21:a00006 (full text in `sources/reports/delpher_report.md`, B1). Key passages (OCR, verbatim):
- *"Wij voeren midden Juni 1947 ongeveer 400 zeemijlen Zuidoostelijk van de Marshall eilanden, toen wij van de 'Ourang Medan' op de 600 metergolf dit radiobericht ontvingen: 'S.O.S. from ship ourang medan stop pse ships with SW get urgent DH . medio° stop'."* [= "please ships with SW (short wave) get urgent … medico"]
- *"'SOS van de 'Ourang Medan' 20 gr. Westerbreedte 179 gr. Oosterlengte stop Wij drijven stop tweede officier dood op de brug stop kapitein en hoofdmachinist dood in kaartenkamer stop waarschijnlijk gehele bemanning overleden stop deel der bemanning …' Hier brak de uitzending plotseling af … Alleen twee woorden kwamen nog door: 'Ik sterf'."*
- Ship described as *"een vrachtschip van ruim 5000 ton … met één schoorsteen, veertig jaar oud en in die veertig jaar zeker wel tienmaal van naam en vlag en reder verwisseld. Tussen 1935 en 1937 had het … vier houten slaapdekken in de ruimen … als Chinees transportschip."*
- *"Overal lagen lijken. Er lag ook een dode hond. En het verschrikkelijkste was de krampachtige houding der doden, terwijl hun gezichten een afschuwelijke angst uitdrukten … De meeste doden waren Oosterlingen van verschillend ras"*; 22 dead; one lifeboat missing; *"'Alle man van boord! Brand in luik vier!'"*; three further explosions; the ship burns out and sinks; *"De Stille Oceaan is op dat punt enige duizenden meters diep."*

### 7.2 De locomotief (Semarang), 3 Feb, 28 Feb, 13 Mar 1948

## 9. Caveats and recommendations

1. **Trove must still be searched properly** (API key or a browser session): recommended queries `"Ourang Medan"`, `"Orang Medan"`, `Ourang Medan~` (fuzzy), `"death ship" AND "Solomon"` limited to 1940-11-01..1940-12-31, `"Silver Star" AND Malacca` 1948, and Pacific Islands Monthly/"Australian Women's Weekly" for retellings. Also check the Trove-hosted *Pacific Islands Monthly* index — my mirror sweep covers 1940–41 and 1947–60 only.
2. **Papers Past 1940** beyond the Evening Post (Press, Otago Daily Times, Auckland Star, NZ Herald, provincial dailies) is unsearched, as is everything 1946–1950; the DigitalNZ index simply lacks them. The Evening Post negative is nevertheless meaningful, as it carried the same overseas cable services (Press Association/Reuters/AAP, UP, "official wireless") as the other NZ dailies.
3. **NewspaperSG full OCR** is not exposed on the new site; my transcriptions come from reading clipping images. The 2022 Straits Times article is locked; it can be read at NLB or via the ST website/podcast ("The Unsolved Mysteries of South East Asia: The ghost ship in the Strait of Malacca", 2022).
4. The Dutch findings (Leekster Courant 24 Jan 1948 = Elsevier's Weekblad; Pacific/Marshall Islands setting; no Silver Star/Malacca in any Jan–Mar 1948 Dutch or Indies text; Scherli presented as survivor/author) are covered in `sources/reports/delpher_report.md` — flagged here because the KB SRU route is open and quick (`code/trove-sg-nz_sru.py`), and because they bear directly on when the Malacca/Silver Star details entered the story (not in Jan–Mar 1948 Dutch/Indies print, on this evidence; the Straits of Malacca version therefore seems to arrive with the May 1952 US Coast Guard *Proceedings* editorial or an intermediate US/UK 1948–52 retelling).
