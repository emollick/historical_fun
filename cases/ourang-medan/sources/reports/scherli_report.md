# Silvio Scherli and the Italian/Triestine side of the "Ourang Medan" story

File names are given in brackets below. Of these, `ilpiccolo_19401016_p3.pdf`, `ilpiccolo_19410205_p3.pdf`, `illustrazione_1940_49_n51.jpg` and `gu_19301004_scherli_decree.png` are in `images/pages/`, `ilpiccolo_solr_Ourang.json` is in `sources/search-logs/` and the helper script is `code/scherli_ilpiccolo_solr.py`; working copies of the other fetched texts, page images, crops and transcriptions are not included in this folder.

## 0. Short answers

1. **Silvio Scherli was a real person.** Silvio Scherl / Scherli (byline "Silvio Scherl-Scherli"), of Trieste; very probably the "Silvio Scherk fu Giuseppe, nato a Trieste il 29 aprile 1901" whose surname the Prefect of Trieste "reduced" to *Scherli* in 1930; a merchant-marine radio officer ("ufficiale marconista mercantile", civil register, Oct 1943; "marconista triestino", Dec 1950), travel/photo-journalist (1933) and self-described "free lance journalist" (1954); married Gabriella Zanini in Trieste on 11 Sept 1943; family address Largo Barriera Vecchia 16 (his 1954 letterhead; mother and brother listed there in the 1952 city directory); died in Trieste on 7 March 1985 aged 83 (death notice and civil register, *Il Piccolo* 9 March 1985).
2. **The story is his.** The earliest printing anywhere is his own signed feature "Il mistero dell'«Ourang-Medan»", *Le Ultime Notizie – Il Piccolo delle Ore Diciotto* (Trieste), 16 Oct 1940, p. III, dated to "la notte del 13 novembre 1939", ~200 miles SE of the Solomons, with the same two photographs later reused in 1948. Ten days later the paper boasted it had been "reproduced by all the newspapers of the Kingdom and will also be published in the United States and in other countries"; it reached Austrian papers (20/29 Oct 1940, citing "Piccolo di Trieste"), the Associated Press / British papers (21–22 Nov 1940, datelined Trieste), *L'Illustrazione Italiana* (8 Dec 1940), *7 jours* (France, 29 Dec 1940 and 7 Sept 1941), *China Mail* (27 Feb 1941). A sequel in *Il Piccolo delle Ore Diciotto* of 5 Feb 1941 invented a sole survivor found in Fiji.
3. **The 1948 Dutch serial is a translation/reworking of the 1940 article plus the 1941 sequel**, with the date moved to June 1947, the place to the Marshall Islands and the survivor to Taongi. The Dutch SOS "S.O.S. from ship ourang medan stop pse ships with SW get urgent DH medico stop" is the 1940 Italian "S.O.S. de Ourang-Medan pse SW DH medico urgent" with the same Italian gloss. **No Italian printing of the first-person account in 1947 was found**; the Il Piccolo group archive (which includes the *Giornale di Trieste* 1947–54 and the Allied *Giornale alleato* 1945–47) has no "Ourang" hit after 5 Feb 1941, and the first post-war Italian reappearance located by anyone is *L'Illustrazione del Popolo*, 31 Oct 1948 (per Paijmans), i.e. after the Dutch printings.
4. What De locomotief says about Scherli: it never states his profession; it calls him "de auteur" who "vertoeft thans te Triest" and "verzekert, voor de authenticiteit van dit verhaal in te staan", while a sub-headline wrongly calls him "De Enige Overlevende". The survivor is nameless ("Zijn ware naam mag ik niet vertellen. Hij was een Duitser"), a first mate signed on in Shanghai without papers, who died after five days on Taongi and was "buried beside the old palm in the cemetery behind the mission building"; the story reached the author through a letter of "a Franciscan missionary of the island Tcangi [Taongi], Marshall Islands". The only personal name is on the captain's passport: "Alfred Schutzwald uit Hamburg". Every checkable element fails (Taongi is uninhabited and has never had a mission; no US torpedo boat "W 716" existed in 1939; the "exclusive" 1940 photographs were re-used for an alleged 1947 event; hold numbers, dates, coordinates and casualty counts contradict each other across versions).
5. **The 1940 British/AP version ties directly to Trieste and to Scherli**: it appeared 5 weeks after his article, with the same particulars (SOS "send doctor – urgent" then "send a warship"; twelve bodies, three of them deck officers; SE of the Solomons; explosion, fire, sinking), and the Yorkshire Evening Post/AP copy was datelined Trieste (Hargraves 2015; Skeptoid 2020).

## 2. The Italian genesis of the story (1940–41)

### 2.1 *Le Ultime Notizie – Il Piccolo delle Ore Diciotto*, Wed 16 Oct 1940, p. III — "Il mistero dell'«Ourang-Medan»", signed SILVIO SCHERL-SCHERLI

Source: archivio.ilpiccolo.it (free OCR-searchable archive of the Il Piccolo group). Viewer URL pattern: `https://archivio.ilpiccolo.it/sfoglio/aviator/aviator.php?newspaper=TO00208776&edition=piccolo&issue=19401016&startpage=3`; page PDF `https://dshare-deploy.grupponem.it/archivio-storico/ilpiccolo/_deploy/TO00208776/19401016/piccolo/pdf/00003.pdf` [ilpiccolo_19401016_p3.pdf; full transcription made from the page image: transcription_ilpiccolo_1940-10-16.txt; OCR: ilpiccolo_ocr_TO00208776_19401016_p3.txt]. Two photographs: "…sul ponte di comando trovammo il corpo del secondo ufficiale…" (dead officer with binoculars) and "L'«Ourang-Medan» sta per inabissarsi" (ship keeling over) — the same two pictures reappear in *L'Illustrazione Italiana* 8 Dec 1940 and in *De locomotief* 3 Feb 1948 [img/locomotief_19480203_photo_officer.png, img/locomotief_19480203_photo_wreck.png, img/illustrazione_1940_49_n51.jpg].

Key passages (Italian, then translation):

> "La notte del 13 novembre 1939 a circa duecento miglia a sud-est del gruppo delle Salomone mentre si navigava verso il canale di Panama, intercettammo la seguente segnalazione: «S. O. S. de Ourang-Medan pse SW DH medico urgent», che tradotto in lingua chiara vuol dire: «S. O. S. del piroscafo Ourang-Medan, preghiamo navi con installazioni onde corte ottenerci urgente consulto medico». Già per se stessa la segnalazione era molto strana inquantochè il marconista dell'«Ourang-Medan» aveva fatto precedere la sigla «DH medico» dal segnale di soccorso «S. O. S.» …"
> — "On the night of 13 November 1939, about two hundred miles south-east of the Solomon group, while sailing towards the Panama Canal, we intercepted the following signal: 'S.O.S. de Ourang-Medan pse SW DH medico urgent', which in plain language means: 'S.O.S. from the steamer Ourang-Medan, we beg ships with short-wave installations to obtain urgent medical advice for us'. The signal was in itself very strange, because the Ourang-Medan's radio operator had put the distress signal S.O.S. before the code 'DH medico' …"

> "Le stazioni mediche di «Funkarzt Elbe Weser» della Germania, il centro medico di Roma, «S. Marie-de-la Mer Radio» (Francia) risposero quasi simultaneamente … Anche l'«Australian Broadcasting Company», sull'onda commerciale dei 600 metri ripeteva per radiofonia: Silenzio! Silenzio! S.O.S. dell'«Ourang-Medan» sui mari della Micronesia!"
> (Real radio-medical services are named — the German *Funkarzt* Elbe-Weser service, Rome's CIRM, the French Saintes-Maries-de-la-Mer coast station — the professional colouring of a radio officer. Note "mari della Micronesia" although the ship is placed SE of the Solomons, i.e. Melanesia; in 1948 the ship was moved to the Marshalls, which are in Micronesia.)

> "Dopo qualche minuto l'«Ourang-Medan», con l'apparecchio ausiliario, rispose: «Probabilmente secondo ufficiale morto, altri membri dell'equipaggio pure uccisi. Annullate consulto medico. S. O. S. urgente assistenza nave guerra. S. O. S. lat... long... punto stimato. Equipaggio ha...». E qui improvvisamente la comunicazione cessò del tutto dileguandosi con una serie di punti e di linee senza alcun significato ed inintelligibili."
> — "…'Probably second officer dead, other crew members also killed. Cancel medical advice. S.O.S. urgent assistance warship. S.O.S. lat… long… estimated position. Crew has…'. And here the communication suddenly ceased altogether, dissolving into a meaningless, unintelligible series of dots and dashes."

> "Le congetture … erano le più disparate: Ammutinamento? caso di pirateria? rivolta di forzati? (l'«Ourang-Medan» l'anno prima trasportava un carico di forzati dall'Inghilterra all'Australia). … Nel frattempo una torpediniera americana e precisamente la «W 716» comunicava che aveva dirottato e sperava raggiungere l'«Ourang-Medan» entro le 24 ore. … la torpediniera americana comunicava per radiofonia: «We are coming at full speed»."
> — "Mutiny? piracy? a convicts' revolt? (the year before, the Ourang-Medan had carried a cargo of convicts from England to Australia) … Meanwhile an American torpedo boat, namely the 'W 716', signalled that she had altered course and hoped to reach the Ourang-Medan within 24 hours … 'We are coming at full speed'."

> "Infatti verso le ore 14 avvistammo all'orizzonte una grossa nave, senza bandiera a poppa, leggermente inclinata a tribordo e con l'elica immota. … Qua e là cadaveri di marinai ingombravano la coperta. Notammo che non uno di codesti cadaveri portava i segni di ferita o comunque avesse gli abiti chiazzati di sangue. … Sul ponte di comando trovammo il corpo del secondo ufficiale. … Contammo in tutto dodici cadaveri dei quali tre ufficiali di coperta. E gli altri? Una nave come l'«Ourang-Medan» doveva avere almeno 40 persone di equipaggio. … Proprio in quel momento uno scoppio rintronò in una delle stive … Una colonna di fumo bluastra, come un enorme bengala, usciva dalla boccaporta numero due. … Il giorno dopo, all'alba, l'incendio … erasi spento. Ci avvicinammo, sotto vento, e potemmo assistere al suo lento ma inesorabile inabissamento."
> — "…a large ship, no flag at the stern, listing slightly to starboard, propeller still … Here and there sailors' corpses cluttered the deck. We noticed that not one of these corpses bore signs of wounds or had blood-stained clothes … On the bridge we found the body of the second officer … We counted twelve corpses in all, three of them deck officers. And the others? A ship like the Ourang-Medan must have had at least 40 crew … Just then an explosion thundered in one of the holds … A column of bluish smoke, like an enormous flare, came out of hatch number two … The next day at dawn the fire had burned out. We approached from leeward and watched her slow but inexorable sinking."

> "Del mistero dell'«Ourang-Medan» non restava che una collezione di fotografie, molte delle quali passate in esclusività alla Marina da guerra americana a cui incombe il non facile compito di risolvere l'enigma. SILVIO SCHERL-SCHERLI"
> — "Of the mystery of the Ourang-Medan nothing remained but a collection of photographs, many of which passed in exclusivity to the American Navy, on which falls the not-easy task of solving the enigma."

The narrator never names his own ship or its nationality; the rescuing vessel is simply "we" (an unnamed merchant ship whose master orders two boats away). The 1941 sequel calls the 1940 narrator "un ufficiale giuliano che fu spettatore al suo drammatico epilogo" ("an officer from Venezia Giulia who witnessed its dramatic ending").

### 2.2 The paper's own syndication note, 26 Oct 1940, p. III [ilpiccolo_ocr_TO00208776_19401026_p3.txt]

> "Pubblichiamo il secondo articolo di Silvio Scherl-Scherli sui Drammi del mare. Il primo, Il mistero dell'Ourang Medan, pubblicato il 16 corrente sulle Ultime Notizie, è stato riprodotto da tutti i giornali del Regno e sarà pubblicato pure negli Stati Uniti e sui giornali di altri Paesi."
> — "We publish the second article by Silvio Scherl-Scherli on the Dramas of the Sea. The first, The Mystery of the Ourang Medan, published on the 16th inst. in the Ultime Notizie, has been reproduced by all the newspapers of the Kingdom and will also be published in the United States and in the newspapers of other countries."

This is the paper announcing, in October 1940, that the story was being sold abroad — which is exactly what happened next (Austria, AP/UK, France).

### 2.3 Sequel: *Il Piccolo delle Ore Diciotto*, Wed 5 Feb 1941, p. III — "Luce sulla misteriosa fine del piroscafo «Ourang Medan» – Il ritrovamento di un superstite della tragica nave salvato dagli indigeni di un'isola della Malesia"

Unsigned, with a fake foreign dateline "VITI LEVU (Figi), 5" (a same-day dispatch from a British colony to a Trieste paper in February 1941, with no agency credit, is not credible). Page PDF `…/TO00208776/19410205/piccolo/pdf/00003.pdf` [ilpiccolo_19410205_p3.pdf; full transcription from the image: transcription_ilpiccolo_1941-02-05.txt]. Key points:

> "La misteriosa e tragica fine della nave australiana «Ourang Medan» (L'uomo Nero), che appassionò il pubblico nel racconto di un ufficiale giuliano che fu spettatore al suo drammatico epilogo, è stata svelata. … Come i lettori ricorderanno, il giorno 19 novembre 1939 la nave australiana «Ourang Medan» chiedeva per radio «S.O.S. urgente bisogno assistenza medica, secondo ufficiale ucciso...»."
> (Now 19 Nov 1939, not 13 Nov; the ship is "Australian" and its name is glossed "The Black Man"; the sub-title says the survivor was rescued on "an island of Malaysia" while the text says Fiji.)

> "Lo scorso mese di dicembre 1940 si presentò al Governatore di Viti Levu, isola del gruppo delle Figi, un indigeno della costa settentrionale dell'isola e gli raccontò che da oltre un anno la sua tribù ospita un uomo bianco che fu raccolto moribondo a bordo di una scialuppa che sulle fiancate portava il nome della nave «Ourang Medan»."
> — "Last December 1940 a native of the north coast presented himself to the Governor of Viti Levu, Fiji, and told him that for over a year his tribe had been sheltering a white man picked up dying in a lifeboat bearing the name Ourang Medan."

The survivor's tale: sailed from Singapore "con un carico di 2500 casse il cui contenuto non era precisato sulla polizza di carico o meglio era falsato" for a West-Australian port; "Il comandante era pure proprietario della nave"; crew of Malays and Indians; ordered on to Panama; bosun dies on "11 ottobre" opening a ventilator; "Il giorno 13 fu fatale": two stokers and two engineers die welding "il fondo della stiva n. 4"; "Centinaia e centinaia di grossi topi … uscivano a frotte da ogni dove"; the second officer, binoculars in his left hand, dies mid-sentence on the bridge; the engine-room bosun comes back from the chartroom shouting "tutti morti, tutti morti… fuggite"; two boats leave (11 men and 4 men); the narrator's three Malay companions die one after another; after two weeks water and biscuit run out, "Poi non ricordo più nulla". Finding of the Governor's inquiry: at Singapore the ship had loaded "una grande partita clandestina di spiriti di nitroglicerina nonchè una partita di cianuro di potassio solido e liquido e numerose casse di acido solforico"; the crates broke with the rolling, "l'alcool evaporò lasciando la nitroglicerina esplosiva"; the ship "anni prima era adibito al trasporto dei forzati". "Non si è mai saputo chi fosse il destinatario della merce."

Every ingredient of the 1948 second and third instalments (survivor in a lifeboat, companions dying one by one, rats swarming out, hold 4, sulphuric acid and nitroglycerine, alcohol evaporating to leave explosive nitroglycerine, the crooked owner-captain, false papers, the story told to an authority figure) is already here. The 1941 French re-sale in *7 jours* (7 Sept 1941, Gallica ark:/12148/bpt6k32533346, p. 9; ContentSearch snippets [gallica_cs_bpt6k32533346.xml, gallica_7jours_1941.xml]) adds a real name for verisimilitude: "Sir Harry Charles Luke, gouverneur anglais des îles Fidji" (Luke really was Governor of Fiji 1938–42) and the headline "LES MEMBRES DE L'ÉQUIPAGE SONT MORTS ASPHYXIÉS, UN PAR UN, ET LE BATEAU MAUDIT A ÉTÉ COULÉ PAR SA CARGAISON"; the first French printing, *7 jours* 29 Dec 1940 (ark:/12148/bpt6k32533027, p. 6) has "torpilleur américain n° 716", "200 milles au sud-est du groupe des îles Salomon", "Funkartz Elbe Weser", "Centro Medico Roma".

### 2.4 Other 1940–41 printings (all downstream of Trieste)

## 3. The 1948 Dutch serial — what it says about Scherli, and how it maps onto 1940/41

Sources read: Leekster courant 24 Jan 1948 (crediting "een verslaggever van Elsevier's Weekblad") [full text in sources/reports/delpher_report.md, B1]; De locomotief (Semarang) 3 Feb 1948 (`https://resolver.kb.nl/resolve?urn=ddd:010862872:mpeg21:a0050:ocr`, a0051), 28 Feb 1948 (ddd:010862918:mpeg21:a0033–a0036), 13 Mar 1948 (ddd:010862945:mpeg21:a0036–a0038, a0042) [delpher_ddd_*.xml; page-level ALTO/JP2 also fetched]. Elsevier's Weekblad itself (17 Jan, 14 Feb, 28 Feb 1948 per Paijmans notes 1–4) is not on Delpher.

Extracted statements about the author/source:
- Profession: **never stated**. 3 Feb: first-person narrator on the rescuing ship ("Wat hier vermeld wordt steunt op ons logboek"; boarding party "De kapitein, de eerste stuurman, de marconist, twee matrozen en ik"). 28 Feb sub-headline: "De Enige Overlevende, Silvio Scherli, Vertelt zijn Verhaal" — the paper's sub-editor took him for the sole survivor, which contradicts the text (the survivor dies). Editorial box, 28 Feb: "De auteur, Silvio Scherli, vertoeft thans te Triest. Hij verzekert, voor de authenticiteit van dit verhaal in te staan. Daar wij over generlei verdere informatie dan die, welke in deze vervolgartikelen vervat is, beschikken, moeten wij met de persoonlijke verzekering van de auteur volstaan. Red. Loc." ("The author, Silvio Scherli, is at present in Trieste. He assures us that he vouches for the authenticity of this story. As we have no information beyond what is contained in these follow-up articles, we must make do with the author's personal assurance.") 13 Mar: "De conclusie ligt voor de hand, dat het ganse verhaal een fantasie, een boeiende romance is. Hiertegenover staat de verklaring van de auteur, Silvio Scherli, te Triest, die in zijn verzekering omtrent de authenticiteit volhardt."
- How he obtained the survivor's story: through a letter from "een Franciscaner missionaris van het eiland Tcangi [Taongi], behorend tot de Marshall-eilanden … Die schrijft het volgende: «Vijf dagen heb ik in mijn eenzaam huis gastvrijheid verleend aan een schipbreukeling…»", received "enige maanden" after the sinking.
- Survivor: nameless; first mate ("Als eerste stuurman hield ik toezicht"), signed on at Shanghai by a captain "die zijn ware naam niet noemde … Misschien was de kapitein een Duitser"; "Ik was een van die duizenden zwervers … Zonder huis, zonder vrienden, zonder naam." Died on Taongi: "Zijn ware naam mag ik niet vertellen. Hij was een Duitser. Hij biechtte en ontving de Communie. Hij werd begraven bij de oude palm op het kerkhof achter het missie gebouw." Not Italy: the survivor died on the atoll.
- The only personal name: the captain's passport "afgegeven door de Chinese autoriteiten te Shanghai, met de naam Alfred Schutzwald uit Hamburg". Untraceable.
- Dates: "midden Juni 1947" (3 Feb / Leekster); cargo loaded in a Chinese river mouth "vlak na de oorlog"; disaster on "de vijftiende dag"; twelve days in the boat; then months before the missionary's letter.
- Radio text: "S.O.S. from ship ourang medan stop pse ships with SW get urgent DH medico stop" — the 1940 Italian "S.O.S. de Ourang-Medan pse SW DH medico urgent" (Italian "medico" kept), with the same gloss ("wij verzoeken alle schepen met kortegolfzenders … om directe medische hulp" = "preghiamo navi con installazioni onde corte ottenerci urgente consulto medico") and the same remark that an SOS preceding a medical request is anomalous; the 600-metre band; a 16-hour run to the position (1940: "entro 16 ore"; 1948: "binnen zestien uur"); no wounds, no blood; explosion in a hold; fire all night; sinking at dawn; "zijn geheim medenemend" = "portando con sè il segreto".

## 4. Checkable details and results

- **Taongi/Bokak Atoll**: "an uninhabited coral atoll in the Ratak Chain of the Marshall Islands" (Wikipedia "Bokak Atoll"); no fresh water, never a mission station (also Skeptoid #756). The survivor/missionary episode cannot have happened as told.
- **"W 716"**: Paijmans, FT Feb 2021, note 30: the US Coast Guard informed him "that no torpedo boat named W716 was operational in 1939, and that this particular type of coding was not even in use at the time".
- **Sir Harry Luke** (7 jours 1941 only): real Governor of Fiji 1938–42 (Wikipedia "Harry Luke") — a true name attached to an invented event; Il Piccolo 1941 only says "il Governatore di Viti Levu". No Fiji record of an Ourang Medan castaway is known (Trove/Papers Past: see sources/reports/trove-sg-nz_report.md; my Papers Past query returned nothing).
- **Photographs**: identical pictures serve for a Nov 1939 event (1940) and a June 1947 event (1948); in 1940 they were said to have gone "in esclusività alla Marina da guerra americana". Paijmans: "Trieste is a large port, so Scherli could easily have staged the photograph of the dead naval officer on board any ship."
- **Radio detail**: Funkarzt Elbe-Weser, CIRM Rome, Saintes-Maries-de-la-Mer Radio, 600 m/41 m bands — accurate professional colour consistent with his documented trade (ufficiale marconista).
- **Internal contradictions**: 13 vs 19 Nov 1939 vs October dates vs June 1947; hatch 2 vs hold 4; 12 vs 22 dead; 15 vs 7 men in boats; Fiji vs Taongi; "Malaysia" vs Fiji in the same 1941 page; "west latitude".
- **"Alfred Schutzwald"**: no trace anywhere (web, archive.org full text).
- **Export Trade 1959**: unrelated trade report (see §1).
- **Ship registers**: not my task, but note Skeptoid/Hargraves/Wikipedia: no *Ourang Medan* in Lloyd's Register; *Silver Star* had been renamed *Santa Cecilia* in 1945.

## 8. Assessment

1. Silvio Scherli existed: a Triestine merchant-navy radio officer (b. 1901, d. 1985) who wrote travel pieces with his own photographs from 1933, a series of "sea dramas" for the Trieste evening paper in 1940–41, and later freelance journalism. The evidence that he lived in Trieste in the 1940s is solid for 1940–41 (signed articles, editor's replies) and 1943 (marriage at San Giusto, civil register), and for the 1950s (city directory, letterhead, local reports); for 1947–48 specifically there is only De locomotief's "vertoeft thans te Triest".
2. The Ourang Medan story is his authored fiction of October 1940, presented as a first-person memoir of an (unnamed) rescue-ship officer, illustrated with two photographs whose "exclusive transfer to the US Navy" he claimed in print. He supplied a sequel with a sole survivor within four months (Feb 1941), and re-sold the pair — updated to June 1947, the Marshall Islands and Taongi — to Elsevier's Weekblad in January 1948, from which De locomotief, De West, Austrian papers, the American Weekly (Oct 1948), the Italian *Illustrazione del Popolo* (Oct 1948), the Brazilian *Revista da Semana* (1950), the USCG *Proceedings* (1952) and Otto Mielke (1954) descend. Every checkable specific is false or contradicted; the constant across all versions is the Italian-flavoured radio formula "SW … DH medico".
3. The 1940 British/AP version is not independent corroboration: it is the Trieste article as syndicated by AP (datelined Trieste per Hargraves and Skeptoid; five weeks after Il Piccolo's own note that the piece "will be published in the United States and in other countries"), with AP turning Scherli's unnamed rescue ship into "a ship of the British Merchant Marine".
4. Nothing links Scherli to any real ship in the Solomons in November 1939; his documented sea service (1935–38, 1950, 1954–55) is on Italian Atlantic liners, an Italian tramp steamer and an Esso tanker. No source names the ship he claimed to be serving on in 1939, and the 1941 sequel refers to him only as "un ufficiale giuliano".
5. Residual uncertainty: (a) the 1930 decree spells the surname "Scherk" (very probably the same man, given age and Trieste; not proven); (b) I could not see Elsevier's Weekblad, the Austrian 1940 reprints, the Yorkshire Evening Post dateline or the Ancestry crew lists myself — those are cited from Paijmans, Hargraves, Skeptoid and the 2024 blog.
