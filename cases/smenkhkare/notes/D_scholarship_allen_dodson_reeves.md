## 1. James P. Allen

### 1.1 Allen 2009, "The Amarna Succession" — read in full (a)

**Reconstruction (explicit sequence).**
1. Akhenaten reigns to Regnal Year 17 (two Year-17 dockets: one from the Royal Tomb burial complex; the other, Pendlebury CoA III pl. 95 no. 279, originally "Regnal Year 17: honey [...]", erased and relabelled "Regnal Year 1: w[ine ...]") (P5 with n. 14).
2. In Years 16-17 Akhenaten appoints a **female coregent, Ankh(et)kheperure Neferneferuaten**, whom Allen identifies as **Akhenaten's fourth daughter Neferneferuaten-tasherit ("Neferneferuaten Jr.")** — NOT Nefertiti and NOT Meritaten (P14-P15). She was also Akhenaten's wife ("effective for her husband") (P15).
3. Neferneferuaten reigns 3 years in total (highest date: Year 3, Pawah graffito TT 139), partly overlapping Akhenaten and partly as **sole ruler** after his death; the Amun cult in "the temple of Ankhkheperure in Thebes", the "Akhenaten-less" epithets (mr jtn, pA Hm Axt-jtn, HqA) and the Osirian epithet Axt n h(j).s belong to the sole-reign phase (P5, P15, n. 18).
4. **Smenkhkare Djeserkheperu follows Neferneferuaten** (i.e. order Akhenaten → Neferneferuaten → Smenkhkare), reigns "less than a year"; his only date, the Year 1 docket "from the house of Smenkhkare" (Pendlebury pl. 86 no. 35), "could come from the reign of Tutankhamun" (P5 with n. 16, P15-P16). He takes over Neferneferuaten's prenomen element Ankhkheperure and her chief queen Meritaten, and appropriates her burial equipment (canopic coffinettes etc.), i.e. "denied her a pharaonic burial" (P16 and n. 66).
5. Tutankhamun accedes 1-2 years after Akhenaten's death, aged 10-11, born in Akhenaten's Year 9-10 (P6, P16).
6. **KV55 body = Smenkhkare**, a son of Amenhotep III and Tiye and younger brother of Akhenaten; **Tutankhamun's father = Smenkhkare**; Thutmose IV = Tutankhamun's great-grandfather (jt jt jt.f on the surveying instrument) (P6-P7, P16-P17).
7. **Dahamunzu**: explicitly left open ("a question outside the parameters of the present article"), but Allen notes that if it was Neferneferuaten, her request for a Hittite prince ("to me he will be husband, but in Egypt he will be king") need not mean demotion — she could have intended a coregency like the one she had shared with Akhenaten; whereas Meritaten as coregent-then-queen would be an "improbable demotion" (n. 66).

**Evidence relied on, with page refs (preprint pages).**
- Titulary variants (P1-P2): Smenkhkare always (anx-xprw-ra)|(smnx-kA-ra Dsr-xprw)|; Neferneferuaten (anx-xprw-ra + epithet)|(nfr-nfrw-jtn + epithet)|, epithets "desired of Akhenaten"/"of Neferkheperure"/"of Waenre"; feminine forms anxt-xprw-ra, mrt, Axt n h(j).s "effective for her husband", anx.tj Dt, mAat xrw (n. 3 citing Allen GM 141 and Gabolde 1998, 147-62, 213-19).
- Association with Akhenaten: Smenkhkare only on the calcite jar Carter 405 (cartouches of Akhenaten + Smenkhkare, both erased; fig. 1 after Loeben's reconstruction); Neferneferuaten on the **box lid Carter 1k** (Akhenaten + Neferneferuaten + "Chief Queen Meritaten"; fig. 2 after Gardiner's hand copy) and on the "Coregency Stela" UC 410 + Cairo JE 64959 where her double cartouche is cut over a single cartouche of Nefertiti (P2, nn. 4-6). Allen rejects Gabolde's view that Neferneferuaten and Meritaten on box 1k are the same person: "a reference to two individuals remains the simplest and most transparent interpretation" (n. 6, citing Murnane OLZ 96).
- Coregency: juxtaposition of cartouches "not conclusive proof" (jar could be a memorial dedication) (P3); the Memphis "coregency relief" (Newberry JEA 14) is dismissed following Málek 1996 and Löhr 1975 — the smaller figure is probably a princess (P3, nn. 8-11); the stelae Berlin 17813 and 20716 show a male and a female king interacting as living persons, so Neferneferuaten's reign was "at least partly contemporary" with Akhenaten's (P3, n. 12, crediting Harris AO 35 for noting the junior king's sex).
- Sole reign indicators: the Pawah graffito's offerings to Amun in the "temple of Ankhkheperure in Thebes" ("perhaps her mortuary temple"); pectoral Carter 261p(1) with Nut/Onnophris; the "Akhenaten-less" epithets (P5, nn. 17-18).
- Length: max 3-4 years / min under 1 year between Akhenaten's death and Tutankhamun's accession; if Smenkhkare had also been coregent, Neferneferuaten's reign would have had to fall entirely within Akhenaten's, which Allen rejects (P5).
- KV55 (P6-P7): body male, 18-25 at death; coffin and canopics made for Akhenaten (Grimm 2001 — Allen abandons his own 1988 Kiya-coffin argument, n. 24), but the body cannot be Akhenaten's because Akhenaten fathered Meritaten by Year 4-5 and so cannot have died at 25 in Year 17 (n. 55 makes this explicit); hence the body is Smenkhkare's, the equipment appropriated. He notes the unexplained female arm-position (n. 21).
- Parentage of Tutankhamun (P7-P9): Hermopolis block "king's son of his body, his desired, Tutankhuaten" facing "[Ankhesenpa]aten" (fig. 5); zA can mean son/grandson/son-in-law but n Xt.f mry.f implies lineal descent; Amenhotep III excluded (no long coregency — Dorman on Kheruef); Aya's "his son" is not literal. **Key inference (P9): Akhenaten's appointment of a female coregent shows he had no son** — "Neferneferuaten's coregency therefore most likely indicates that Akhenaten was not the father, nor the grandfather, of Tutankhamun".
- The -tasherit granddaughters (P9-P14): Meritaten-tasherit and Ankhesenpaaten-tasherit are real (Hermopolis recuttings are "prima facie evidence", n. 34, against Gabolde's "phantom children"), fathered by Akhenaten on his own daughters; Room gamma nurse-and-child text restored as Meritaten's, not a grandchild's (P11-P12); chronology: Meritaten Jr. born Year 14-15, Meketaten died in childbirth Year 15, Ankhesenpaaten Jr. born Year 16-17 (P14). Consequently Ankhesenpaaten born no later than Year 3-4, and Neferneferuaten Jr., born "within a year of them", reached 13 in Year 16-17 — old enough to be coregent and prospective mother of an heir (P15).
- Identity of the coregent (P14-P15): Nefertiti's candidacy "was in the ascendant for a time until the publication of a shawabti of hers, evidence that she died as a queen, not a pharaoh" (Loeben 1986; n. 59 also notes Bovot's two-shawabti reading); Manetho's "his daughter Akenkherēs" points to a daughter; Meritaten excluded because she appears as Chief Queen beside both Neferneferuaten (box 1k) and Smenkhkare (Meryre II) — the latter would be an "unthinkable demotion"; the **nomen argument**: a pharaoh's nomen is always the pre-accession name, so the coregent bore the name Neferneferuaten before coronation, and "since it now seems clear that the coregent was not Nefertiti, she must have been the only other woman known by that name: Akhenaten's fourth daughter, Neferneferuaten Jr." (P14-P15; n. 62 on the absence of tA Srjt; n. 64: Meritaten as chief queen and "mistress" of the house in EA 11 "seems clear evidence that Nefertiti had already died").

**Counter-arguments Allen makes against rivals.**
- vs. Nefertiti-as-king (Harris/Samson): the shawabti (queenly titulary at death) and Manetho's "daughter"; Nefertiti probably dead before the coregency (nn. 59-60, 62, 64).
- vs. Meritaten-as-Neferneferuaten (Krauss, Gabolde): box 1k names Neferneferuaten and Chief Queen Meritaten as two persons; a coregent-pharaoh cannot be demoted to queen of Smenkhkare (P14, n. 61: Gabolde's "political expediency" is "unconvincing").
- vs. KV55 = Akhenaten (Grimm/Gabolde and the epigraphy of the coffin): age at death vs. fatherhood by Year 4 (P6-P7, n. 55).
- vs. Smenkhkare as coregent of Akhenaten before Neferneferuaten: the Merire II scene shows Smenkhkare "in place of Akhenaten", so any overlap would have been months only; and the Year 1 "house of Smenkhkare" docket may be Tutankhamun's (P5).
- vs. Gabolde's "phantom" granddaughters (n. 34, P10-P11).

**What would refute Allen 2009 (by his own logic).** Proof that Nefertiti was alive and still Chief Queen after Year 12 up to the end of the reign (his n. 62 concedes "the evidence is unclear") — precisely what the Dayr Abu Hinnis Year 16 graffito later supplied; or a demonstrable pre-accession identity of the coregent other than a woman named Neferneferuaten.

## 2. John R. Harris — origin of the Nefertiti-as-king thesis

### 2.1 Harris, "Nefertiti rediviva", AcOr 35 (1973) 5-13 — (a) read in full (OCR of the Oslo scan; page refs are the journal's)
- **Thesis and method.** Re-attribution of the two small unfinished Berlin stelae. (i) Pase stela **Berlin 17813** (pp. 5-7): "when first discussed it was presumed to depict Akhenaten and Nefertiti", but since Newberry (JEA 14, 1928) and Carter it had been accepted as Akhenaten and Smenkhkare. Harris's argument: the seven empty cartouches admit "only one logical reconstruction" — two pairs beside the Aten are the Aten's; the three larger cartouches above the food table can only be "a king and a queen — whereas two kings would require four" (p. 6, with parallels Cairo JE 44865 and Berlin 14145, n. 8). Iconography confirms: the right-hand figure (double crown) is slightly larger and wears a king's pectoral; the left-hand figure "has rather more obvious breasts, and extends an arm in what is conventionally a woman's embrace"; and the **neck criterion** ("first noted, apparently, by Schäfer ... cited most recently by Cooney"): convex neck with an angle = male, smoothly concave = female (p. 7, nn. 13-15). (ii) **Berlin 20716** (pp. 7-8): no cartouches, but the right-hand crowned person "has a quite clearly 'concave' neck, and is therefore a queen, in spite of the crown"; parallel to the Meryre II scene; "will surely be Nefertiti".
- **Consequence for the coregency** (pp. 8-9): "without the two stelae under discussion, the whole of the representational evidence for the co-regency [of Akhenaten and Nefernefruaten/Smenkhkare loses its] real significance, since none of the items remaining is independently valid"; the Hermopolis block 406-VIIA (two kingly pairs of legs) and the Ashmolean sealing 1921.1149 (two crowned figures, red and blue crowns, hekat sceptres) "could equally well refer to Akhenaten and Nefertiti" (nn. 21-22). "Effectively, therefore, an independent co-regency of Akhenaten and Nefernefruaten/Smenkhkare must rest on the juxtaposition of their cartouches (of which, as yet, there are only two certain examples, with two others open to doubt) and, at the least, there should now be a moratorium on all homosexual fantasies" (p. 9). **n. 23**: "If in fact Nefertiti was also Nefernefruaten/Smenkhkare (Harris, GM 4 (1973) 15-17), then the co-regency evidence is, of course, cumulative."
- **Nomen doctrine** (n. 5, p. 5): "Smenkhkare" is "misleading ... not only was it the latest (and final) variant of the nomen ... but, with accompanying variants of the prenomen, it is less common than forms of the earlier name Ankhkheprure mry X : Nefernefruaten mry Y". I.e. **one ruler, Nefernefruaten first, Smenkhkare last** (the "name-change" model).
- **Nefertiti's kingly attributes** (pp. 9-12): stands at the king's right in statuary; smites a captive; wears kingly headgear — the short round-bottomed wig with diadem, the multiple atef, the flat-topped cap with feathers/horns/disc, the close-fitting cap; the **blue crown** on a Hermopolis block (777-VIIIA) whose figure has feminine drapery and a female neck (p. 11, n. 38); the duplicate-outline scenes in the tombs of Meryre II, Huya and Panehesy imply "virtually equal status" and show her with the blue and the red crown (pp. 11-12).
- **Conclusion** (p. 13): the blue crown is the coronation crown, so "if it can be shown that Nefertiti assumed the blue crown (for which Hatshepsut offers an obvious precedent), it follows inevitably that she had attained to the status of kingship, and this may be further confirmed by the otherwise unexplained duplication of her cartouche, as though, like a king, she possessed a prenomen and nomen" (n. 51: Petrie TeA pl. 15 (82); a mould in the Ashmolean and "eighteen in the collection at University College, London" — information from **Mrs Samson**). "I have suggested already that Nefernefruaten-Nefertiti became Ankhkheprure Nefernefruaten" (n. 52 = GM 4). The "disgrace" of Nefertiti (Helck, Munro) is "even more difficult to allow" (p. 13).
- **NB on credit**: this 1973 paper does **not** mention the feminine *t* of Ankh*et*kheperure; its evidence is cartouche-counting and iconography. The feminine-*t* observation is attributed to Harris 1974 by Dodson 2009 (p. 34) and to Samson by Reeves 2015 (n. 31); Dodson 2020 (p. 128) loosely says "in 1973". Allen 2009 (n. 12) credits Harris 1973 with first noting the junior king's sex on the stelae.

### 2.2 Harris, "Akhenaten or Nefertiti?", AcOr 38 (1977) 5-10 — (a) read in full
- The Karnak "sexless" colossus Cairo JE 55938 "was made as an image of Nefertiti and not of Akhenaten" (p. 6): it bears only Aten cartouches; it is one of four colossi whose crown sits directly on the head, all four deliberately smashed and defaced, "as though a particular stigma attached to her, which was not shared by Akhenaten" (p. 7, cf. the Nefertiti talatat structure at Karnak, n. 17); concave neck (p. 7); the double uraeus is queenly, never worn by Akhenaten (pp. 8-9, n. 25); her twin sceptres, double crown and beard "are matched in the case of Hatshepsut, as is the 'Osirid' form" (p. 9). n. 30 (p. 9): "cf. Harris GM 4 (1973); AcOr 35 (1973); 36 (1974) 11-21. I feel that this point at least may fairly be emphasised, irrespective of whether one then is prepared to accept the identification with Nefernefruaten." Theological reading: Akhenaten-Shu / Nefertiti-Tefnut (p. 10).

### 2.3 Harris, "Nefernefruaten regnans", AcOr 36 (1974) 11-21 — (c) NOT obtainable
Not digitized with the rest of vol. 36 at journals.uio.no. What others report: a catalogue of the monuments of Ankhkheprure with figures (Allen 2009 n. 5 cites p. 13 (1a) for box 1k, n. 9 cites p. 17 (2a, 2d); Dietrich 2024 n. 17 cites p. 16 (c), 17 (fig. 2c) for the Memphis block); Dodson 2009 p. 34 says that "in the first of a series of papers" (1974) Harris "noted the existence of versions of the long prenomen that seemed to include the feminine t-ending", concluding that Neferneferuaten "(and thus Smenkhkare) was actually a woman, and none other than Nefertiti", who "after Akhenaten's death ... had further changed her name to Smenkhkare to mark her status as an independent ruler".

### 2.4 Harris, "Neferneferuaten", GM 4 (1973) 15-17 — (b) core passage quoted by Reeves 2001 pp. 170-171
Reeves quotes: "In the 13th year of the reign of Akhenaten (or at some time not far removed), queen Nefernefruaten-Nefertiti, having assumed the crown of kingship but not the full titulary, seems suddenly to dissolve from sight. At about the same time, there appears as co-regent an otherwise unknown person, bearing the nomen Nefernefruaten but with a different epithet, and having the given prenomen Ankhkheprure. Under the co-regency, the position of [great royal wife] devolves upon Meritaten, who continues in this capacity in association with Nefernefruaten alone, and when the latter adopts the name Smenkhkare — possibly as sole ruler.... Two kingly persons of similar name, and not clearly distinguishable in any respect, may thus be seen to succeed each other with no perceptible interval, though without any overlap. The case for a simple identification is sufficiently obvious ..." Reeves also reports (p. 170) that Harris resolved Gauthier's 1912 objection by showing that "the first and original name of the co-regent of Akhenaten was Nefernefruaten, and ... the adoption of the alternative nomen Smenkhkare was a subsequent modification".

## 4. Nicholas Reeves

### 4.1 Reeves, *Akhenaten, Egypt's False Prophet* (2001) — (a) chs 8-9 read in full; KV55 section (pp. 81-84) read
**Reconstruction.**
1. Nefertiti's status rises steadily; the Year 12 "durbar" scenes (Meryre II, Huya), with king and queen drawn in near-identical outline, "document an early stage in the formal process of the queen's promotion to the status of co-ruler" (p. 172); the Ankhetkheprure ring bezels were "perhaps prepared for the regnal year 12 coronation" (caption p. 171). Berlin 17813 (three cartouches = king + queen, Harris) and **Berlin 25574** (a fourth cartouche "squeezed in" later at a peculiar angle: "Three cartouches ... identify a king and his queen; four cartouches identify a pharaoh and his co-regent. Nefertiti, demonstrably, had become a king", p. 169).
2. Name sequence (p. 172): queen Nefertiti → queen Nefernefruaten-Nefertiti → co-regent Ankhkheprure+epithet Nefernefruaten+epithet → "co-regent, then ruler Ankhkheprure Smenkhkare". **One person.** "The identity of Ankhkheprure Smenkhkare ... the suggestion that Nefertiti might have further developed her name to 'Smenkhkare' and gone on to rule independently has met with fierce resistance. The reason? The body in ... KV55" — "the proverbial red herring of Amarna studies" (p. 173). Both names share the same great royal wife Meritaten (p. 173).
3. Pawah graffito: "Year 3" = the co-regent's own dating "which would equate to around Year 14 of Akhenaten himself" (caption p. 163); it shows a functioning Amun cult "within the mortuary temple of the heretic's co-ruler" and "a decidedly softer line" — power "now vested in the hands not of Akhenaten himself but of ... Ankhkheprure Nefernefruaten" (p. 164).
4. Akhenaten dies after the Year 17 vintage (p. 174); **Dahamunzu = Nefertiti** (following Harris's 1993 lecture: Vandersleyen's "the king's wife par excellence"; timing of Tutankhamun's death vs. the autumn letter; **EA 170** on the Amqa attack shows the Amarna archive still open, i.e. before Amarna's abandonment) (pp. 176-177); Zannanza murdered; "Nefertiti-Smenkhkare" then disappears, "most probably because of death ... in which natural causes played but little part" (p. 178). Caption p. 177: "Her hold on power evidently continued, as Ankhkheprure Smenkhkare, into the reign of Tutankhamun"; p. 180: power "retained by Nefertiti-Smenkhkare, with their reigns — like those of Hatshepsut and Tuthmosis III — running concurrently".
5. **KV55 = Akhenaten** (pp. 81-84): Weigall's archaeology "very sound"; Hussein & Harris (ICE 1988) age "mid-thirties"/"in excess of 35"; the coffin and canopics were Kiya's; reburial from Amarna temp. Tutankhamun of "mother and son", Tiye later removed by Ramesside workmen. Her burial equipment (second coffin, canopic coffinettes "all of which carried the portrait of their original owner") shows Nefertiti's burial "had clearly been an impressive one ... purely Osirian" (p. 178).
6. **Tutankhaten = son of Akhenaten by Kiya** ("most probably the child of this same woman", p. 179; p. 160: Kiya "probably giving birth to Tutankhaten — the king's only son"); the -tasherit girls = Akhenaten's children by Meritaten and Ankhesenpaaten (p. 160).
7. The shabti fragments of Nefertiti are "votive offerings prepared during the donor's life", "of no relevance whatsoever to her rank at death" (p. 170).
**Counter-arguments**: against KV55 = Smenkhkare ("not a scrap of archaeological or inscriptional evidence could be mustered from Davis's tomb to support Smenkhkare's association with any part of it", p. 83; circularity of the "elder brother" argument); against Nefertiti's disgrace/death (Kiya was the one erased, p. 170); against the two-person reading of the prenomen (2001 does not yet engage Allen 1988/1994 explicitly beyond p. 173).
**1990 position** (*Complete Tutankhamun*, opensource archive.org text, p. 24 area): "The appearance of a king Ankhkheprure Smenkhkare-djeserkhepru is documented towards the end of Akhenaten's reign, and it is tempting to see this ruler as the ultimate manifestation of the former great royal wife, Nefertiti ... Smenkhkare disappears after a brief independent reign" — so the Nefertiti = Smenkhkare idea is already there in 1990.

### 4.2 Reeves, "The Burial of Nefertiti?" (ARTP Occasional Paper 1, 2015) — (a) read (Factum Arte PDF; page refs = paper's pp. 1-16)
- Historical premise (pp. 2-5): "in an astonishing 80% or more of the tomb's core burial equipment (to include the large gilded shrines, sarcophagus, coffins, gold mask, and canopic equipment)" there is evidence of re-use; two groups — "a stray scattering of pieces seemingly once intended for the burial of Akhenaten himself" (Harris 1992) and "by far the larger proportion, items initially prepared for the use of Akhenaten's junior co-regent ... Ankhkheperure (+ epithet) Neferneferuaten (+ epithet)" (p. 4, nn. 24-27). Body shape (Carter 289b, 458), iconography (second coffin 254; coffinettes 266g) and inscriptions identify the co-regent as a woman, "most likely the great royal wife Neferneferuaten-Nefertiti in newly elevated guise" (p. 4, nn. 29-32). "This same lady's rise evidently continued, culminating in her appointment as sole pharaoh following Akhenaten's death and the adoption of a new and developed form of her semi-regal name – Ankhkheperure Smenkhkare-djeserkheperu" (p. 4).
- **n. 27 name-forms "in likely chronological order"**: (a) Ankhkheperure-beloved of Neferkheperure : Neferneferuaten-beloved of Waenre; (b) Ankhkheperure-beloved of Neferkheperure (or Waenre) : Neferneferuaten-she who is beneficial for her husband; (c) Ankhkheperure-beloved of the Aten : Neferneferuaten-the ruler; (d) Ankhkheperure : Meryaten ("lit. Beloved of the Aten [= Akhenaten]"). "The co-regent's sex is clearly established as female and, as the epithets imply, her status wholly dependent on Akhenaten."
- **n. 32 (position change)**: "I had originally dated this elevation as co-regent to around Year 12 of Akhenaten's reign; the recent discovery of a graffito dated Year 16, however, in which Nefertiti is referred to still as great royal wife, presumably indicates it was later (Van der Perre 2014)." Nefertiti "is the only candidate for whom a consistent progression in status may from the very start be discerned" (names Nefertiti → Neferneferuaten-Nefertiti; titles Hmt nsw wrt → Hmt nsw aAt, Reeves 1978). He characterises Allen 2009, Gabolde 2009 and von Falck 2012 as identifying the co-regent with **Meritaten** (sic — for Allen this is inaccurate: Allen 2009 argued for Neferneferuaten-tasherit) "notwithstanding that both Neferneferuaten and Meritaten are mentioned together, and as distinct entities" on Carter 001k.
- **n. 33**: "I do not subscribe to the view which posits the existence of two regal entities sharing the Ankhkheperure prenomen ... (Hornung 2006, 207) – not least because both 'individuals,' besides sharing virtually the same prenomen, are found associated with the same great royal wife, Meritaten"; the Golden Throne (Carter 091) shows "a clear sequence of inscriptional re-use from Akhenaten through Neferneferuaten to Smenkhkare and Tutankhaten/amun" (Reeves 2014 = BES 19).
- **n. 34**: Smenkhkare's reign "exceptionally short, being brought to a swift (and unhappy) end following her appeal to the king of the Hittites" (Dahamunzu = Nefertiti, citing Harris in Reeves 2001 and Miller 2007); Tutankhamun buried both Akhenaten and Smenkhkare; Nefertiti, "too senior to employ her existing and only 'semi-pharaonic' co-regent's funerary equipment", was "assigned Akhenaten's own burial furniture, suitably re-inscribed"; hence Akhenaten's "care-less interment ... within the Valley tomb of his mother, Tiye (KV 55)" in Kiya's hastily adapted coffin. **n. 9**: his opinion is "now modified to regard Akhenaten's presence within KV 55 as a primary interment rather than as a burial transferred from el-Amarna" (a change from 2001).
- n. 36: "The Theban mortuary temple of (pharaoh) Ankhkheperure is mentioned in the well-known Pere graffito". Her burial at Thebes "under the name of Smenkhkare, I believe is now virtually certain" (p. 5). Part Two: the Factum Arte scans of the west and north walls — putative doorway jambs beneath the plaster; KV62 as a queenly corridor tomb turning right; the north wall painted for Nefertiti and re-labelled (Ay's figure originally Tutankhamun's, Tutankhamun's originally Nefertiti's).
- n. 11: the four KV62 shrines are "a mixed set" — Akhenaten's (II?) and Neferneferuaten's (I, III, IV?); KV57 possibly begun for Akhenaten.

### 4.3 Reeves, "The Gold Mask of Ankhkheperure Neferneferuaten", JAEI 7:4 (2015) 77-79 — (a) read in full

## 5. Aidan Dodson

### 5.1 *Amarna Sunset* (2009) — (a) ch. 2 "The Waning Sun" (pp. 27-52) and ch. 5 "The Zananzash Affair" (pp. 89-93) read in full; ch. 1 end and ch. 3 opening read; Appendix 3 (Royal Names, pp. 170-171) is a table lost in OCR
**Reconstruction.**
1. Year 12 durbar; cluster of royal deaths (Meketaten, Tiye, two youngest girls) in Years 13/14, perhaps epidemic (pp. 25-26, 38).
2. **Smenkhkare = coregent from c. Year 13/14**, married to Meritaten: the TA2 (Meryre II) scene was sketched immediately after the durbar in the artists' working sequence "probably before the end of Year 12 or early in Year 13" (pp. 28-29); the jar Carter 405 (Loeben's reading Akhenaten + Smenkhkare) "highly suggestive of a joint rule" (p. 30); "Coronation Hall" bricks; Memphis block; gold daisies with Smenkhkare + Meritaten in KV62 (p. 31). Smenkhkare was **not** Akhenaten's son (too old for the KV55 body) but "would fit nicely as a younger brother of Akhenaten" and heir presumptive after Tutankhuaten (p. 38); he died "within a year or two" (p. 39).
3. **Neferneferuaten = Nefertiti**, appointed "as Smenkhkare's replacement" c. Year 14/15 (p. 42), initially perhaps without full titulary (Berlin 17813 with three cartouches: Akhenaten + blue-crowned Nefertiti; the UC 410 stela recut, pp. 41-42). Rationale: Akhenaten appointed "in succession two adults" as guarantors of the revolution and of the underage Tutankhuaten's accession (p. 38).
4. Akhenaten dies Year 17; the "Year 1 over Year 17" docket shows a new count began only then (p. 44); **TT139 Year 3 = Tutankhaten's Year 3**, Neferneferuaten "transitioned from being Akhenaten's coregent ... to being the coregent of King Nebkheperure Tutankhaten, sharing the new king's regnal years" (p. 44); Tell el-Borg jar handles of both (p. 45). The "Temple of Ankhkheperure" uses the simple prenomen → **Smenkhkare's** foundation, a possible Amun establishment during his coregency → persecution of Amun dated to Akhenaten's very last year(s) (pp. 43, 45-46). The graffito epithets may be "meryetaten" or "even 'meryetamun'" (p. 45).
5. Neferneferuaten's last glimpse is Year 3; her funerary equipment (coffinettes, trappings, bow, box, bracelets, pectoral; perhaps the sarcophagus) was reused for Tutankhamun, so she "certainly was not buried as a king" — "either died and was posthumously denied her kingly status, or was deposed" (pp. 49-51); the name-change to Tutankhamun follows her disappearance (p. 51). TA29 (45 m) probably her tomb, TA27 Tutankhaten's (p. 49).
6. **KV55 = Smenkhkare**, in Kiya's coffin (pp. 39-41): body "in his twenties", too young for Akhenaten; the traditional-text middle coffin of Tutankhamun was made for Smenkhkare (glass inlay like KV55; square face; sunk cartouche interiors; resemblance to the 1966 facial reconstruction) but rejected by Akhenaten as religiously incorrect (pp. 40-41). The -tasherit girls = children of Smenkhkare and Meritaten (p. 39); "Meketaten's baby seems likely not to have existed" (p. 39).
7. **Dahamunzu = Ankhesenamun**, Nipkhururiya = Tutankhamun (pp. 89-93): "emendation should always be a last resort; and in any case, on the reconstruction already put forward in this book, Nefertiti was a king by the time Akhenaten died, with a son to place on the throne, while Smenkhkare had died some time before Akhenaten" (p. 89). Tutankhamun's parents: Akhenaten and (probably) Nefertiti ("Nefertiti, as Great Wife and probably mother of the heir to the throne", p. 38) — published before the Feb. 2010 DNA study.
**Counter-arguments** (pp. 34-37): vs. Harris/Samson single-person model — the Meryre II scene and Memphis block show a married, male Smenkhkare; Krauss's Meritaten-as-Ankhetkheperure "middle way"; **Allen 1988** cut the knot: no extended prenomen with the nomen Smenkhkare and no simple prenomen with Neferneferuaten; **Gabolde 1998** read Axt n hi.s, "confirmed beyond any doubt in 2004 by Allen and Gabolde's exhaustive reassessments of the palimpsest inscriptions" on the coffinettes; vs. **Meritaten** = Neferneferuaten: box 1k "requires some rather tortured logic"; vs. **Neferneferuaten-tasherit** (Allen): "at most, around ten years old" when the king appears, with two elder sisters and Tutankhuaten alive, and no way to have earned "effective for her husband" (pp. 36-37); vs. the **shabti**: pre-made equipment "strayed from some palace storeroom" (p. 37); vs. Kiya (p. 36).

### 5.3 *Nefertiti, Queen and Pharaoh of Egypt* (2020) — (a) Introduction, chs 3-4 (pp. 63-105) and pp. 128-129 read in full
**Reconstruction (2020).** Smenkhkare coregent c. (p. 138 area, on parentage): Allen 2016: 10-11 makes Tutankhuaten's father **Smenkhkare "by an otherwise unknown sister"**.

## 10. Addendum: verified quotations

### 10.1 Dietrich 2024 on Reeves 2023 vs Dodson 2020b (read in full, (a))
Dietrich, CRE 2023 (Archaeopress 2024), p. 105 n. 18 (continuation of the note on the order of the two Ankhkheperure rulers): "Arguments for a sequence Smenkhkare - Neferneferuaten based on the dipinto in the tomb of Pairy (TT 139) as presented by e.g. Dodson (2020b) have been convincingly rejected by a corrected reading by Reeves (2023). Interestingly, for the one ruler model, the order of names is also debated (see n. 15)." Immediately before this she notes (same note) that the arguments for Neferneferuaten preceding Smenkhkare include that Neferneferuaten "is connected to both Amarnian and 'orthodox' theology, whereas Smenkhkare is only known in connection to the Amarna religion."
Her conclusion, p. 105 (main text): the Hermopolis talatat assemblage 406/VII+777/VIII+826/VIII depicts "coregents – one male and one female – and by the caption, the female one is Neferneferuaten. She is likely to be also the female coregent in the scenes with unidentified coregents (Berlin ÄM 17813 and Berlin ÄM 20716) and the male coregent is likely to be Akhenaten. This leaves the coregency of Akhenaten and Neferneferuaten currently as the only Amarnian coregency with substantial and convincing evidence." She also states (p. 104-105) that there is "no depiction of Amenhotep III and Akhenaten or Akhenaten and Smenkhkare matching the criteria established for pictorial evidence of coregents."
Implication: the *only* witness available to me for the content of Reeves 2023 is this sentence; it establishes (i) that Reeves 2023 concerns the reading of the TT139 graffito's "pr/Hwt Ankhkheperure" phrase, (ii) that Dietrich (an independent chronologist) regards it as refuting Dodson's use of the graffito to place Smenkhkare *before* Neferneferuaten (Dodson's 2020 model: Smenkhkare first, then Neferneferuaten; his TT139 argument is that the "temple of Ankhkheperure" with the plain prenomen is Smenkhkare's Theban Amun foundation, which Reeves's title declares a "phantom", i.e. non-existent). What exactly Reeves re-reads remains (c).

### 10.2 Dodson 2020, Preface (pp. ix-xi), read in full (a) — his own account of his change of position and of the 2018 revision
- "the past few years have revealed new data—or new ways to look at old data—that make even quite recent works obsolescent regarding some aspects of Nefertiti's career. This is especially true regarding its end, where the 2012 discovery of a single graffito showing that she was alive and still functioning as King's Great Wife a year before her husband's death set to naught a range of earlier theories."
- "the very title of this book indicates the way that the emerging data have fundamentally altered the author's own views on Nefertiti's career: my very first published paper, back in 1981, was an explicit denial of the possibility of her ascent to the status of pharaoh, an idea that I now unreservedly embrace!" (The 1981 paper is his JEA 67 comment on Samson; see §3.)
- "Much of the first half of this book builds on my wider studies of the second half of the Eighteenth Dynasty, Amarna Sunset, published in 2009 (with an extensively revised edition in 2018), and Amarna Sunrise, which appeared in 2014." — so the 2018 *Amarna Sunset* is described by its author as "extensively revised"; its content is otherwise still (c) for me, and §5.2's inference that the 2018 model equals the 2020 model rests on this sentence plus the identical sequence in Dodson 2020 ch. 3-4.
- On the 2010 DNA studies: "it is quite clear that only 'preferred' conclusions were provided in the original publication, rather than the full range of options for the interpretation of the raw DNA data. However, some of the ignored possibilities have now been worked through, indicating that there are significantly different options available for the identification of both the owners of certain human remains and their relationships—all assuming, of course, that the DNA results are, in fact, 'real'!" and "I have had no alternative but to consider what the apparent DNA seems to be telling us, and to produce a scheme not at variance with this. Yet ... I have tried to ensure throughout that my hypotheses on such matters are moderated by reference to the monumental sources, so that no fundamental conclusions rest solely on the apparent implications of alleged DNA determinations." This is the methodological basis for his KV55 = Smenkhkare (brother of Akhenaten) identification while still making Tutankhamun the son of Akhenaten and Nefertiti (§5.3; cross-refer to dossier B on the DNA).
