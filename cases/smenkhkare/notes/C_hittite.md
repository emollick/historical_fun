## 2. The Deeds of Suppiluliuma (CTH 40), Fragment 28 = the "Seventh Tablet" (KBo 5.6 + duplicates)

### 2.1 Manuscripts, find-spots, date of the copies
- Košak, Konkordanz (a): KBo 5.6 = Bo 2003, CTH 40.IV.1.A, Temple I area (Siegelová 2015: 68 — presumably from the Great Temple), script "sjh." (späthjunghethitisch, i.e. a late-13th-century copy, not a Mursili-period original); KBo 14.12 (293/b + 284/c + 301/c + 346/c + 555/c ... = E3), Büyükkale Building A rooms 4-5, "jh."; KBo 14.11 (700/b + 464/c = E1) and KUB 34.25 (199/a = E2), Büyükkale A room 4, "jh."; KUB 31.25 = CTH 40.IV.2 (its attribution to the Deeds questioned by Groddek, GM 218 (2008) 41 n. 27, defended by Miller, AfO 53 (2015) 172 n. 5). Editions listed: Güterbock, JCS 10 (1956) 90-98 (Fragment 28) and 107-108 (Fragments 29-34); Del Monte, L'opera storiografica di Mursili II re di Hattusa I: Le gesta di Suppiluliuma (Pisa 2008/2009) 86-90 (IV.1); Klinger, TUAT NF 2 (2005) 147-150; Theis, AOAT 390/1 (2011) 301-331.
- All extant copies are therefore 13th-century library copies of a composition by Mursili II (the narrator says "my father" throughout; colophon E3 iv: "[x-th table]t of the Manly Deeds (LÚ-natar) of Suppiluliuma"). The colophon of A reads (Güterbock, JCS 10, 96 (a)): A iv 16-18 `DUB.7.KAM [NU] TIL / A-NA ṬUP-PÍ [U]D.KA.BAR / na-a-wi a-ni-ia-an` — "Seventh tablet, (text) not complete. Not yet made into a bronze tablet." Güterbock (1956: 47, 90) noted that the copies had tablets of different lengths, so "seventh" is the count of copy A only (a long-columned duplicate might be tablet 4 in its series; Wilhelm-Boese 1987 place the episode at about col. i of tablet 6 of the long-columned series — Miller 2007: 278 (a)). Large parts of cols. i-iii of A are left blank, which Güterbock took to mean that the Vorlage was broken (Miller 2007: 278).

### 2.2 Narrative position in the Deeds' own structure (Güterbock's translation (a), JCS 10, 90-98)
- A i 1-50: Kaška campaigns of "my father" — building of towns (Athulissa, Tuhupurpuna, Almina), a plague breaks out in the army at Almina, Hittite victory, conquest of Kasula (1000 deportees) and Tumanna; "Thereafter he came back to Hattusa to spend the winter. But when he had celebrated the year festival, he went into the country of Istahara ... and when he reestablished Istahara, he came back to Hattusa to spend the winter" (i 40-50). So col. i alone contains two winters in Anatolia.
- A ii 1-20: Hurrian front. Tribal troops attack "my brother" (Telipinu the Priest, Suppiluliuma's son, installed in Syria); Arziya and Carchemish make peace with him but the city of Carchemish itself does not; Telipinu leaves 600 men and chariots under Lupakki "commander of ten of the army" in Murmuriga and goes to Hattusa; "my father was in Uda performing festivals". The Hurrians (with Takuhli the amumikuni) surround Murmuriga.
- A ii 21-25: "To the country of Kinza, which my father had conquered, troops and chariots of Egypt came and attacked the country of Kinza." Word reaches Suppiluliuma that Murmuriga is surrounded.
- A ii 26-E2: Suppiluliuma mobilises, reviews troops at Talpa in Tegarama, sends his son Arnuwanda and Zita the chief of the bodyguard ahead; they defeat the enemy; Suppiluliuma comes down, does not find the Hurrian army, "went down to the town of Carchemish and surrounded it" (with boats on the river, E2).
- A iii 1-17: the Amka raid, the death of the Egyptian king, the queen's first letter (text below).
- A iii 18-25: council of the Great Ones; Hattusa-ziti the chamberlain sent to Egypt.
- A iii 26-43: "until Hattusa-ziti came back from Egypt, my father finally conquered the city of Carchemish. He had besieged it for seven days, and on the eighth day he fought a battle against it for one day and [took] it in a terrific battle on the eighth day, in [one] day" (iii 26-31); piety towards the upper citadel (Kubaba and dKAL), plunder of the lower town, 3,330 deportees to the palace; installation of his son Sarri-Kusuh (= Piyassili) as king of Carchemish (E3 iii 15-20); "he [went] back into the land of Hatti and spe[nt] the winter in the land of Hatti" (E3 iii 21-23).
- E3 iii 24 / A iii 44 - iv 15: "But when it became spring, Hattusa-ziti [came back] from Egypt, and the messenger of Egypt, Lord Hani, came with him"; the queen's second letter; "since my father was kindhearted, he complied with the word of the woman and concerned himself with the matter of a son" (iv 13-15). End of tablet A.
- E3 iv 1-39 (after a gap of 6-12 lines): Suppiluliuma's reproach to Hani; Hani's reply; the tablet of the Kurustama treaty is fetched and read aloud; Suppiluliuma's declaration of renewed friendship. End of tablet E3.
- Fragments 29-31 (KUB 32.25?; KUB 23.7 = Bo 6519 [now assigned to the Kurustama treaty duplicates, CTH 134, per Konkordanz]; KUB 19.4 = Bo 4543 + 9181 (+ KUB 23.8 = Bo 6552, + Bo 7744; joins by Miller 2007/2008, ZA 98, 118f. no. 31)): treaty language "Hatti and Egypt shall be [friendly] with each other to the end of days"; then the killing of Zannanza (text below).

Which war/year: the Deeds themselves give no year-count here. The episode belongs to the Syrian ("Hurrian") war that ends with the fall of Carchemish; the later summary by Hattusili III (KUB 19.9 i, CTH 83) that Suppiluliuma fought "20 years" in Anatolia and "6 years" in the Hurrian land is the source of the conventional "six-year Hurrian war", and the siege of Carchemish is generally placed in its last year (Güterbock 1956; Kitchen 1962: 3ff.; Bryce 1990: 98, 100 — all (c) via Miller 2007: 282-283 n. 131, (a); Miller and Parker 2002: 53ff. read the 20 + 6 as totals of years spent campaigning, not consecutive blocks, giving a total reign of c. 26 years). Within Fragment 28 the sequence is internally dated only by seasons: the raid on Amka and the Egyptian king's death fall in the same campaigning season as the siege (late summer/autumn), the first letter arrives during the siege, one winter passes, and Hani arrives in spring (Güterbock 1956: 120-121 (a): "a winter passed between the first letter of the Egyptian queen, which Suppiluliuma received during the siege of Carchemish, and the arrival in Hattusa of Hattusaziti and Hani. The reading of the Kurustama treaty, accordingly, takes place in the Hittite capital"). Zannanza was therefore sent in the following spring/summer at the earliest, i.e. some 6-9 months after the king's death.

### 3.2 §1 (kola 1-14): the length of the plague; deaths of Suppiluliuma and Arnuwanda II — Rieken's German (a), my English
"[Storm]god of Hatti, my lord, [and gods of Hatti,] my lords, Mur[sili the king,] your servant, sent me: Go and speak to the Stormgod of Hatti ... thus: This is what you have done: you let the plague into the interior of the land of Hatti, and the land of Hatti was very heavily oppressed by the plague. **There was dying under my father (and) my brother** (kolon 7: 'Es wurde unter meinem Vater (und) meinem Bruder gestorben'). The plague is not removed from the land of Hatti ..." — Singer's rendering (as cited by Miller 2007: 283 n. 132 and by van den Hout 1994: 85ff., who takes the 20 years literally): "for twenty years now people have been dying in Hatti" (b). The First Plague Prayer (CTH 378.1 = Singer no. 12, KBo 54.6+ etc.) adds that Suppiluliuma had murdered Tudhaliya the Younger and that "my father ... my brother Arnuwanda" died — (b), via the intro page and Singer/Miller citations; the Ten-Year Annals (CTH 61.I, KBo 3.4 i 1ff.) open with the statement that Arnuwanda died shortly after Suppiluliuma and Mursili came to the throne "still a child" (c, standard; Goetze 1933; Beckman COS 2.16).

Singer's translation of A i 16-23 (quoted verbatim by Miller 2007: 268 (a)): "Since the men of Hatti and the men of Egypt were bound by the oath of the storm-god of Hatti, and the men of Hatti proceeded to get the upper hand, the men of Hatti thereby suddenly transgressed the oath of the gods. My father sent infantry and chariotry, and they attacked the borderland of Egypt, the land of Amqu. And again he sent, and again they attacked. When the men of Egypt became afraid they came and asked my father outright for his son for kingship. But when my father gave them his son, as they led him off, they murdered him. My father was appalled and he went to Egyptian territory, attacked the Egyptians, and destroyed the Egyptian infantry and chariotry." Miller 2007: 268 n. 72 (a): the "and again he sent, and again they attacked" clause is found only in A; in A the first part is written on the edge and the second over an erasure — possibly a scribal slip or an abandoned attempt to detail a multi-pronged attack.

Bearing: (i) the prayer treats the Amka attack(s), the request for a son, the murder, and the Egyptian counter-campaign as one continuous chain; the plague enters Hatti with the prisoners of the counter-campaign, which the Deeds (Fragment 36, Arnuwanda "went ahead to Egypt") and CTH 154 place immediately after Zannanza's death. (ii) It presents the attack on Amka as a breach of a sworn Hittite-Egyptian treaty (the Kurustama tablet; CTH 134 = KBo 8.37 + KBo 53.250, dupl. KUB 23.7, KUB 40.28, Bo 6943; ed. Singer 2004, GsForrer 591-607; dated by Klinger-Neu 1990 and Simon 2007 to the Middle Hittite period, i.e. before Suppiluliuma — Konkordanz (a), Singer/Sürenhagen (c)); the Deeds (E3 iv 26-34) likewise have Suppiluliuma read out the same tablet. Krauss 1978: 36-40 (c, via Miller 2007: 270) used this to argue that the Amka attack of the Deeds/prayer must be the first breach of peace and therefore precede the Amka attack attested in the Amarna letters — hence Nibhururiya cannot be Tutankhamun; Murnane 1983: 279 objected; Miller 2007: 270-271 finds the point "suggestive and not to be discounted" but not watertight, adding that the Amarna letters show Hittite-Egyptian relations already at "DEFCON 2" for years before Akhenaten's death, whichever king Nibhururiya is. (iii) The "20 years" plus the prayer's own date (Mursili's later years) and the prisoners' arrival (the year after Nibhururiya's death) give a rough arithmetic: Nibhururiya's death ≈ 20 years before the Second Prayer; if the prayer is c. Mursili year 18-20, then Nibhururiya died c. Mursili year -2 to 0, i.e. about the last years of Suppiluliuma — consistent with both Suppiluliuma's death "shortly after" (Bryce; van den Hout 1994 literal) and with Miller's ca. 4-6 years (Miller 2007: 273, 284). The number cannot discriminate between Akhenaten and Tutankhamun by itself.

Lupakki appears in the Deeds (A ii 11, iii 2) and in the so-called "Fifth" Plague Prayer / KUB 31.121+121a (CTH 379; Singer 2002 no. 13-14) as well as in EA 170: 14-15 (Lu-pa-ak-ku); Hani in the Deeds (A iii 44 etc.) and in EA 161, 162, 369 (Moran 1992: 381) — coincidences used by Parker 2002 and Miller 2007: 269 for a single Amqu episode (a/b).

Note on CTH 379/72: Košak's Konkordanz (a) now files Miller's KUB 19.15 + KBo 50.24 (+ Bo 2442, 359/v, 707/v; joins Miller 2004-2015, following Singer 2013: 198 n. 122) under CTH 72 "Report on the dispute with Egypt in Syria" (Miller, hethiter.net/:CTH 72, online edition in English) with the remark "also CTH 379", i.e. Singer's no. 14 is (part of) the same document. Literature list there: Sürenhagen 1985; Singer 2002: 66-69; Groddek GM 215 (2007) 95-107; Miller AoF 34 (2007); 6. IKH (2008) 533-554; Simon AoF 36 (2009) 340-348; Wilhelm WO 39 (2009) 108-116; Lebrun-Degrève RANT 5 (2008) 127-129.

## 5. The Amarna letters

### 5.1 EA 9 (Burna-Buriaš II of Babylon to "Nipḫurrireya")
Knudtzon 1915: 88 (a, archive.org OCR; letter 9 = BM 29785 = BB 2, W 7), lines 1-4:
```
1 a-na ni-ip-ḫu-ur-ri-ri-ia šàr māt mi-iṣ-ri-i[ki]
2 ki-bí-m[a]
3 um-ma bur-ra-bu-ri-ia-aš šàr māt ka-ra-d[u-n]i-ia-aš
4 aḫu-ka-ma a-na ia-a-ši šu-ul-mu
```
"To Nipḫurrireya, king of Egypt, say: thus Burra-Buriyaš, king of Karaduniaš, your brother: for me all is well ..." (Moran 1992: 18 "Nibhurrereya"; Rainey 2015 — (c)). The letter warns the pharaoh not to receive the Assyrians, "my vassals" (ll. 31-35), and complains about gifts (c, Moran). It is the only Amarna letter whose addressee's name begins with Nib-/Nip- + ḫurrireya, and since Edel 1948 and Kühne 1973: 73 it has usually been assigned to Tutankhamun (Nb-ḫprw-Rʿ), on the grounds that (a) nib- renders nb "lord/all" as in Nib-mu(w)areya = Nb-mꜣʿt-Rʿ (Amenhotep III), whereas every certain writing of Akhenaten's Nfr-ḫprw-Rʿ begins nap-/nab- (Naphururiya etc.); (b) Tutankhamun resided at Akhetaten in his first year, so one late letter could have been left there. Moran 1992: xxxiv-xxxix (c) accepted "no later than the first year or so of Tutankhamun". Miller 2007: 265-267 (a) argues the reverse: (1) Tutankhamun left Akhetaten within his first year (Krauss 1997a: 248: after the wine harvest of year 1); a Babylonian reply to a new king within that window is "rather speedy"; (2) EA 9 contains no congratulation on an accession; (3) no other Amarna letter can be confidently assigned to a successor of Akhenaten (the EA 27 docket must be read "year 2", not "12"; EA 16 cannot be to Ay; EA 210 probably not to a king); (4) a current letter would have moved to Memphis with the court; (5) Burna-Buriaš's attempt to block Egyptian-Assyrian relations fits the middle/late reign of Akhenaten (cf. EA 15-16 of Aššur-uballiṭ), not Tutankhamun's; (6) a letter to a c. 6-year-old king should show some sign of it. Miller 2017: 99 n. 9 (a) adds a Babylonian argument: Burna-Buriaš's own dynastic marriage with Assyria (grandfather of Kara-ḫardaš/Kara-KIN-daš) means EA 9 must precede about the midpoint of his 27-year reign, "if EA 9 had been intended for Tutankhamun it would have been written by Burna-Buriaš II in the waning days of his rule". If EA 9 is to Akhenaten, then "Nipḫurrireya" is a second cuneiform writing of Nfr-ḫprw-Rʿ with an /i/ vowel, and the Hittite Nibḫururiya loses its diagnostic force. Hess 1993: 116 (c) would add EA 210 (Ni-í]p-ḫu-ri-[ia) as a third.

### 5.2 EA 41 (Suppiluliuma to "Ḫuriya")
Knudtzon 1915: 303 (a, OCR), letter 41 (Cairo 4747, WA 18, W 35), lines 1-3: `[um-m]a mšu-ub-bi-[l]u-li-u-ma šar[r]u r[abû] [šar māt Ḫa]tti a-na mḫu-u-ri-i-a [šar māt Mi-i]ṣ-ri-iki aḫi-ia ki-bí-[m]a` — "Thus Šuppiluliuma, Great King, [king of Ḫa]tti, to Ḫuriya, [king of Eg]ypt, my brother, say." The body refers to "the messengers I sent to your father", "what your father said/asked (about troops?) between us", "just as your father and I were friendly". Identification of Ḫuriya: (a) Akhenaten (Na<pḫ>uriya defectively written): Kühne 1973; Moran 1992: 114 n. 1; Miller 2007: 279-282 (a) — "father" in the body (not the formulaic address) means father; the friendly relations with the addressee's father fit the last years of Amenhotep III, not the end of Akhenaten's reign; no Amarna letter can be shown to be to a successor of Akhenaten. (b) Smenkhkare (Ḫuriya = <Ana>ḫuriya, ʿnḫ-ḫprw-Rʿ): Wilhelm-Boese 1987: 97f. (c); Bryce 1990: 100-103 (c). (c) Tutankhamun: Freu (c, via D-M 2011: 147). The question matters only if one accepts Wilhelm-Boese's compression of Suppiluliuma's reign; on the Akhenaten reading of EA 41 Suppiluliuma was already king before Amenhotep III died (Miller: accession c. 1356, reign c. 26 years per KUB 19.9's 20 + 6).

Variants thus: Napḫur(u)riya, Napḫururiya, Napḫuririya, Napḫuriya, Namḫurya — first syllable with /a/ in all certain cases.
- Tutankhamun (Nb-ḫprw-Rʿ): only if EA 9 is his; no other candidate.
The philological argument: nib-/nip- = nb (Edel 1948: 14: "eine absolut einwandfreie Umschreibung des Namens Tutanchamons"; Bryce 1990: 97; Murnane 1983: 277; Schulman 1978: 43; Kitchen 1985: 43f.; van den Hout 1984: 84f.; Freu 2004: 52f. — all (c) via Miller 2007: 264 n. 51) versus nap- = nfr; counter-arguments: (1) phonetic — Helck 1994: 19: "nap entspricht naf/nef- = nfr und nib/p einem niw/new- von nb ... Der hörbare Unterschied zwischen Nfr-ḫprw-Rʿ und Nb-ḫprw-Rʿ war minimal"; Redford 1973: 49: praenomina with ḫprw were abbreviated in speech by elision of the first element; Krauss 1978 (c) — Krauss's counter as reported by Miller is chiefly the historical one (§3.3 above: the Amka attack is the first breach of the Kurustama peace and cannot postdate the Amarna-period Amka attack) together with his acceptance that Nibḫururiya can stand for Akhenaten; a GM 2000 note (Google Books snippet, author not verified) states that "Nibhururija can be interpreted both as a transcription of" Nb- and Nfr-ḫprw-Rʿ and that "Nibhururija had died in the summer or in the autumn, after which the queen sent her" letter; (2) graphic — the very wide variation in the other spellings (Miller 2007: 264); (3) confusion — Meyer 1992: 91ff.: Mursili's scribe mixed up Nebkheprure and Neferkheprure, two kings 2-4 years apart (Miller: simpler than Wilhelm-Boese's confusion with ʿAnkhkheprure); (4) EA 9 (§5.1). Miller's verdict (2007: 265): Bryce's/Edel's "precise rendering" is "at best an oversimplification"; the spelling "is likely not the reliable indicator that it is often assumed to be".

### 5.4 EA 170, 174-176, 363: the Amqu attacks
- EA 170 (Baaluya and Bet-ili to Aziru, then in Egypt), Knudtzon 1915: 675f. (a, OCR), ll. 14-27: `ša-ni-tú ṣābē māt ḫa-at-te / Ilu-pa-ak-ku el-te-qú-nim / ālāni māt am-ki ù iš-tu ālāni / Ia-ad-du-mi el-te-qú-nim-mi / ù be-lí-ni li-i-te / ša-ni-tú ki-a-am ni-eš-te-mi / Izi-ta-na / el-li-kám-mi / ù 9 līm(?) ṣābē šēpi / it-ti-šu ša el-li-kám / ù a-ma-ta la nu-tar-ri-iṣ / šum-ma i-na ki-it-ti i-pa-aš-šu-nim / ù i-ga-ša-du-nim / i-na māt nu-ḫa-aš-še / ù Ibat-ti-ilu / a-šap-par a-na pa-ni-šu` — Moran (as quoted by Miller 2007: 269 (a)): "Moreover, troops of Hatti under Lupakku have captured cities of Amqu, and with the cities they captured Aaddumi. May our lord know (this). Moreover, we have heard the following: Zitana has come and there are 90,000 infantrymen that have come with him. We have, however, not confirmed the report, whether they are really there and have arrived in Nuhašše, and so I am sending Bet-ili to him."
- EA 173 (Knudtzon OCR l. 53899: "[ ... chariots against] the land of Amki ..."), EA 174 (Bieri of Hašabu), 175 (Ildayya of Hazi), 176 (anonymous), 363 (ʿAbdi-Riša of Enišasi): "we were in the cities of Amqu, cities of the king my lord, and Etakkama (Aitakkama) the man of Kinza went before the troops of Hatti and set the cities of the king on fire" (Knudtzon OCR ll. 53950-54098: `a-na māt am-ki ālāni šarri bēli-ia ù al-li-uk Ie-ta-ga-ma amēl māt ki-in-za a-[na pa-ni] ṣābē māt ḫa-at-ti ...` (b)). EA 140 (Byblos) and EA 197 (Biryawaza) mention Aitakkama's Hittite-backed aggression (c).
- Placement relative to the Deeds: these letters date to Aziru's stay in Egypt, i.e. to the last year or so of Akhenaten's reign (Izre'el-Singer 1990; Singer 1990: 155ff.; Moran; Miller 2007: 287 (a)). The Deeds (A iii 1-4) name Lupakki and Tarhunta-zalma as the commanders sent to Amka while Suppiluliuma besieged Carchemish, and Zita (Zitana) as the GAL MEŠEDI sent ahead with Arnuwanda (A ii 29-31). Two readings: (i) one Amqu campaign, multi-pronged, at the end of Akhenaten's reign = the campaign of the Deeds and the prayer, hence Nibhururiya died at the end of Akhenaten's reign (Krauss 1978; Parker 2002; Miller 2007: 267-271, who calls two attacks a dozen years apart "a wholly ad hoc solution necessary solely for those who advocate the Nibhururiya = Tutankhamun identification"); (ii) two attacks — one c. Akhenaten's last year (Amarna) and one at Tutankhamun's death (Deeds/prayer, with the "again he sent, again they attacked" of KUB 14.8 i 19-20 as the second) — Houwink ten Cate 1963: 275; Schulman 1978; Kitchen 1985: 44; Barta 1983; Freu 2004: 38f.; Bryce 1990 (c); Wilhelm-Boese 1987: 101-104 also need two attacks, 3-4 years apart (Miller 2007: 268 n. 69). Note that the "namma" clause exists only in ms. A of the prayer.

### 5.5 EA 10 and 11: Mayati
EA 10 (Burna-Buriaš to Napḫururiya) and EA 11 (same, with a Babylonian princess being sent) mention "Mayati" (Ma-ia-ti), universally taken as Meritaten: EA 10: 43-49 gifts "for Mayati" (Moran: "your daughter"?) and EA 11 rev. 25ff. (Moran: the gift "for the mistress of the house/ Mayati") — (c): I could not locate the lines in the Knudtzon OCR (the name is split by line breaks); Moran 1992: 19-22 and Krauss 1978: 61ff. (c). Bearing: Meritaten already held a prominent, quasi-queenly position in the international correspondence of Akhenaten's later reign (before Burna-Buriaš's mid-reign, per Miller 2017), which supports (not proves) the candidacy of Meritaten as the taḫamunzu if the widow is Akhenaten's (Krauss 1978; Gabolde 1998; Parker 2002), and it is the reason Krauss/Gabolde read the "Mayati" of the Babylonian letters as the later ʿAnkhetkheprure.

## 6. Chronology

### 6.1 The Hittite relative sequence (Deeds + prayers + annals)
- Suppiluliuma's reign: c. 26 years if KUB 19.9's "20 years (Anatolia) + 6 (Syria)" are totals (Parker 2002: 53ff.; Miller 2007: 282-283 (a)); c. 20 years on Wilhelm-Boese's 1987 analysis of the Deeds' tablet-count (roughly half before the taḫamunzu episode, half after — Miller 2007: 276-279 (a); Wilhelm 2009: 113 (c): 14 before / 6 after if Smenkhkare); c. 40 years in the older literature (Güterbock; Kitchen 1962). Devecchi-Miller 2011: 158 (a): "Amenhotep III reigned from 1391/90 to 1353/2"; EA 41 (to Akhenaten) and the Rib-Adda/Mittanni correspondence put Suppiluliuma's activity in Syria already in the last years of Amenhotep III and the first years of Akhenaten, hence accession c. 1356 (Miller 2007: 283, Fig. 1).
- Nibhururiya's death: late summer/autumn of the year of the Carchemish siege; Hani arrives the following spring; Zannanza sent that summer; Zannanza killed; the Egyptian counter-campaign (Arnuwanda "went ahead to Egypt") and the prisoners bring the plague to Hatti (year +1); Suppiluliuma dies of the plague some years later (Deeds and prayers give no number; Bryce/van den Hout: "shortly"; Klinger 2006: 319: 2-4 years; Miller: c. 4-6 years, Fig. 1: Suppiluliuma † c. 1330); Arnuwanda II reigns c. 1 year and dies of the plague (c. 1330-1329); Mursili II accedes c. 1329/8 (Miller Fig. 1) — Mursili's years 7 and 9 (Syrian revolts; KUB 19.15+ with Arma'a still viceroy) c. 1322 and 1320; Horemheb's accession therefore not before c. 1320/19 on Miller's reading.
- The solar omen of Mursili's year 10 (KBo 4.4 ii 15-25, Extensive Annals; "the Sun-god gave a sign" as Mursili marched to Azzi): candidate eclipses per Devecchi-Miller 2011: 167 n. 71 (a) from the NASA Five Millennium Canon: 14 May 1338, 26 Aug 1315, 24 June 1312, 17 Aug 1306, 24 Apr 1290, 15 June 1284, 14 Apr 1281; marginal: 13 Mar 1335, 15 Aug 1333, 17 Oct 1328, 17 Oct 1309, 5 Aug 1305, 17 Sep 1279, 6 Jul 1275. Preferences: Wilhelm-Boese 1987: 105ff.: 13 Apr(?) 1308 [D-M: Wilhelm's "earlier preference for 17 Oct 1309"]; Wilhelm 2009: 115: 24 June 1312 (hence Mursili's accession 1321/1322 — the date behind "Mursili II 1321-1295" in Bryce 2005 and many handbooks); Huber 2001 (JAOS 121): preferred 1339 (= 14 May 1338), second 1334, third 1311/1312; Taracha 2008 (NABU 2008/14): 4 June 1321 (which D-M doubt was visible in central Anatolia); Miller: 26 Aug 1315 would fit his scheme best but he declines to "nail" year 10 to any eclipse (D-M 2011: 166-167). The 1312 eclipse gives Mursili year 1 = 1321/1322, Suppiluliuma † c. 1323/1322, and makes Tutankhamun's death (1324/1323 on HKW 2006, or 1327/1326 on the higher variants) coincide neatly with Nibhururiya's — this is the arithmetic that keeps the Tutankhamun identification alive in the handbooks (Bryce 2005; Freu; Stavi 2015 — (c)). The 1338/1335 eclipses would put Mursili year 10 at the end of Akhenaten's reign, impossible for everyone; the 1308/1306 options push Mursili's accession to 1318/1316 and Suppiluliuma's death to c. 1319/1317, which again favours Tutankhamun or even later.

### 6.2 Egyptian absolute dates (HKW 2006 as cited by Miller 2007: 255 n. 16 (a); Aston (in press 2010) via D-M 2011: 159 n. 55 (a))
- Hornung-Krauss-Warburton 2006: 492f.: Amenhotep III 1390-1353; Akhenaten 1353-1336; Smenkhkare/Neferneferuaten ʿAnkhkheprure 1336-1334; Neferneferuaten ʿAnkhetkheprure 1334-?; Tutankhamun ?-1324; (Ay 1323-1319; Horemheb 1319-1292 by implication). Miller's Fig. 1 (Krauss 1978-based for this stretch, von Beckerath 1997 otherwise): Akhenaten † 1336/34; Smenkhkare 1336/34-1333/32; Tutankhamun 1333/32-1323; Ay 1323-1319; Horemheb 1319-. Aston: 17 years Akhenaten, 4 Smenkhkare/Neferneferuaten, 1 ʿAnkhetkheprure, 9 Tutankhamun, 3 Ay, 14 Horemheb (i.e. Horemheb reduced from 27/28 to c. 14 years, which D-M show creates a c. 10-year deficit against the 96+ attested Babylonian regnal years between Burna-Buriaš II and Kadašman-Enlil II, unless Ramesses II's accession is raised from 1279 to c. 1295 or 1305 — D-M 2011: 158-168 (a)). Season: Tutankhamun was buried in (or around) March/April (floral collars; Krauss 1996: 248, SAK 23; Hornung 2006: 206ff.), so with the 70-day embalming he died in late January/February; Ay presided at the burial (Miller 2007: 271-272 (a)). Nibhururiya, by contrast, died in late summer/autumn (Deeds: same season as the Amka raid and the siege; the "winter" then passes before Hani's spring arrival). Hence the Tutankhamun identification requires either a c. 6-month exposure of the body during the negotiations (Bryce 1990: 104f.; Barta 1983) or a mistaken burial season; the Akhenaten identification requires nothing here (Akhenaten's own death/burial season is unknown).

## 8. Assessment: what must be true for Nibhururiya to be Akhenaten, and what each assumption costs
The question: Ankhkheperure Smenkhkare / Neferneferuaten between Akhenaten and Tutankhamun; was one of them Nefertiti?

A. For Nibhururiya = Akhenaten (Miller/Krauss/Gabolde/Parker/Helck/Reeves):
1. The Hittite scribes wrote Nfr-ḫprw-Rʿ with an initial /i/ (Nib-/Bib-). Cost: against the otherwise consistent nap-/nab- of the Amarna letters — unless EA 9 (Nipḫurrireya) is Akhenaten's, which Miller argues on five independent grounds and Miller 2017 strengthens with Babylonian data. If EA 9 is Akhenaten's the cost is nil; if EA 9 is Tutankhamun's the cost is a unique scribal confusion (Meyer) or a phonetic elision (Helck/Redford).
2. Akhenaten left no (recognised) son. Cost: the KV55 = Akhenaten and KV35YL = mother results (2010) make Tutankhamun Akhenaten's son biologically (Dossier B); one must then accept that the widow (Nefertiti or Meritaten) and Hani lied to Suppiluliuma about "no son", that Hattusa-ziti was deceived or complicit, and that Mursili's narrative suppressed it — Miller 2007: 258-262 admits this "should not be taken lightly" but constructs a scenario (Suppiluliuma gambling on the queen + Ay against child claimants). Gabolde's version (Meritaten pushing aside her brother) has the same cost. Dodson's/Reeves's version (Nefertiti as the widow, ruling as Neferneferuaten) has it too, unless "son" is read as "adult son".
3. The single, multi-pronged Amqu campaign of the Amarna letters (Lupakku, Zitana, Aitakkama; Aziru in Egypt) is the Amka raid of the Deeds and the prayer. Cost: nil on the Hittite side (the "again he sent" clause is in one ms. only, and Hittitologists would not read a 12-year gap into namma); on the Egyptian side it requires Akhenaten to have died in the same campaigning season as EA 170 etc., i.e. late in his year 17 — which is what the Egyptian evidence (wine dockets to year 17) allows.
4. Horemheb (Arma'a) was not yet king in Mursili's year 9, so Horemheb's accession ≥ c. 1320 and Suppiluliuma † c. 1330, only 4-6 years after the episode. Cost: the Deeds devote c. 5 tablets to those few years (Wilhelm-Boese's objection; Miller concedes it is "disconcerting"); Simon's point that a contemporary *Ḥarmaḥa should surface with ḫ; Groddek's and Wilhelm's alternative readings of KUB 19.15+ (Arma'a already pharaoh) would collapse the synchronism and reopen Tutankhamun. Also the year-10 omen can then only be the 1315 (or 1321) eclipse, neither favoured by astronomers.
5. The taḫamunzu episode must fit between Akhenaten's death and Smenkhkare's accession (Miller) or after Smenkhkare's death with Meritaten referring back to Akhenaten (Miller n. 92 option 2), or Zannanza = Smenkhkare (Gabolde). Cost: an interregnum of c. 9-12 months (autumn to the following summer) with the widow holding power; the wine dockets of "year 1" attributed to Smenkhkare/Neferneferuaten must be squeezed; the Egyptian queen's second letter says "in Egypt he will be king", which implies no king yet — i.e. Smenkhkare had not yet acceded when Hani left (spring), so Smenkhkare's year 1 would begin after the summer of Zannanza's death at the earliest, or he is Zannanza.

B. For Nibhururiya = Tutankhamun (Güterbock/Edel/Kitchen/Bryce/Freu/Groddek/van den Hout/Stavi?):
1. Spelling: no cost (Nib- = nb) unless EA 9 is Akhenaten's.
2. "No son": no cost; Ankhesenamun's refusal of a "servant" (Ay) is natural.
3. Two Amqu campaigns c. 12 years apart with the same commander (Lupakki) and the same envoy (Hani) in both, and with the second attested only in Hittite sources and the first only in Egyptian ones. Cost: the coincidences and the "mutually exclusive datasets" (Miller); Krauss's Kurustama-breach argument.
4. Season: Tutankhamun buried March/April → died Jan/Feb; Nibhururiya died late summer/autumn → the body waited c. 6 months while the queen negotiated (Bryce). Cost: no Egyptian evidence for a delayed burial; but the embalming period could in principle be extended, and Ay's presence at the burial then follows the failure of the scheme.
5. Chronology: Suppiluliuma must have reigned c. 40 years (c. 1360/50-1322) if EA 41 is to Akhenaten (or EA 41 must be to Smenkhkare with "father" = predecessor), and Arma'a in KUB 19.15+ must be pharaoh Horemheb or someone else; the 1312 eclipse then fits Mursili year 10 and Tutankhamun † 1324/3 = Suppiluliuma's last year(s). Cost: the lopsided Deeds distribution is eased; the EA 41 "father" must be explained away; KUB 19.15+ must be read against Miller.

C. For Nibhururiya = Smenkhkare (Wilhelm-Boese 1987): requires Nib- to stand for ʿnḫ-ḫprw-Rʿ (the weakest spelling case of the three), EA 41 to Smenkhkare, and a 20-year reign split evenly; Wilhelm's own later moves and Miller's critique leave this the least supported option.

Bearing on the Nefertiti question: the Hittite dossier does not name the queen; it says only that she was "the wife of the king" (taḫamunzu), that her husband Nibhururiya had died, that she had no son, that she would not marry a "servant", and that Hani called her "our lady ... solitary". If Nibhururiya is Akhenaten, the candidates are Nefertiti (if alive: Miller's, Reeves's, Belmonte's, Dodson's(?) choice) or Meritaten (Krauss 1978, Gabolde, Parker) or Kiya (Helck). The Hittite side cannot distinguish them; what it does supply is (i) an interregnum of c. 9-12 months after Akhenaten's death in which a woman exercised royal authority without a king, which would be the natural slot for the female ʿAnkh(et)kheprure Neferneferuaten only if she had not yet taken kingly titles (her second letter presupposes that Egypt needs a king), and (ii) the statement that she would rather take a foreign husband than elevate one of her own subjects, which sits awkwardly with a queen who then reigned as king herself. If Nibhururiya is Tutankhamun, the Hittite texts say nothing at all about the Akhenaten-Tutankhamun interval and the identity of Neferneferuaten must be settled on Egyptian evidence alone.

My weighting: the Tutankhamun identification remains the majority handbook view because it costs nothing philologically and nothing on the "no son" point, and because the 1312 eclipse arithmetic is seductive; but its two ad-hoc requirements (a second Amqu campaign invisible in Egyptian sources; a half-year delay of the royal burial) are real. Miller's synchronism is the strongest single new datum since 1956 and, if Arma'a = Horemheb-before-accession holds, it is decisive for Akhenaten; it is contested on the spelling (Simon) and on the reading of KUB 19.15+ ii 2' (Wilhelm, Groddek), and it produces the lopsided Deeds. The 2010 DNA results (Tutankhamun as Akhenaten's son) raise the cost of the Akhenaten identification on the "no son" point considerably, and this is where the succession models should carry the argument: the Hittite evidence is consistent with either identification, is inconsistent with Smenkhkare, and can be made to favour Akhenaten only by accepting KUB 19.15+ as Miller reads it.
