# G. Open-access and semi-open articles — what was obtained

All files are in `sources/`. Files added for this dossier are marked **[new]**; other files named here were already in the folder and were checked, not re-downloaded.

## Access notes
- quod.lib.umich.edu (WSFH Proceedings, both `/w/wsfh/` and `/cgi/t/text/text-idx?c=wsfh`) and catalog/babel.hathitrust.org return a Cloudflare "Just a moment" challenge (HTTP 403) to curl with a browser User-Agent. journals.publishing.umich.edu/wsfh returns an Anubis bot-check page. None could be read.
- Wayback Machine: the CDX API works over https (`https://web.archive.org/cdx/search/cdx?...`; the http:// form is "Blocked by egress policy"); archived pages are served with `https://web.archive.org/web/<ts>id_/<url>`. `archive.org/wayback/available` works.
- Persée: article pages work; `docAsPDF` returns 403 with any User-Agent; the per-page text endpoint works: `https://www.persee.fr/doc/page/<docid>/<docid-with-page>` (URL found in the article page's `data-content-url` attributes). This gave the second pages of both Persée items below.
- Rate limits hit: Google Books API (429, daily quota 0), OpenAlex (daily budget exhausted), Semantic Scholar (429), scholar.archive.org (rate limit). Crossref API works.
- cairn.info: 403 JS challenge. lemonde.fr search: 410. Web search cannot query lemonde.fr, lefigaro.fr, lhistoire.fr, liberation.fr, lexpress.fr, lepoint.fr (crawler blocked by those sites). read.dukeupress.edu, academic.oup.com (curl), noozhawk.com (curl) and masque-de-fer.com (503) refused direct fetches. SUDOC needs a cookie jar (`curl -c/-b`) and then works. BnF SRU (`catalogue.bnf.fr/api/SRU`) works but intermittently resets the connection.

## 1. Sonnino, "On the Trail of the Iron Mask: The Candidacy of Claude Imbert", WSFH Proceedings 19 — NOT OBTAINED
- Routes tried: quod.lib.umich.edu index, `text-idx` and article URLs (Cloudflare 403); HathiTrust catalogue and full-text search (Cloudflare 403); journals.publishing.umich.edu (bot check); Wayback CDX for the whole `/w/wsfh/` tree (1,039 captured URLs: volumes 1-5, 7-9, 11, 13, 15-17, 24-25, 28, 31-48; no capture of any `0642292.0019` page); archive.org (no WSFH Proceedings scanned); Google Books API, OpenAlex, Semantic Scholar, IA Scholar (rate-limited); Crossref (no DOI for the article).
- The 2013 Wayback capture of `http://quod.lib.umich.edu/w/wsfh/0642292.*` confirms that "Vol. 19 (1991)" is hosted on quod, so the text exists there for anyone with normal browser access.
- Citation discrepancy: Madry's Works Cited gives "Proceedings of the Annual Meeting of the WSFH 19 (1992): 89–98" while her in-text citation is "Sonnino 'Imbert' 104"; fr.wikipedia gives vol. 19, 1991, pp. 99-108; Sonnino's own CV (sonnino_cv_2016_ucsb.txt, revised 10 March 2016) gives "Vol. XIX pp. 261-272" with no year. Crossref lists the reworked version as chapter 2 of the 2016 book, "The Candidacy of Claude Imbert", pp. 27-36 (DOI 10.5040/9798216457824.ch-002; in copyright, not downloaded).
- Secondhand content: Madry (p. 4) says the 1992 article presented Imbert as "a likely candidate" while Sonnino "admits that the archives produced an échec for his suspect". The archive.org search fragments of Sonnino 2016 (sonnino_2016_snippets.md, lines 284-288, 339, 366, 630) show what the candidacy was and why it failed: Imbert was an agent of Cardinal de Retz (instrumental in Retz's escape in 1654), later linked to Fouquet-Croissy and supposedly given "a new identity and, in due time, ... a new life as a valet"; Sonnino then "found Imbert, alive and kicking in 1675, 1679, and 1681", after which "the candidacy of Claude Imbert blew up in my face".

## 3. Madry, "The Valet: The Marquis de Louvois's Invited Guest...", Cahiers du dix-septième 17 (2016), 1-27 — FULL TEXT (already in folder)
- Files: madry2016_valet_cahiers17.pdf (= madry_2016_cahiers17_the_valet_louvois_invited_guest.pdf, identical md5 81f0bb3a...) and .txt extractions. Verified against the journal's OJS copy (identical md5, 561,236 bytes): https://ojs.lib.uwo.ca/index.php/cahiers17e/article/download/21651/16875/55855 . Article page: https://earlymodernfrance.org/journal/2016-volume-xvii/valet-marquis-louvois%E2%80%99s-invited-guest-mystery-man-iron-mask (the bare `/journal/2016-volume-xvii/` URL returns 404). Whole-volume PDF (1.36 MB, not downloaded): https://www.earlymodernfrance.org/files/Cahiers17_17(2016).pdf . Sarah Madry is an independent scholar, Chapel Hill, NC.
- Argument. Louvois's phrase "comme ce n'est qu'un valet" in his letter to Saint-Mars of 19 July 1669 (text pp. 8-9, from Delort, Détention des philosophes I, 155-56) is not a description of the prisoner's occupation but a pun, and it shows Louvois knew nothing about the prisoner except his name (pp. 1-2, 20). The pun (pp. 13-16): the prisoner's name had many spellings (Noone 212: Daugier, Doger, Dogier, d'Auger, d'Augier, d'Oger, d'Ogier...); French playing cards named the Jack of Spades "Hogier" (Ogier le Danois), and the Jack is in French the "valet"; "valet" was also a current insult (Héroard; Condé called "valet de Mazarin"). The real target (pp. 17-18): Louis d'Auger de Cavoye, Louvois's successful rival in 1668 for Marie-Sidonie de Lenoncourt, marquise de Courcelles (source: Pougin's 1855 edition of her memoirs and Hillairet's Dictionnaire des rues de Paris, II, 501); Cavoye was jailed for duelling in July 1668 and the Courcelles adultery case ran in 1669, so Louvois was mocking Cavoye, and Saint-Mars (whose wife's sister was Louvois's mistress, n. 7) would have got the joke. Conclusions (pp. 20-22): Louvois had no background knowledge of the prisoner on 19 July 1669; the spelling is "Dauger", not Caire's and Petitfils's "Danger" (p. 21, n. 3); "Eustache Dauger" was the name Louis XIV believed real, since Louvois would not pun on an invented alias; Louis XIV kept the secret from Le Tellier, Louvois and Saint-Mars alike; the prisoner "was as likely a valet as he was a shoemaker or a bureaucrat" (p. 22). Consequence (pp. 23-24): the "valet" no longer bars a Bourbon blood-relation hypothesis, and historians must keep asking why Louis XIV imprisoned him. Ancillary data used: Brugnon's Mélanges Colbert reimbursement order for Vauroy's escort, quoted via Vergé-Franceschi 261 (pp. 9-10); Du Junca's register (Arsenal ms 5133, f. 37v) (p. 3). No new archival document is produced; the evidence is printed sources, playing-card decks on Gallica (Trioullier, 1760s) and Ranum on card games.

## 4. Mongrédien, "Le problème du Masque de fer" [sommaire: "Deux documents inédits sur le Masque de Fer"], XVIIe siècle 17-18 (1953), 55-58 — OCR CHECKED, complete and legible
- mongredien1953_xviie_siecle_17-18.txt, lines c. 3676-3932 (p. 55 begins "Dans un livre solidement documenté, M. Maurice Duvivier"; running heads "LE PROBLÈME DU MASQUE DE FER 57" and "58"; ends "Georges MONGRÉDIEN" before "Le sens de l'Apologie de Pascal"). The same pages are in mongredien_1953_xviie_siecle_deux_documents_inedits_extract.txt and in xviie_siecle_1953_nos17-18_full_issue_djvu.txt. OCR noise is limited to stray marks at line starts and a few garbled words; every sentence is readable, so no better copy was sought.
- Content: p. 55 — Duvivier (1932) took Lair's Eustache Dauger and identified him with Eustache Dauger de Cavoye; Mongrédien (1951, pp. 211-219) had rejected this as "un fragile échafaudage d'hypothèses". pp. 56-58 — two documents from the archives of the baronne de Sarret de Coussergues: Eustache de Cavoye's letter to his sister the marquise de Fabrègues, Saint-Lazare, 20 June 1678 ("détenu depuis plus de dix ans par la tirannie de M. de Cavoy mon frère"), and his undated petition to the King ("destenu dans les prisons de St Lazare par une lettre de cachet de Vostre Majesté depuis unze ans et demy"). Footnotes give the family acts (will of 6 May 1664; cession of 15 August 1665; Louis de Cavoye's arrest 5 July 1668; the role of Clérac). Conclusion p. 58: Cavoye entered Saint-Lazare in 1668 and was still there in 1680, so he cannot have been at Pignerol from 1669; the valet Eustache Dauger "a toutes les chances d'être le prisonnier masqué mort à la Bastille en 1703", whose story "restera sans doute toujours mystérieuse".

## 5. Reviews of Petitfils, Le Masque de fer (2003/2004)
- **[new] review_venturino_2005_dhs37_petitfils_complete_pp645-646.txt** — Diego Venturino, Dix-huitième Siècle 37 (2005), 645-646, now complete (the earlier file review_venturino_persee_2005_petitfils.txt stopped at the end of p. 645). https://www.persee.fr/doc/dhs_0070-6760_2005_num_37_1_2699_t1_0645_0000_2 . Identification as stated by the reviewer: the prisoner "s'appelait Eustache Danger, valet de Henriette d'Angleterre, Madame", active during the secret Louis XIV–Charles II negotiations; "Très probablement Eustache avait vu des pièces qu'il ne fallait pas voir et, craignant son indiscrétion, Louis XIV avait décidé de le faire disparaître"; "l'identité du Masque de fer est dévoilée, mais les raisons de l'emprisonnement demeurent inconnues" (p. 645). Secrecy/mask: Saint-Mars surrounded the prisoner with mystery to raise his own prestige; "on cachait le prisonnier non parce qu'il était connu, mais parce qu'il était inconnu et, au fond, sans importance depuis les années 1680"; Barbezieux's hoax that he was the king's brother; Voltaire's Siècle de Louis XIV (p. 645); p. 646: the legend's afterlife; "cet ouvrage marque une étape décisive".
- review_genet_hnet_2004_petitfils.txt (already in folder) — Stéphane Genet, H-Francais, October 2003, on the 2003 Perrin edition: thesis 1, Eustache Danger, "humble valet affecté au service de Madame, Henriette d'Angleterre", who had handled secret documents on Charles II's conversion and the Franco-English alliance against the United Provinces and "aurait été trop bavard (auprès des Hollandais ?)", arrested at Calais; thesis 2, the mask was a mystification by Saint-Mars, bored at Exilles without a prisoner "de valeur", never requested by Louvois or Barbezieux, worn only in public; Barbezieux's boast; Voltaire.
- Not found: reviews in XVIIe siècle, Revue historique, Histoire économie et société, Annales, Le Monde or Le Figaro 2003-2004. Persée full-text search for "Petitfils 'Masque de fer'" returns only Venturino; Cairn and the newspapers are not reachable from here (see access notes).

## 6. Batiffol, review of Burgaud and Bazeries, BEC 55 (1894), 359-360 — FULL TEXT, both pages
- **[new] batiffol1894_bec55_pp359-360_review_burgaud_bazeries.txt** — from the archive.org OCR of the Toronto copy (https://archive.org/details/bibliothquedel55sociuoft, djvu.txt lines 19827-19870) and, for p. 360, the Persée per-page endpoint (https://www.persee.fr/doc/page/bec_0373-6237_1894_num_55_1_447793_t1_0359_0000_2/bec_0373-6237_1894_num_55_1_T1_0360_0000).
- Content: the Bulonde thesis rests entirely on reading the unexplained cipher group 330 in Louvois's dispatch to Catinat of 8 July 1691 as "avec un masque"; "Ils n'ont pas d'autre preuve" (p. 359); the identity of an unimportant man barely known to contemporaries would not have been surrounded with such mystery; and the thesis is provably false, because Bulonde would have to have died on 19 November 1703, whereas the dossier Labbé de Bulonde in the Cabinet des titres (pièces originales t. 1612, dossier 37417) has at n° 22 a receipt by Bulonde dated 28 November 1705 and at n° 10 a note that he died in 1709 (pp. 359-360). Footnote 1 (p. 360): Geoffroy de Grandmaison had already pointed to these documents in L'Univers of 9 January 1894. The book's merit is the publication of war-archive documents on the Italian campaign and the decipherment of government dispatches.

## 9. Stanislas Brugnon — references found
- (a) "L'identité de l'homme au masque de fer", in Il y a trois siècles... (Cannes, 1989) — cited in the bibliography of Sonnino 2016 (sonnino_2016_snippets.md, lines 515-516). At Cannes Brugnon produced the "conduite" in the Mélanges Colbert (BnF): Colbert's reimbursement of 3,000 livres to Vauroy for four men Dunkirk-Calais, five men Calais-Pignerol and four men back (Madry pp. 9-10 after Vergé-Franceschi 261; Petitfils 2004; Sonnino 2016 ch. "Calais"), and, per Noone, "a document which until that time had been [unknown]" establishing the authenticity of three published letters. His own position, quoted on cannes.com (cannes_mediatheque_masque_de_fer_star_cannoise.txt) and in the Pinerolo proceedings: the prisoner of Sainte-Marguerite died there in 1693 ("à l'heure de minuit", carried by the cadet Jacques Souchon) and never went to the Bastille.
- (b) Reconstitution du registre d'écrou des prisonniers de l'île Sainte-Marguerite à compter du 30 avril 1687 et jusqu'au 19 mars de l'année 1704, Paris, S. Brugnon, 1992, 51 f., ill., fac-sim., 30 cm (BnF catalogue record; cited by Petitfils 2004 as "Reconstitution du registre d'écrou des prisonniers..."). This is the only separately catalogued Brugnon title; no published Pignerol chronology was located, but Petitfils credits Brugnon with the Pignerol/Sainte-Marguerite datings he uses (the identity of the prisoner in the citadel, La Prade's transfer of January 1694, sums in the comptes du roi, BnF Fr 14112). The Pinerolo proceedings describe him as "chargé de mission honoraire au Ministère des Finances"; Petitfils (2004) calls him "le regretté Stanislas Brugnon", whose papers he used through his widow.
- (c) The Saint-Lazare order of 17 August 1678: the English Wikipedia article (wikipedia_en_man_in_the_iron_mask_reference_extract.txt, line 318) states that the King answered Cavoye's petition with a letter to the head of Saint-Lazare dated 17 August 1678, signed by the King and Colbert, ordering that "M. de Cavoye should have communication with no one at all, not even with his sister, unless in your presence or in the presence of one of the priests of the mission"; Petitfils (2004) lists among the proofs of Cavoye's detention "une dépêche du roi au prieur de cette maison". Neither the text of the order nor Brugnon's role in finding it is available online; the attribution to Brugnon could not be verified from open sources.
