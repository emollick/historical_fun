HOW SPORTSMEN CAN HELP.
Mr. Miller Wants Their Influential Ald in
Securing Revised Game Laws,
The following explains itself:
To the Sportsmen of Virginia:
I was directed by the Virginia Fleld
Sports Association at its annual meeting,
held in this city in October last, to pre
pare a bill to be presented to the Legis
lature for the protection of game. I have
done so, and for your information I beg
to say that the needed changes and addi
tions have been asked for, and I trust
‘that you will urge your representatives
in the General Assembly to support them.
It is impossible for me to answer all the
letters I get telling me to "get a bill
through,” &ec. 1 have visited the legisia
‘tive body frequently, and always get a
respectful hearing, and they all say, “We
heartily endorse yoursbill, and wiil vote
for it.” I have been before the last four
sessions of our legislature on the same
business, and got nice treatment, and
from the hearty manner in which they
endorsed my propositions, I thought it
was a dead sure thing; but when they
had scattered to their homes the ‘‘game
bili” was among the good things over.
looked. This is positively my last at
tempt, and if we cannot get the bill
through now, at a time when the effects
of last winter's snow are remembered as
the most destructive to game since the
winters of '36 and '57, it is useless to try
any further. If something is not done by
this Legislature for the protection of cur
game, it is but a question of a year or
two mora when we will enjoy “shooting
in Virginia” through the medium of books
written on that subject by the older peo
ple.

The changes asked for are as follows:
“The open season for birds is changed to
November 1st, instead of October 15th, as
it now is.” This is for the East Blue
Ridge section. In the West Blue Ridge
country October 15th is asked for by
many sportsmen as the opening time, and
January 1st as the closing time, but we
ask for the 1st of November all over the
State as the time for the opening, while
it runs to February 1st in the East, Birds
are not to be shot in the snow at any
time—in or out of season. Shipment of
game out of the State, dead or alive, is
positively forbidden. Trapping and net
ting, too, ars prohibited. The payment
for the scalps of hawks, owls, minks,
and weasels are asked for as a law of
the State, requiring all counties to pay
for them, instead of as now, some coun
ties paying, while others do not, The
scalps of foxes, killed by dogs only, to be
paid for. Hares are protected, because
when hunted in July, August, September,
and October the hounds destroy a great
many young birds. While we beg for a
geperal law with regard to other game,
and discourage “local” laws, still we are
willing to exempt those counties from the
peration of the general law when hares are
destructive to young fruft trees and vege
tables. The changes asked for for deer,
turkeys, wild water-fowl, and pheasants
are immaterial as to the open and close
season, but we ask for such penalties for
the violation of the law as will make
their rigid enforcement a certainty.
POLK MILLER,
President Virginia Field-Sports Ass'n,
