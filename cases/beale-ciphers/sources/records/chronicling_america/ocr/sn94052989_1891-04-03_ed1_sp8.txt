MEXICAN DELICACIES.
The Composition of Tamalos and
Tortillas.
Of those dishes which are peculiarly
Mexican in their composition and character
the most commou and at the same time the
most valued are tamnles and tortillas. The
natural home of both of these delicacies is
iv the geographical basin between Russian
and Telegraph hills and Kearny anl Dupont
streets, where the livelihood of scores of
people is derived from their manufacture.
There is a popular belief that sea-gulls
figure largely among the ingredients of
which tamales are made, but unless in ex
ceptional cases this impression 15 erroneous.
Chicken forms the basis, while eornmeal,
with strong seasoning agencies, and frijoles,
or red heaps, compose the adjuncts. Before
the feathers are removed the fowl is thor
oughly steamed. The chicken is then
plucked, and the meat chopped into fine
pieces. Meal from ground com is added,
and an orthodox hash is formed by the ad
dition' of frijoles, chopped fine. Bed pep
pers In liberal quantity are thrown in, and
the whole is flavored by two or three pickled
olives.
PLACED IN corn-husks.
Theolla podrida is then placed in dried
corn-husks, shaped like a large-sized sausage,
with the ends closed and fastened together
with strips of the husks. The formation of
the tamale is then complete, and when it has
been cooked by steaming it is ready for table
service. Every epicure in San Francisco is
familiar with the flavor of taiualc, but for
the benefit of the uninitiated the advisability
might be suggested of ordering a coo! liquid
in case of an emergency at the first discus
sion of the delicacy.
Hinckley alley Is almost entirely given up
to the compounding nf tortillas, which
resemble in appearanco thin buckwheat
cakes, and which have an insipid, doughy
taste. Each of the dilapidated old wooden
bouses on the thoroughfare just men
tioned has an underground basement,
and here the tortillas are made.
Corn is ground by means of small stove
mills, tho power of which is derived from
Mexican women's arms.
MIXED AND BAKED.
The meal is mixed with water until a thick
stiff dough is obtained, and this is pressed
thin with a stove rolling-pin upon a stone
block used as a table. The lady operator
then takes a piece of dough aud manipulates
it for a few minutes, throwing it rapidly
from hand to hand. On an adjacent stone
oven are some red coals, over and upon
which is a piece of zinc. On this the piece
of dough is dropped and allowed to remain
until it becomes inflated, the signal of ma
turity in the CooEingo! Mexican bread and
cakes. It is then removed from the im
promptu oven and the tortilla is ready for
consumption.
The taste cf the tortilla, four of which
