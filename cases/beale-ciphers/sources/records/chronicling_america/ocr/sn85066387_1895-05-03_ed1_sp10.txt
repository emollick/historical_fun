CHICAGO MARKETS.
CHICAGO, 111., May 2.— Wheat opened strong

on the day before, filled the bulls with enthusiasm

an advance of l A@ 3 /±c. July opened with buyers

of forty minutes from the start it had tumbled
back to 64% c, and wobbled up and down between
that and 64% c for more than an hour after. The
early cables quoted the following advances: Liver
pool, Id higher and strong; Paris, 10 centimes
higher for flour and 5 centimes higher for May
wheat: Berlin, higher, the equivalent of 3%cper
bushel for May and 214 c per bushel for September.
Those quotations, together with the dry weather
east of the Mississippi and the fear of what the
men who control the cash wheat might do, made
the combination which put the price up. Rain in
generous quantities in many of the winter wheat
districts and light showers over a wider area, with
a general rainy condition drifting steadily east
ward, were the reasons for the decline which fol
lowed the first advance. The market had a quick
reaction from 64% c for July to 653/ c before -12
o'clock, the orders to buy at the decline proving
more than enough to absorb the offerings and re
awakening the earlier panicky feeling of the
shorts.
The foreign cables to the Board of Trade, giving
the close of the markets, quoted strong advances at
Berlin and Paris, the former being given at an ad
vance equivalent to 2%c per bushel for May, 214 c
for September, and the latter about 214 c higher for
both the present month and the September-Decem
ber term. These strong cables were given as the
reason for the second bulge already referred to,
but as the handlers of cash wheat reported no de
mand, an abundance of July wheat was for sale at
65c and over, the market became weaker than
ever, and the price broke lower than on the earlier
declines. July closed at 64»/ c.
