A STRANGE CASE.
Can a Man Be Imprisoned for One-Half of
HU l ife?
What is considered an unique case in
the history of American jurisprudence
took p'ace at Sioux Falls, S. D., the
question being-: “Can a man be impris
oned for one-half of his life?" Two
years ago John Leford, of Deadwood,
was sentenced to the peuitentiary for
fifteen years for attempting to rob
the Homstake treasure coach. The
statutes of Dakota at that time
provided that where an unsuccessful
attempt was made to commit high
way robbery the penalty shall be one
half of the greatest penalty for a suc
cessful commission of the crime. The
severest penalty for highway robbery
is imprisonment for life. The court in
Le ford's case thought fifteen years
would be half of his life, and an argu
ment was heard before Judge Aitkens
for the release of Leford from the pen
itentiary upon the grounds that a pen
alty of one-half of a man’s life was so
vague and indefinite that he should not
be deprived of his liberty. The judge
granted the release of the prisoner.
John Leford was given his freedom.
