title: Lines by My Late Wife, on Being Requested to Write in an Album
author: Catharine Elizabeth Taylor Moore (Moore's wife, d. 1830)
date: pub. 1844
source: Poems (New York: Bartlett & Welford, 1844), pp. 133-134; scan https://archive.org/details/poemsmoor00moorrich (leaves n150 ff.)
form: iambic tetrameter/trimeter quatrains (common measure, abcb)
lines: 20
attribution: not Moore (Catharine Elizabeth Taylor Moore (Moore's wife, d. 1830))
notes: NOT by Moore: by his late wife, signed 'La Mère de Cinq Enfans' (italic, right-aligned, after the last stanza; the Preface refers to it). Addressed to 'dear Sarah' / 'dear Coz'. Excluded from the Moore corpus. Heading as printed: 'BY MY LATE WIFE, / ON BEING REQUESTED TO WRITE IN AN ALBUM.'. Editorial: SIGNATURE p134: La Mère de Cinq Enfans. (italic, right-aligned)

In vain, dear Sarah, you command,
In vain would I obey.
Fain would my, now degraded, hand
The heavenly Lyre essay.

Gently I try to touch the chords,
But ah! I vainly try.
My hand bestows its usual slap;
The Lyre returns a cry.

I seek the Muse, with humble voice
Her sweetest smiles to woo;
In vain—my tongue resumes its tone,
And scolds when it should sue.

At this, as you may well suppose,
No fav'ring glance appears;
And Helicon, when I would sip,
Is turned to briny tears.

In short, dear Coz, Apollo's now,
To me, an angry God.
My music now is cries and screams,
My Lyre a Birchen Rod.
