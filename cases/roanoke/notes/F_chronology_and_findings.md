## What the record establishes (and what it does not)

1. The colonists' stated plan on 22 August 1587 was a move of about fifty miles "further vp into the maine", to be made "presently", by carriage of goods. White repeated in 1590 that "alway they were prepared to remoue from Roanoak 50 miles into the maine". The 1587 seat had been forced on them; the ordered seat was the Chesapeake, 130 miles north by Lane's reckoning.
2. The only name they carved was CROATOAN, the island of Manteo's kin, about 47 statute miles south-south-east and not on the mainland. Under the agreed code the word means "the place where they should be seated" at the time of carving; the absence of the cross means they did not leave in distress. The code says nothing about whether all went, or whether they moved on afterwards.
3. The Roanoke site was left in order: houses dismantled, a palisade built after August 1587, everything portable and every boat and light gun removed by water, only the heaviest iron and lead left. The chests had been dug up "long sithence". By July 1588 a Spanish reconnaissance saw the harbour works and English debris on the banks and no people.
4. Croatoan could not feed a hundred and sixteen extra people for long on its inhabitants' own statement (1587) and on Lane's use of it (1586) as a twenty-man fishing and lookout post. A first move to Croatoan and a later move to the mainland are both consistent with the record; so is a split.
5. No English party set foot on Croatoan after 1 August 1587. White anchored off it in 1590 without landing; the searches of 1602 and 1603 never reached the sounds; the 1603 search aimed at the Chesapeake.
6. Nothing in the 1584-1603 English or Spanish record connects the colonists with Powhatan; Spain never attacked the settlement; Pedro Díaz's contemporary guess was famine.
7. England presumed survival through 1599 (Hakluyt) and 1602 (Ralegh). No report of the colony's fate reached print before the Jamestown reports of 1608-1612.
8. Precedents in the same record: the Secotan tradition (Barlowe 1584) of shipwrecked white men who built boats and were lost; Grenville's men (1586) who rowed off "whither as yet we know not"; Lane's dispersal of his colony to Croatoan, Hatorask and the mainland to eat (1586).

## Points the sources do not settle

- Whether the pinnace could have made an ocean passage with any number of people: its tonnage is not given (the 1588 relief pinnaces were 25 and 30 tons); Lane's pinnace "drew too deep water for that shallow sound".
- The Spanish wording of what González saw (Geiger: "a ship-yard, indicated by the presence of sloops"; Quinn, unverified here: "a slipway for small vessels" and "wells made with English casks"). Not obtainable (López 1931 not online; HathiTrust and Google Books searches blocked).
- The 1587 charter's list of Assistants; White's 1587 letter to Ralegh and Ralegh's letter to the colonists (both lost); Harriot's "Chronicle" (lost).
