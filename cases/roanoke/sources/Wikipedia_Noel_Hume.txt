From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

**Ivor Noël Hume** , [OBE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_the_British_Empire "Order of the British Empire") (30 September 1927 – 4 February 2017) was a British-born [archaeologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaeologist "Archaeologist") who did research in the United States. A former director of [Colonial Williamsburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonial_Williamsburg "Colonial Williamsburg")’s archaeological research program and the author of more than 20 books, he was heralded by his peers as the "father of [historical archaeology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historical_archaeology "Historical archaeology")".[1]

## Biography

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Ivor_No%C3%ABl_Hume&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Biography")]

Born in London, Noël Hume studied at [Framlingham College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Framlingham_College "Framlingham College"), Suffolk and [St. Lawrence College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Lawrence_College,_Ramsgate "St. Lawrence College, Ramsgate"), Kent. He spent a short stint in the [British Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Army "British Army") during [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II"), and as an assistant stage manager for a London theatre, before deciding to pursue archaeology as a career and joining the staff of [Guildhall Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_of_London "Museum of London") in London where he worked from 1949 to 1957. His early speciality was 17th and 18th century wine bottles. He became chief archaeologist and director of the expanded [Colonial Williamsburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonial_Williamsburg "Colonial Williamsburg") archaeology program in 1957[2] and served in that capacity for the next three decades.

Noël Hume discovered and excavated the 17th century site of [Wolstenholme Towne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolstenholme_Towne "Wolstenholme Towne"), at [Carter's Grove Plantation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carter's_Grove_Plantation "Carter's Grove Plantation") just east of [Williamsburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Williamsburg,_Virginia "Williamsburg, Virginia").[3] Wolstenholme at [Martin's Hundred](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin's_Hundred "Martin's Hundred") was one of the early Virginia settlements after Jamestown and evidence of the [1622 Indian attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_massacre_of_1622 "Indian massacre of 1622") was found in the deaths of several of the citizens. Major excavations in Colonial Williamsburg included work at the original site of [Eastern State Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_State_Hospital_\(Virginia\) "Eastern State Hospital \(Virginia\)"), conducted in 1972 — which was the largest site work since the excavation of the [Governor's Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor's_Palace_\(Williamsburg,_Virginia\) "Governor's Palace \(Williamsburg, Virginia\)") in 1930 — the [James Geddy House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Geddy_House?action=edit&redlink=1 "James Geddy House \(page does not exist\)") and shop, [Weatherburn's Tavern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weatherburn's_Tavern?action=edit&redlink=1 "Weatherburn's Tavern \(page does not exist\)") and outbuildings and the cabinetmakers shop. Noël Hume retired as Director of the Department of Archaeological Research at Colonial Williamsburg in 1988. In retirement, he directed an excavation (1991–92) at the [Fort Raleigh National Historic Site](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Raleigh_National_Historic_Site "Fort Raleigh National Historic Site") on [Roanoke Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roanoke_Island "Roanoke Island"), [North Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Carolina "North Carolina"), and discovered [Thomas Harriot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Harriot "Thomas Harriot")'s 1585–86 "science center" there.[4]

## Scholarship and publicity

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Ivor_No%C3%ABl_Hume&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Scholarship and publicity")]

Noël Hume's work is noted for the effort to put the social life and economic overtones of history into the discoveries unearthed through archaeological examination — thus: [historical archaeology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historical_archaeology "Historical archaeology"). When he began his career, "historical archaeology did not exist as an academic discipline. It fell to Noël Hume's books, lectures, and television presentations to help bring it to the forefront of his profession, where it stands today," the [University of Virginia Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Virginia_Press "University of Virginia Press") said in its fall 2010 catalogue, which features his autobiography, _A Passion for the Past: The Odyssey of a Transatlantic Archaeologist._ Carmel Schrire, [Rutgers University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rutgers_University "Rutgers University"), author of _Digging through Darkness: Chronicles of an Archaeologist_ , said of Noël Hume's book: "Noël Hume is a household name. This book should be a professional classic, to be read alongside other memoirs like those of [Grahame Clark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grahame_Clark "Grahame Clark"), [Glyn Daniel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glyn_Daniel "Glyn Daniel"), [Gertrude Caton-Thompson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gertrude_Caton-Thompson "Gertrude Caton-Thompson"), and [Mortimer Wheeler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mortimer_Wheeler "Mortimer Wheeler")." In 1991 Noël Hume received the [J. C. Harrington Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._C._Harrington_Award "J. C. Harrington Award"), presented by the [Society for Historical Archaeology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Society_for_Historical_Archaeology "Society for Historical Archaeology") for his life-time contributions to archaeology centered on scholarship.[5]

On 17 June 1963, Noël Hume appeared as a "central character" on an episode of the game show _[To Tell the Truth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/To_Tell_the_Truth "To Tell the Truth")_.[6]

Noël Hume died in Virginia on 4 February 2017.[7]

## Accolades

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Ivor_No%C3%ABl_Hume&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: Accolades")]

Noël Hume was recognised by [Queen Elizabeth II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Elizabeth_II "Queen Elizabeth II") of Great Britain in 1993 and was made an Officer of the [Order of the British Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_the_British_Empire "Order of the British Empire") (O.B.E.) for service to the British cultural interests in Virginia.
