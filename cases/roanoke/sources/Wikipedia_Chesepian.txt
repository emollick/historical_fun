The **Chesepian** (**Chesapeake**) were a [Native American tribe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_Americans_in_the_United_States "Native Americans in the United States") who lived near present-day [South Hampton Roads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Hampton_Roads "South Hampton Roads") in the [U.S. state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._state "U.S. state") of [Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia "Virginia"). They occupied an area which is now in the independent cities of [Norfolk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norfolk,_Virginia "Norfolk, Virginia") and [Virginia Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia_Beach,_Virginia "Virginia Beach, Virginia") (formerly [Norfolk County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norfolk_County,_Virginia "Norfolk County, Virginia") and [Princess Anne County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princess_Anne_County,_Virginia "Princess Anne County, Virginia")).[1][2]

## Name

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Chesapeake_people&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Name")]

The name _Chesapeake_ is an [anglicisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglicisation_\(linguistics\) "Anglicisation \(linguistics\)") of the Algonquian word, _K'che-sepi-ack_ , which translates as "country on a great river."[2] The name for the Native American tribe is spelled many different ways, "Chesapian" is commonly used.[3][4][5] In 1585, [Ralph Lane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ralph_Lane "Ralph Lane") used both "Chesapians"[6] and "Chesapeaks",.[2] [John Smith's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Smith_\(explorer\) "John Smith \(explorer\)") charts and writings also show variety but most frequently used "Chesapeaks".[2] [John White's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_White_\(colonist_and_artist\) "John White \(colonist and artist\)") illustrations used "Ehesepiooc".[2]

## Settlements

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Chesapeake_people&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Settlements")]

They occupied an area which is now the [Norfolk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norfolk,_Virginia "Norfolk, Virginia"), [Portsmouth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portsmouth,_Virginia "Portsmouth, Virginia"), [Chesapeake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chesapeake,_Virginia "Chesapeake, Virginia") and [Virginia Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia_Beach,_Virginia "Virginia Beach, Virginia") areas.[7] The main village of the Chesepian was Skicoak on the peninsula east of the [Elizabeth River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_River_\(Virginia\) "Elizabeth River \(Virginia\)") either on that river[8] or near the [Lynnhaven River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lynnhaven_River "Lynnhaven River").[9]

Two other Chesepian towns were Apasus and [Chesepioc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chesepioc_\(settlement\) "Chesepioc \(settlement\)") (Chesepiuc), both on the same peninsula in what is now the city of Virginia Beach. Chesepioc lay on [Great Neck Point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Neck_Point "Great Neck Point") east of the Lynnhaven River.[10] Archaeologists and others have found numerous Native American arrowheads, stone axes, pottery, and beads in Great Neck Point. Several native burials were found as well.[10]

## Language and affiliation

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Chesapeake_people&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Language and affiliation")]

Although they spoke an [Eastern Algonquian language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Algonquian_language "Eastern Algonquian language") like many tribes within the [Powhatan Confederacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Powhatan_Confederacy "Powhatan Confederacy"), archaeological evidence suggests that the Chesepian people originally belonged to another group, the [Carolina Algonquian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pamlico "Pamlico").

## History

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Chesapeake_people&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: History")]

The culture of the Chesapians is called "Late Woodland" and they depended heavily on the resources of the Chesapeake Bay, notably the fish and shellfish.[11][12]

There is evidence that some of the survivors of the [Roanoke Colony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roanoke_Colony "Roanoke Colony") settled with the Chesapians after the failure of their settlement.[13][14]

In 1607, after the decimation by Powhatan,[15] the Chesapians had about 100 warriors and a total population estimated at 350.[2] By 1669, they ceased to exist as a tribe.[2]

### Demise

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Chesapeake_people&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: Demise")]

[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Chesapeake_Burial_Ground_Plaque_@_First_Landing_State_Park_\(7368835318\).jpg)Grave marker of relocated remains of Chesapeake natives

According to [William Strachey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Strachey "William Strachey")'s _[The Historie of Travaile Into Virginia Britannia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Historie_of_Travaile_Into_Virginia_Britannia "The Historie of Travaile Into Virginia Britannia")_ (1618), the Chesepian were wiped out by the [Powhatan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Powhatan "Powhatan"), the leader of the [Virginia Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia_Peninsula "Virginia Peninsula")–based [Powhatan Confederacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsenacommacah "Tsenacommacah"), sometime before the arrival of the English at Jamestown in 1607. The Chesepian were eliminated because Powhatan's priests had warned him that "from the Chesapeake Bay a nation should arise, which should dissolve and give end to his empire".[16][15]

Though historians of the period express little doubt that the Powhatans eradicated the Chesapeake tribe, Strachey's belief that these rumored prophesies indicated the Christian God's intervention on behalf of the Jamestown Colony against "The Devil's Empire" appears, in hindsight, rather eccentric.[17]
