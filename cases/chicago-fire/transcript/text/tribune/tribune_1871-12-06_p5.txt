The total amount of National Bank currency
which has been returned to the Comptroller to
be destroyed, and have new notes issued in its
stead, is $54,510,313. Of this amount. £23,918,627
was returned during the last year. Of this latter
amount, §3.035,799 were notes of banks either in
process of voluntary liquidation, or in the hands
of receivers. This would leave nearly $21,000,000
of currency which has been sent to the Comp
troller because it was worn or mutilated. From
these figures we may gathor an approxi
mate notion of the wear and
tear of paper currency. The $31,000,000
is 7 per cent on the total amount of $322,052,030 of
National Bank currency in actual circulation on
September 30, and it is probable that if the muti
lated notes were not continually being replaced
by new ones, tno absolute loss on suchas would
become entirely valueless would bo at least 5 per
tent per annum. This ratio would, of course, be
increased as the whole volume of currency grow
older, and in fifteen or eighteen years nearly the
whole of such currency as was not hoarded would
be either worn out or lost. This affords uu ap
proximate idea of the profits formerly derived by
the wild-cat banks from this source of profit.
