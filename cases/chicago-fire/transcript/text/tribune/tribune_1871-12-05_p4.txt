The question now is, What have the peo
ple of Chicago learned, and how will they
put what they have learned in practice:
Our merchants cannot build of asbestos;
they cannot excavate their stores and dwell
ings from the solid rock, nor can they put up
structures entirely of iron, cast in a single
piece. These materials are expensive, even
in small quantities, difficult to work, and
cumbersome to handle. Their means are
least when most sorely needed. With the
money that men had before the tire, and the
excellence that the great conflagration en
tailed, there is no doubt a very
different city would have been
produced than that which was destroyed.
