FATAL EXPLOSION.
A Portion of Bussell’s Distillery Blown
np and a Man Instantly Killed.
Foil Particulars of the Disaster—Coroner’s
Inquest—Theories Advanced.
An explosion, attended with loss of life and
considerable damage to property, took place in
Russell’s distillery, No. 274 Third avenue, be
tween Taylor and Twelfth streets, about noon
yesterday. Tho building Is a frame one, the
stills being situated in the rear part. A few
minutes before 12 o’clock, tho “beer runner”
turned ©n the steam, for tho purpose of blowing
the swill from one of the chambers Into a vat
provided for the purpose; but scarcely had ho
done so, than an explosion occurred. The cham
bers or tubs, of which there were three, situated
one above the other, were sent into tho air;
boards and pieces of Iron flew in all directions,
and what was a moment before a substan
tial building, was rendered almost a total
wreck. The northwest comer, or
about 35x40 feet, including nearly all
In it from the ground to tbo second floor, was re
duced to kindling-wood, portions of tho building
being thrown a hundred feet away. Large pieces
of scantling were blown through the house of a
Mr. Anderson, in the rear of No. 2C6 Third ave
nue, but no one within was injured. Directly
north of the distillery, a ten-foot alley interven
ing, there is a one-story cottage, In which live a
colored family named Foreman. Mrs. F. and a
girl were in the kitchen washing, and, while busi
ly engaged, were nearly frightened to death by
the advent of boards, timbers, pieces of iron,
swill, and whiskey which rained down upon the
little house, nearly burying it. Tho side of the
kitchen was penetrated by several rafters, and
the stove was struck by a Large board and thrown
upon a sofa. It was broken in small bits, but the
fire in it did no damage. Mrs. Foreman and the
girl imagined an earthquake was in progress, and
jumped out of a back window, reaching the
ground unhurt.
There were in the distillery seven or eight men,
who were employed about tho building. When
the explosion occurred, all but two escaped un
hurt. One or two jumped out of the windows,
the others making their way down tho stairway,
which is in thq southern part of the building and
was not damaged. The two workmen who did
not get out were George Bennett, the beer run
ner,” and Geo. Ladd, the tub washer. Both were
on the second floor, near a washtub, Bennett,
who had charge ol the work, having pulled the
valve to let on the steam to clear tho chamber”
of refuse. When the report of the explosion was
heard by those in the office adjoining tho distil
lery, they immediately rushed out to seo what
had occasioned it. Perceiving that a portion of
the distillery was demolished, they naturally
concluded that some one had been killed or in
jured, and commenced a search among tho debrit
for tuc victims. Bennett and Ladd were found
lying near each other on the ground
floor, the former being dead, and the
latter badly scalded. Both were picked up and
carried into the office. In addition to being
scalded, Ladd had his left leg broken. Other
men, who were also searching for injuredpersons,
found a boy named Fred. Brogenwald nnder the
ruins on the north side of the building. This lad.
It is thought, was seated in a wagon filled with
com, which was standing in the alley mentioned
awaiting to be unloaded. Tho driver had left but
a moment before the explosion to light his pipe,
and thereby avoided being made a victim. The
wagon was overturned by tho force of the explo
sion, and fell Into Freeman’s yard, the alley
being several feet above it. The boy Brogenwald
was found near tho wagon, covered with mash
and com. His face and arms were scalded, but
his condition is not at all dangerous. He was re
moved to his home in the neighborhood, and re
ceived medical attention. As soon as the Coro
ner arrived, Ladd was sent to the Coon tv Hospi
tal, and a jury was empannelled to hold an in
quest on the body of Bennett. As he was the only
person who could explain the cause of the explo
sion, the aevelopment* at the inquest amounted
to very little. There weretwotheories advanced,
but as one of them was more plausible than tho
other, and seemed to bo entitled to credence it was
accepted by tho jury. As stated, the chambers or
tubs were three in number, situated one above
tho other. Connected with tho lower one were
two pipes, one fixed to tho boiler through which
steam passed, and tbe other runninginto the swill
vat When the chambers were last cleaned it is
supposed that some of the swill remained in the
pipe, and, it being uncovered, that tho recent cold
weather hardened tho refuse, and effect
ually choked up the pipe. When
Bennett pulled up the gate,” which allowed
the steam to enter the lower chamber (this being
the way in which the tubs were cleaned or blown
out), there was no place for the vapor to escape,
and hence the explosion. This theory governed
the verdict, which attributed Bennett’s death to
an accident. The deceased was about 45 years of
ape. and leaves a wife and two children, who live
at No. 77 North Curtis street. He had been en
gaged in tno distilling business for fifteen years,
and waa considered to be a competent man.
The damage done to the building and ma
chinery is not definitely known, as no estimate

structure and replace the machinery and tubs
