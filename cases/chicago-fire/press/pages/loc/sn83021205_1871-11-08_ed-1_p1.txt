THE ELECTION IN WORCESTER.
The Voir ever Polled in Ihr Cily.
Various circumstances combined to make the
election a very interesting one in this cityyes
terday. The excrement of the contest centered
in the vote for Senator and for representatives
in the 11th district; but the final and highly
satisfactory result which is given in the tables
below, shows that all that is necessary in an
emergency such as came up yesterday, is that
the members of the republican party come to
the polls and vote. Simply by a little more care
than usual, the schemes of the democrats were
met and defeated; and although they strained
every nerve and brought up every available
man, their efforts gave them neither the senator
or any of the six representatives elected in the
city. Five thousand and forty-five votes were
cast, against 4097 last year, and 4808 thrown
tor Grant in 1868.
The voting places were thinly attended when
the polls opened, at nine o’clock; but towards
noon the crowds increased, and dining the hour
between twelve and one there was a perfect rush
of mechanics to the ballot lioxes. In the after
noon also, until four o’clock, at which time the
polls closed, the enthusiasm was kept up.
Of the four regular party tickets in the field,
two were encroached upon by the name of the
democratic nominee for senator—Thomas
Gates—and the torn th made a show
so feeble as hardly to call for mention;
in fact the prohibitory tickets were
laid in idle piles in most of the ward
rooms, while the labor reform ticket fared no
better in wards one and two. The bogus re
publican ticket had the name of Thomas Gates
substituted for that of Adin Thayer on the
regular ticket for senator, and the labor reform
ticket suffered from a substitution of the same
name in place of M. S. Johnson’s on a ticket
exactly like the regular one in every other way.
The regular labor reform ticket, bore the name
of the republican nominee for sheriff—A. B. R.
Sprague; and of the democratic nominee for
district attorney—W. A. Williams. The pro
hibitory ticket bore the names of the republican
nominees for councillor (E. B. Stoddard), sheriff
(A. B. Sprague),district attorney (W. W.Ricc),
and clerk of courts, (Joseph Mason). In
fact, Mason’s name appeared upon
all of the tickets but the labor reform,
which bore John Dana’s. The labor reformers
so sadly behind the times at the start as to
begin the day voting for the late lamented J. S.
C. Knowlton for shetiff; but on discovering
the error the republican nominee was put on.
The labor reform vote was 257 less than last
year.
The usual amount of scratching, clipping,
pasting, and voting outside the nominations was
done, and among the stray votes for governor
ten or a dozen were cast for Butler. One voter
in ward 3 cast a vote for him on which no other
names appeared, but in place of other officers a
long-drawn-out explanation of the reasons
prompting the voter to his action. They were
mostly culled from Butler’s speeches.

which show that the republican majority in the
eleventh district was even larger than last

POSTAL CARS.
The post office department is pressing forward
a system of railway postal cars that will, it is
expected, revolutionize the present system of
distribution of mails and enable the department
to materialh modify if not abolish all the dis
tributing post offices on thegreat railway routes,
and transfer the work ofdistribution to the cars.
By the means proposed all the distributions
which now take place at the end of a route and
of course involve the loss of one mail, will be
distributed in the postal car before the end of
the main route is reached, and so be ready to be
transferred already assorted to the localities.
MEETING OF THE SENATE FINANCE COMMITTEE
To-day being the time fixed for the meeting of
the senate committee on finance, the members
now in the city—namely, Sherman,
Scott and Bayard—assembled at the capital this
morning, but, as there was no quorum, adjourn
ed until to-morrow. Messrs. Morrill of Vermont,
Wright, Ames and Fenton are expected in the
course of two or three days, and the regular
daily sessions will begin before the expiration
of the week. The tariff and revenue laws will
be fully considered by the committee, in order
to prepare a bill to report to the senate as a sub
stitute for bills which passed the house last ses
sion, putting coal and salt on the free list. The
various statements that have been published,
outlying the intended action of the committee,
are purely conjectural, and it is believed that
the full interchange of opinion contemplated
will develop alout the same diversity of opinion
among the members of the committee that is
known to have existed at the close of the last
session.
