Escape from Jail.—On Friday niglit last,
about 7 o’clock, George Henderson, a peniten
tiary convict,, who escaped from the Western end
of the Chesapeake and Ohio Railroad, some
■months since, and recently arrested with a stolen
horse, belonging to Mr. Kincaid of Alleghany,
in his possession, and a negro man,named Wm.
Brown, committed on the charge of burning the
Barn of Mr. Clias. S. Patterson of this County,
escaped from our County Jail.
Henderson had been contemplating an escape
and had loosened hiskobbles. On Friday evening
he asked a colored boy, Wesley White, em
ployed by the Jailor to get him a plate of oys
ters, which he did, but, as be could not put the
plates through the iron grating, Henderson pre
vailed upon him to open the door. When this
was done, he seized the door and attempted to
rush out. Wesley seized him and after a strug
gle, and receiving a severe cut on the breast and
arm, Henderson passed by him, followed by the
colored boy, Brown. As they leaped over the
railing into the street, they raised the cry of fire,
which brought out the Fire Company, and in the
excitement consequent they easily escaped, and
at this time are at large.
Henderson was in the same cell with Mr. Clias.
Carson, charged with killing D. P. Clinebell,
who could also have eseaped, but entertained no
idea of evading a trial and remained in his cell.

Closing Schools for Insufficient Num
ber.—All schools supported wholly by pub
lic funds must as ordered by the regulations,
be closed as soon as it is ascertained, that
the required average attendance was not
reached during the preceding month unless
good evidence is furnished that the deficiency
will not exist in the future.
As goon as the teacher’s monthly report
discloses to the county superintendent the
fact that 'he average attendence for the month
was under twenty, it becomes the duly of
that, officer to communicate the fact, to the
district clerk, wiib instructions to close the
school at once and to keep it closed until
pledges are obtained from parents in the
neighborhood which give assurance that,
there will be afterward a sufficient increase
of pupils to constitute a lawful school.
This rule is as valuable in an educational,
as in an economical point of view. Irregu
lar attendance isaipong the greatest evils to
a school and its pupils. A large propor
tion of parents either from ignorance or oth
er cause are too ready to keep their children
from school for slight reasons, even whilst
desiring them to be educated but if they
find that they are iu serious danger of losing
the school for want of a certain number,
then children will be sent with more regu
larity. Regular attendance is so essential
to education that a school of irregular pu
pils is scarcely worth maintaining, and he
loss to the neighborhood of closing such a
school is more fancied than real, It is-true
that there are always a few faithi’jl ones
who are made to suffer in sue a ease, but
the State it tou poor to pay a teacher or
any one else full wages for partial work.
The school can go on if the public lias to
pay ouly according to the services rendered
as is set forth in the regulations.

Spencerian Copy books and charts, a little

in organizing and conducting writing class

from the several depositories or from the
