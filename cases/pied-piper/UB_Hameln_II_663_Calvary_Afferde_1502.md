## What this charter can and cannot establish

**Can establish.** Where the town of c. 1500 located "Calvary … beneden den Koppen": a devotional Calvary with measured stations running from the east gate to the boundary of Klein-Afferde on the Hildesheim road, moved in 1502 outside the Landwehr. Because the house inscriptions of 1525–1602 use the same words in the same town within a generation, the charter fixes what "Calvarie bi den Koppen" meant to the people who carved it. That removes Kopahn (Dobbertin) and Coppenbrügge/Ith (Woeller, Hüsam) as readings of "Koppen" in the inscriptions, and agrees with Erich 1654 ("Calvarien- oder Kopffelberg … da das Halsgerichte stehet") and Backhaus 1589 ("das Halsgericht allda versteh").

**Cannot establish.** Anything about 1284. It does not show that a group left by that road, that a Calvary or execution place existed there in 1284 (the earliest witness to a Hamelin "Calvary" is the Lüneburg note of c. 1430–50; the stations are a fifteenth-century devotion), or any direction or destination. The "direction east" in the report comes from the Lüneburg note's "extra valvam orientalem", not from this charter. Its value is to the source criticism of the later tradition, not to the reconstruction of the event.

**Novelty qualified.** Not found cited in Udolph 1997, Cammann 1984, DI 28 commentary or the theory literature read for this report; Hüsam 1993 ("Calvaria") and Meinardus 1882 were not accessible, so it may have been noticed before.
